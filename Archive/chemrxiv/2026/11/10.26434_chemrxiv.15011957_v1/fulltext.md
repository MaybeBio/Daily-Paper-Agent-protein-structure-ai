Absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET) models are usually judged on predefined benchmark splits. Those splits are useful, but they do not show whether a model reproduces the pharmacokinetic (PK) numbers that are actually reported for individual drugs. Here we compared ADMET-AI with a fine-tuned NV-KERMT-70M-v2 model (“KERMT”) on the same Therapeutics Data Commons (TDC)-aligned endpoint definitions. KERMT was fine-tuned in two stages: a protocol check on the ChEMBL multitask (ChEMBL-MT) data set, then TDC-aligned regression and classification fine-tuning on merged ChEMBL, Biogen, ExpansionRx/OpenADMET and TDC data. Two retrospective literature comparisons were run. The first used a curated 25-drug oncology panel: for half-life (n = 17) KERMT cut mean absolute error (MAE) from 46.81 to 16.83 h (paired Wilcoxon p = 0.0046, Holm-adjusted p = 0.0139, closer for 13 of 17 drugs); volume of distribution at steady state (VDss, n = 12) and hepatocyte clearance (n = 17) showed no statistically supported difference. The second, reported here for the first time, is an expanded three-way comparison of 53 oncology drugs × 3 endpoints (159 drug-endpoint rows) against curated literature and label values. On the 48 drugs with a usable half-life reference, KERMT more than halved the error (MAE 19.43 vs 43.38 h; median absolute error 12.01 vs 35.30 h; geometric mean fold error, GMFE, 2.72 vs 3.69; within 2-fold 41.0% vs 25.0%), was closer for 34 of 48 drugs (70.8%; 95% confidence interval, CI, 55.9-83.0%), and the paired Wilcoxon test was significant (p = 0.0002; Holm-adjusted p = 0.0004). The advantage persisted after each model's systematic bias was removed (p = 0.0004), so it is not a simple scale offset: ADMET-AI over-predicted half-life for 35 of 48 drugs (mean bias +29.20 h) whereas KERMT was mildly conservative (-13.79 h). For plasma protein binding ratio (PPBR, n = 21) KERMT had lower MAE (7.05 vs 9.44 percentage points) and the only statistically significant rank agreement with literature seen anywhere in this study (Spearman ρ = 0.540, p = 0.012, vs 0.400, p = 0.073), but the paired error difference was not significant (p = 0.157). One endpoint failed outright and we state it plainly: KERMT VDss predictions collapsed to near-baseline behaviour. They occupied a narrow band (46 of 53 drugs between 0 and 10 L/kg; standard deviation 3.64 vs 6.71 L/kg for ADMET-AI), no drug in the panel had an assay-matched VDss reference, and on the six drugs with an approximate reference the per-drug KERMT predictions (MAE 2.18 L/kg) were no better than replacing the entire model with one constant (MAE 2.14 L/kg) and worse than a no-information baseline (1.71 L/kg). KERMT VDss output should therefore not be used. A deliberately strict control bounds the positive findings as well: against a structure-blind constant predictor built by leave-one-out from the reference values, KERMT was statistically indistinguishable on both endpoints (half-life MAE 19.43 vs 16.07 h, p = 0.051; PPBR 7.05 vs 7.24 percentage points, p = 0.812), while ADMET-AI was significantly worse than that constant for half-life (p < 0.0001). We therefore make no claim of accurate numerical point prediction; the supported claims are that fine-tuning removes ADMET-AI's large systematic half-life error and that the PPBR rank correlation is compound-specific signal a constant cannot possess. Physically impossible values occurred in 16 of 159 KERMT rows and 21 of 159 ADMET-AI rows. Across 30 classification endpoints, mean inter-model Pearson correlation was 0.711 and mean binary agreement at 0.5 was 89%, which measures similarity, not accuracy. This version does not yet include official Therapeutics Data Commons benchmark-group evaluation of the fine-tuned models, replicate training seeds, or a training-set overlap audit; those gaps are declared explicitly rather than left implicit. The overall conclusion is endpoint-specific: fine-tuned KERMT is a materially better-calibrated half-life and plasma-protein-binding predictor than ADMET-AI on this panel, it is not yet a demonstrated ranking model for half-life, and its VDss head is unusable.
