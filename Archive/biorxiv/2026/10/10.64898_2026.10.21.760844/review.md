## Review setup

- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials were provided for review
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that small-angle neutron scattering (SANS) with contrast matching, using biosynthetically deuterated bacterial cellulose, reveals a distinct conformational state of the processive cellulase Trichoderma reesei Cel7A when bound to crystalline cellulose under catalytically favorable conditions. Molecular dynamics simulations of the free enzyme did not reproduce the bound-state structural features. The authors conclude that the linker and carbohydrate-binding module modulate interfacial positioning of the catalytic domain dynamically rather than acting as a rigid mechanical unit.

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting application of neutron contrast matching to study a cellulase enzyme at a solid-liquid interface, a technically demanding and relatively uncommon approach. The central claim, that cellulose-bound Cel7A adopts a conformationally distinct state under productive catalytic conditions, is plausible and would be of interest to the biocatalysis and glycoside hydrolase communities. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the rigor of the SANS analysis, the statistical significance of the observed differences, or the validity of the simulation comparisons. The conclusion regarding the dynamic role of the linker and carbohydrate-binding module is presented as strongly supported, but the evidence base visible here is too thin to assess whether this interpretation is uniquely supported or merely consistent with the data.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying enzymatic degradation of insoluble polysaccharides, particularly cellulases and other processive glycoside hydrolases, would find these results relevant to ongoing debates about how linker regions and carbohydrate-binding modules contribute to processive catalysis. The methodological demonstration of using deuterated substrates with neutron contrast variation to isolate protein scattering at an interface could also interest structural biologists working on solid-liquid interfacial systems. Those developing kinetic or mechanistic models of cellulase action on crystalline cellulose would need to account for the proposed interface-selected conformational state.
- **Major strengths** The use of biosynthetically deuterated bacterial cellulose with SANS contrast matching is an elegant experimental strategy to isolate the scattering contribution of the bound enzyme from that of the substrate. The comparison between catalytically favorable and unfavorable conditions provides an internal control that strengthens the interpretation that the observed conformational change is functionally relevant. The combination of experimental scattering with molecular dynamics simulations represents a complementary approach that could provide mechanistic insight beyond what either method alone would yield.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** "SANS measurements performed under conditions supporting high or low catalytic activity revealed distinct differences between the free and cellulose-bound enzyme. Under poorly active conditions, the conformational distributions of free and bound Cel7A were similar. In contrast, catalytically favorable conditions produced an additional interdomain correlation in the bound enzyme"
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract reports a difference in scattering between free and bound Cel7A under active conditions, described as "an additional interdomain correlation," but provides no quantitative information about the magnitude of this effect, the signal-to-noise ratio of the SANS data, the contrast matching quality, or the statistical significance of the observed differences. No Guinier analysis, pair-distance distribution functions, or model fitting results are described. Without these details, it is impossible to assess whether the reported conformational difference is robust or could arise from incomplete contrast matching, residual background scattering, or analysis artifacts.
  - **Why it matters** The central conclusion of the paper rests entirely on the reliability of the SANS contrast matching and the interpretation of scattering differences as conformational reorganization. If the contrast matching is imperfect or the difference is within experimental noise, the entire interpretive framework collapses. SANS experiments at solid-liquid interfaces are technically challenging, and the abstract does not demonstrate that the authors have addressed known pitfalls such as substrate polydispersity, surface roughness effects, or incomplete deuteration.
  - **Resolution test** The full manuscript must provide SANS profiles with error bars, contrast matching validation data, quantitative comparison of scattering curves under different conditions (for example, with appropriate statistical tests), and model fits with residuals. The "additional interdomain correlation" should be identified through explicit structural modeling with confidence intervals, not merely inferred from qualitative differences in scattering curves.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** "Molecular dynamics simulations showed that free Cel7A samples can assume compact, intermediate, and extended conformations, but these solution-state ensembles did not reproduce the structural features of cellulose-bound Cel7A"
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract states that MD simulations of free Cel7A did not reproduce the bound-state structural features, but provides no information about the simulation length, force field, starting structures, sampling methodology, or how the comparison between simulated ensembles and experimental scattering was performed. It is unclear whether the simulations were intended to sample the full conformational space of the free enzyme or to predict the bound-state conformation. The conclusion that the bound state is "structurally distinct" from anything sampled in solution depends critically on the adequacy of the conformational sampling, which cannot be evaluated from the abstract.
  - **Why it matters** The claim that the bound state represents a "structurally distinct" conformation not present in the solution ensemble is a strong negative claim. If the simulations undersampled the conformational space, the absence of bound-like conformations in the simulated ensemble would be uninformative. Conversely, if the simulations did sample such conformations but with low probability, the interpretation would shift from "structurally distinct state" to "conformational selection from a pre-existing ensemble." These are mechanistically different models with different implications.
  - **Resolution test** The full manuscript must provide simulation details including total simulation time, number of replicas, force field parameters, convergence metrics, and a quantitative comparison between simulated ensembles and experimental scattering data. The authors should demonstrate that the simulations adequately sample the conformational space of the free enzyme and that the absence of bound-like conformations is not a sampling artifact.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Claim pointer** "These results identify productive cellulose engagement as a structurally distinct state of the Cel7A holoenzyme and support a dynamic model in which the linker and carbohydrate-binding module modulate interfacial positioning of the catalytic domain rather than functioning as a rigid mechanical unit during processive hydrolysis"
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract presents a mechanistic model of linker and CBM function as "modulating interfacial positioning" rather than acting as a "rigid mechanical unit," but the SANS data as described provide only a low-resolution view of the overall conformational envelope of the enzyme. It is not clear how such data can distinguish between a dynamic modulatory role and alternative models, such as a hinged but still mechanically coupled mechanism, or a model in which the CBM simply anchors the enzyme while the linker provides entropic flexibility. The abstract does not describe any experiments that directly test the proposed mechanism, such as mutations in the linker or CBM, or measurements under different substrate loadings.
  - **Why it matters** The mechanistic conclusion is the primary conceptual contribution of the paper. If the data can only show that the bound conformation differs from the free conformation, this is consistent with many mechanistic models. The specific claim about the linker and CBM functioning as dynamic modulators rather than rigid mechanical units requires additional evidence or at least a more explicit argument for why the observed conformational change rules out alternative models.
  - **Resolution test** The full manuscript should either provide direct experimental tests of the proposed mechanism or present a more carefully reasoned argument, with supporting data, for why the observed conformational reorganization specifically supports the dynamic modulation model over alternatives. If the data are only consistent with but not uniquely supportive of the proposed mechanism, this should be stated explicitly.

## Risk / unsupported claims

- The claim that the bound enzyme exhibits "an additional interdomain correlation" under active conditions is unsupported without quantitative scattering data and statistical analysis.
- The claim that MD simulations of free Cel7A did not reproduce bound-state features is unverifiable without simulation details and convergence metrics.
- The mechanistic conclusion that the linker and CBM "modulate interfacial positioning" rather than acting as a "rigid mechanical unit" is not uniquely supported by the described data.
- The broader generalization to "heterogeneous biocatalysis" is not supported by evidence from a single enzyme system.
- The statement that the bound state is "structurally distinct" from solution states is a strong claim that requires demonstration that the observed difference exceeds experimental uncertainty and is not an artifact of the contrast matching procedure.
