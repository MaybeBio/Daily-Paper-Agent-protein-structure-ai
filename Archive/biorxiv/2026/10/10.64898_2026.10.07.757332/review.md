## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no figures, tables, methods text, or supplementary materials were provided
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present RNA3D-Decoy, a benchmark dataset of 103,549 RNA structure decoys for 677 experimental targets, generated by five AI-based predictors and annotated with multiple quality metrics. They report distinct quality distributions across dataset levels, target-dependent predictor differences, and limited within-target score variation for RNA-protein complexes, and position the resource as suitable for developing and evaluating RNA structure quality assessment methods.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; dataset and code availability statement
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full methods, dataset statistics tables, quality score distribution figures, target selection criteria, predictor version details, train/test split definitions, and any validation or benchmarking results

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract describes a potentially valuable community resource for RNA structure quality assessment. The scale of the dataset and the inclusion of diverse molecular contexts are commendable. However, the abstract alone does not provide sufficient methodological detail to assess the rigor of decoy generation, the validity of the quality annotations, or the practical utility of the dataset for benchmarking. Several claims about quality distributions and predictor behavior are stated without supporting quantitative evidence. The resource may be of interest to the RNA modeling community, but the case for its broad utility is not fully established from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers developing or benchmarking RNA structure prediction and quality assessment methods, particularly those working on deep learning-based approaches. The dataset could also be useful for developers of scoring functions, error prediction models, and model ranking algorithms. The inclusion of RNA-protein complexes may attract researchers in structural biology and integrative modeling.
- **Major strengths** The dataset is large in scale, covering over one hundred thousand decoys across several hundred targets. The inclusion of RNA-only structures, metal ion-containing RNAs, and RNA-protein complexes addresses a recognized gap in QA benchmark resources. The use of multiple contemporary AI predictors reflects the current state of the field. The provision of multiple quality metrics, including interface-specific DockQ scores, is appropriate for the intended applications.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** "Decoys were generated using five biomolecular predictors: AlphaFold 3, ESMFold2, Protenix, Chai-1, and Boltz-2."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract does not specify the version, release date, or configuration of each predictor used. It is also unclear whether default settings were used, whether any post-processing or filtering was applied to the raw outputs, and how the decoys were selected or sampled from the predictor outputs. Without this information, the reproducibility of the decoy generation pipeline cannot be assessed.
  - **Why it matters** Predictor versions and settings materially affect output quality. If the versions are not pinned or the generation protocol is not fully described, users cannot interpret the quality distributions or compare against future predictors. The dataset's utility as a benchmark depends on the decoys being generated under well-defined, reproducible conditions.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a detailed methods section specifying predictor versions, runtime environments, input preparation, any filtering or selection steps, and the exact number of decoys contributed by each predictor per target.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** "Each decoy is evaluated against its experimental reference using TM-score, lDDT, and RMSD, with global and local quality annotations where applicable."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract does not describe how the experimental reference structures were obtained, curated, or validated. It is also unclear which software or implementation was used to compute TM-score, lDDT, and RMSD, and whether local quality annotations are per-residue or per-nucleotide. The phrase "where applicable" is ambiguous and could mask inconsistent annotation coverage across the dataset.
  - **Why it matters** Quality scores are only meaningful if the reference structures are reliable and the metric implementations are standard and consistent. Inconsistent or undocumented annotation protocols could lead to systematic biases that propagate into any downstream QA method trained or evaluated on this dataset.
  - **Resolution test** Describe the source and curation of experimental references, specify the exact software and parameters for each metric, and report the fraction of decoys with local annotations and the definition of those annotations.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Claim pointer** "Broad pooled score distributions also coexist with limited within-target score variation, particularly for RNA-protein complexes, emphasizing the importance of target-level characterization."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
  - **Concern** This is a central analytical claim, but no quantitative support is provided. The abstract does not report any statistics, such as variance components, interquartile ranges, or correlation coefficients, that would substantiate the coexistence of broad pooled distributions and limited within-target variation. The claim is presented as a finding but functions as an assertion.
  - **Why it matters** If within-target variation is indeed limited, this has direct implications for how the dataset should be split and how QA methods should be evaluated. However, without supporting numbers, readers cannot judge the magnitude of the effect or whether it is specific to certain target classes or predictors.
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative evidence, such as variance decomposition, per-target score ranges, or representative examples, that demonstrates the claimed distributional properties.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Interdisciplinary readership
  - **Claim pointer** "RNA3D-Decoy provides a resource for developing and evaluating methods for global quality estimation, local error prediction, and candidate-model ranking."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract does not describe any baseline experiments, example use cases, or comparisons against existing QA benchmarks. It is therefore unclear whether the dataset is immediately usable, whether it offers advantages over existing resources, or whether the quality labels are sufficiently discriminative for the proposed tasks.
  - **Why it matters** A benchmark dataset is most valuable when its properties are characterized in the context of existing methods. Without any demonstration of how the dataset performs in a realistic QA evaluation scenario, potential users cannot assess its practical utility or its added value relative to current resources.
  - **Resolution test** Include a small set of baseline QA experiments, such as ranking performance or error prediction accuracy, using the dataset and compare against existing benchmarks.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "each predictor contributing the highest-TM-score model for a subset of targets" is stated without any supporting numbers or analysis.
- The claim of "distinct quality distributions across dataset levels" is not supported by any reported statistics.
- The claim of "limited within-target score variation, particularly for RNA-protein complexes" is not supported by quantitative evidence.
- The overall utility of the dataset for QA method development is asserted but not demonstrated through any example application or benchmark comparison.
- The reproducibility of the decoy generation pipeline cannot be assessed due to missing predictor version and configuration details.
