## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses an important and timely question: whether high-confidence AlphaFold-Multimer predictions of transmembrane protein complexes are physically plausible with respect to membrane geometry. The authors develop a systematic framework for assessing membrane compatibility and apply it to a large dataset of predicted complexes. The finding that most predictions are geometrically plausible, while a subset show inconsistencies, is useful and actionable for the structural biology community. The case studies with SERCA-phospholamban and the norepinephrine transporter provide concrete illustrations of both successes and remaining limitations. However, several methodological aspects require clarification, and the interpretation of some results may be overstated given the observational nature of the analyses. The manuscript would benefit from additional validation of the geometric criteria and a more cautious framing of the conclusions regarding AlphaFold3's capabilities.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational structural biologists using AlphaFold for complex prediction will find the membrane-geometry assessment framework directly applicable to their workflows. Experimentalists studying membrane protein complexes, particularly those involving small regulatory subunits or lipid-mediated interactions, will be interested in the identified limitations of current prediction methods. Researchers developing or benchmarking structure prediction tools will find the systematic evaluation of prediction quality informative. The work is also relevant to those interpreting large-scale predicted interactome datasets, as it provides complementary criteria for assessing biological plausibility beyond confidence scores.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript addresses a genuine gap in the field by providing membrane-specific geometric criteria for evaluating predicted transmembrane complexes, complementing existing confidence metrics. The scale of the analysis (189,469 complexes) is substantial and provides a statistically robust foundation for the reported distributions. The authors appropriately acknowledge the complementary nature of their geometric checks relative to confidence scores. The case studies are well chosen to illustrate both the capabilities and limitations of current methods. The benchmark of cholesterol-containing targets is a valuable contribution that quantifies AlphaFold3's limitations in a specific and important area.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Methodological validation
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that membrane-geometry assessment provides a valid complementary test of prediction quality, with specific thresholds (15-35 Å hydrophobic thickness, ≤30° axis angle, ≤5 Å midplane mismatch) used to classify complexes as compatible or suspicious.
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "Membrane-geometry assessment of highTM–highTM complexes"; Results section "Membrane geometry and compatibility of highTM–highTM complexes"
  - **Concern** The geometric thresholds used to classify complexes as compatible or suspicious are presented as operational criteria, but no validation is provided to demonstrate that these thresholds meaningfully distinguish correct from incorrect predictions. The authors do not benchmark their geometric criteria against experimentally determined transmembrane complex structures to establish sensitivity and specificity. Without such validation, it is unclear whether the 95.4% "plausible" figure reflects genuine prediction accuracy or simply the permissiveness of the chosen thresholds.
  - **Why it matters** The central claim of the manuscript is that membrane-geometry assessment provides a valuable complement to confidence metrics. This claim rests on the assumption that the geometric criteria are meaningful discriminators of prediction quality. If the thresholds are too permissive, the analysis may overstate the reliability of predictions; if too strict, it may generate false alarms. The lack of validation against experimental structures undermines confidence in the quantitative conclusions drawn from the large-scale analysis.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should validate their geometric criteria against a set of experimentally determined transmembrane complex structures with known stoichiometry and membrane orientation. This could involve computing the same geometric descriptors for experimental structures and demonstrating that they fall within the "compatible" range, and ideally showing that the criteria can discriminate between correct and incorrect predictions in a benchmark setting.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Interpretation of results
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that "Interactions between TM proteins, particularly those containing only one or two TM helices, were frequently dominated by extramembrane contacts" and implies this is a limitation or unexpected finding.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Interface composition of transmembrane protein complexes"; Results section "Extending Membrane-Geometry Assessment to Single- and Double-Pass TM Protein Complexes"
  - **Concern** The observation that lowTM proteins frequently interact via extramembrane contacts is presented as a notable finding, but the interpretation is ambiguous. For single-pass or double-pass membrane proteins, it is structurally expected that most of their mass is extramembrane, so dominance of extramembrane contacts may simply reflect the available surface area rather than a prediction artifact. The authors do not compare their observed contact distributions against what would be expected from the protein geometry alone, nor do they assess whether the extramembrane contacts are biologically plausible. The claim that this represents a limitation of predictions requires a more rigorous baseline for comparison.
  - **Why it matters** The manuscript's framing suggests that extramembrane-dominated interfaces are a potential problem with predictions, but without an appropriate null model or comparison to experimental structures, this interpretation is not supported. If extramembrane contacts are simply a geometric consequence of protein architecture, then the finding is less remarkable and the implications for prediction quality are unclear. This affects the overall narrative about the strengths and limitations of AlphaFold predictions.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide a comparison of their contact composition distributions against a null model that accounts for the relative surface areas of TM and extramembrane regions. Alternatively, they could compare against experimentally determined structures of lowTM-containing complexes to show whether the observed extramembrane dominance is consistent with known biology or represents a prediction-specific bias.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Generalizability of conclusions
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript concludes that "AlphaFold3... failed to reproduce the cholesterol-mediated norepinephrine transporter homodimer" and that a benchmark of 173 cholesterol-containing targets "revealed limited accuracy in predicting cholesterol-binding poses."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "AF3 improves the prediction of transmembrane protein interactions but remains limited in modeling lipid-mediated complexes"; Methods section "Prediction and evaluation of protein–cholesterol complexes using AlphaFold3"
  - **Concern** The conclusion that AlphaFold3 has "limited accuracy" in predicting cholesterol-binding poses is based on a benchmark with specific methodological choices that may not be optimal for assessing AlphaFold3's capabilities. The authors used default parameters, a specific number of cholesterol molecules (14 for NET), and POPG instead of the experimentally observed lipid. The manuscript does not report whether alternative parameter settings, different cholesterol copy numbers, or the use of the experimentally observed lipid species were explored. Given that AlphaFold3's handling of small molecules is known to be sensitive to input composition, the negative result may reflect suboptimal input configuration rather than an inherent limitation of the method.
  - **Why it matters** The claim that AlphaFold3 cannot accurately model cholesterol-mediated interactions is a strong conclusion with implications for users of the method. If the failure is due to specific input choices rather than fundamental limitations, the conclusion would be misleading. The manuscript's framing could discourage researchers from attempting lipid-containing predictions when alternative configurations might succeed. The generalizability of the negative result depends critically on the thoroughness of the parameter exploration.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should systematically vary the number of cholesterol molecules, the lipid species included, and other relevant parameters for the NET case and a subset of benchmark targets to determine whether any configuration recovers the experimentally observed interactions. If no configuration succeeds, the conclusion would be strengthened. If some configurations succeed, the conclusion should be revised to reflect parameter sensitivity rather than fundamental limitation.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Statistical rigor
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript reports that "95.4% of the 125,657 complexes falling within this interval" for joint hydrophobic thickness, and describes distributions of midplane mismatch and axis angles across the dataset.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Membrane geometry and compatibility of highTM–highTM complexes"; Figure 2
  - **Concern** The large-scale analyses report percentages and percentiles but do not provide confidence intervals or measures of uncertainty. Given the dataset is derived from predictions with varying confidence levels, it would be important to know whether the reported distributions are robust to filtering criteria, such as the confidence thresholds used to select complexes from the database. The manuscript does not report sensitivity analyses examining how the results change with different inclusion criteria.
  - **Why it matters** The quantitative claims about the proportion of geometrically plausible predictions are central to the manuscript's conclusions. Without uncertainty estimates or sensitivity analyses, readers cannot assess the robustness of these findings. The choice of inclusion criteria could substantially affect the reported percentages, and the lack of analysis leaves this uncertainty unaddressed.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide confidence intervals for the reported proportions and percentiles, and conduct sensitivity analyses varying the inclusion criteria (e.g., confidence thresholds, minimum TM helix count) to demonstrate that the conclusions are robust to reasonable alternative choices.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity of methods
  - **Affected element** Methods section "Empirical calibration of lowTM–lowTM membrane geometry"
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "Empirical calibration of lowTM–lowTM membrane geometry"
  - **Issue** The description of the empirical calibration procedure for lowTM–lowTM complexes is complex and difficult to follow. The distinction between the repeated sampling used to "characterize geometric variability" and the "separate fixed-seed selection" used to establish thresholds is not immediately clear, and the rationale for this two-stage approach is not fully explained.
  - **Required correction** The authors should clarify the purpose of each sampling stage and provide a more intuitive explanation of why the fixed-seed selection is necessary to avoid pseudoreplication. A schematic or step-by-step description would improve readability.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (validation of geometric criteria against experimental structures), R1-M2 (null model for extramembrane contact dominance), R1-M3 (systematic parameter exploration for AF3 cholesterol predictions)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The concept of using membrane geometry as a quality filter for predicted transmembrane complexes is novel and addresses a gap in existing validation approaches. The application to a large dataset of predicted complexes is a distinctive contribution. Scientific importance: The work has clear practical value for the structural biology community, providing tools to assess prediction quality beyond confidence scores. The identified limitations of AlphaFold3 for lipid-mediated complexes are informative, though the strength of this conclusion is currently limited by methodological concerns. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges computational structural biology, membrane biology, and bioinformatics, and should be accessible to researchers across these fields. Technical soundness: The computational framework is generally well described, but the lack of validation of geometric thresholds against experimental data and the limited parameter exploration for AF3 benchmarks are significant gaps that need to be addressed. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally clearly written, with appropriate context provided for the biological questions. Some methodological sections are dense and would benefit from additional clarification.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript addresses an important problem and presents a useful framework, but the central claims require additional validation. Specifically, the geometric criteria need benchmarking against experimental structures to establish their discriminative power, the interpretation of extramembrane contact dominance needs a more rigorous baseline, and the AF3 cholesterol benchmark conclusions should be tested against alternative parameter configurations. If these concerns are addressed, the work would make a valuable contribution to the field.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that 95.4% of highTM–highTM complexes have plausible joint hydrophobic thickness is presented as evidence of general prediction quality, but without validation of the 15-35 Å threshold against experimental structures, this figure cannot be interpreted as a measure of accuracy.
- The statement that interactions involving lowTM proteins are "frequently dominated by extramembrane contacts" is presented as a notable finding, but no baseline or comparison is provided to establish whether this differs from expectations based on protein geometry alone.
- The conclusion that AlphaFold3 "failed to reproduce" the NET dimer and shows "limited accuracy" for cholesterol poses is based on a specific parameter configuration that may not be optimal; the generalizability of this negative result is not established.
- The claim that AlphaFold3 "recovered an experimentally supported SERCA-phospholamban interaction" is based on a single example and cannot support general conclusions about AF3's improved capability for transmembrane interactions.
- The statement that "membrane-specific geometric constraints provide a valuable complement to conventional confidence metrics" is reasonable but is presented as a conclusion when the supporting evidence is primarily observational and lacks validation against known ground truth.
