## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims made in the abstract
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present the Atlas model family, a set of open-source protein language models and structure prediction tools. AtlasLM-3B, a 3B-parameter model trained on ~1.56 billion sequences including metagenomic data, outperforms ESM2-3B in unsupervised contact prediction. AtlasFold, built on these representations, achieves state-of-the-art accuracy among PLM-based folding models for monomer prediction. AtlasFold-Multimer (AtlasFold-M), a fine-tuned version for complexes, shows antibody–antigen prediction performance comparable to AlphaFold3 and ESMFold2. The system is designed for fast, memory-efficient inference, and all code, data, and weights are released under the MIT License.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or results sections provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, including methods, training details, benchmark datasets, evaluation metrics, comparison protocols, and all figures/tables. Without these, the core claims cannot be independently verified.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents an ambitious and potentially impactful system for protein structure prediction using metagenomic-scale language models. The claims of state-of-the-art performance among PLM-based models and comparability to AlphaFold3 and ESMFold2 are noteworthy. However, the abstract lacks sufficient detail to assess the validity, rigor, and novelty of these claims. The absence of any quantitative results, benchmark specifications, or methodological innovations makes it impossible to evaluate the technical soundness or scientific importance of the work from this material alone.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists, structural biologists, and researchers in protein engineering and drug discovery would be interested. The promise of a fast, memory-efficient, open-source PLM-based folding system that rivals established methods like AlphaFold3 and ESMFold2 could have broad utility for high-throughput structure prediction and antibody design.
- **Major strengths** 1. The scale of training data (~1.56 billion sequences including metagenomic data) is a clear strength, potentially capturing greater evolutionary diversity than existing models. 2. The commitment to open-source release (code, data, weights under MIT License) is commendable and would accelerate community adoption and further development. 3. The unified framework spanning language modeling, monomer folding, and complex prediction is a practical and ambitious design.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
    - **Claim pointer** "AtlasFold predicts all-atom protein structures and achieves state-of-the-art accuracy among PLM-based folding models."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The abstract provides no quantitative metrics, benchmark names, or comparison datasets to support the claim of "state-of-the-art accuracy." It is unclear which metrics (e.g., pLDDT, TM-score, lDDT) were used, which PLM-based models were compared (e.g., ESMFold, OmegaFold, RGN2), and on which test sets (e.g., CAMEO, CASP, or a custom holdout). Without this information, the claim is unsubstantiated.
    - **Why it matters** "State-of-the-art" is a strong, comparative claim that requires rigorous, reproducible benchmarking. The field of protein structure prediction is highly competitive, and readers need to see the exact performance numbers and comparison protocols to judge the significance of the advance.
    - **Resolution test** Provide a table or figure in the full manuscript showing AtlasFold's performance (e.g., pLDDT, TM-score) on standard benchmarks (e.g., CAMEO, CASP14/15 targets) alongside at least ESMFold, OmegaFold, and RGN2. Include error bars and statistical significance tests.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M2
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
    - **Claim pointer** "Fine-tuning AtlasFold for protein-complex prediction produces AtlasFold-Multimer (AtlasFold-M), whose antibody–antigen prediction performance is comparable to that of AlphaFold3 and ESMFold2."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The claim of "comparable" performance is vague and unsupported. No metrics (e.g., DockQ, iRMSD, Fnat, or antibody-specific metrics like CDR-H3 RMSD) are provided. The specific test set for antibody–antigen complexes is not named. "Comparable" could mean within 1% or within 20%, which are very different levels of performance.
    - **Why it matters** Antibody–antigen prediction is a critical application with high stakes for therapeutic design. Vague claims of comparability without quantitative backing are insufficient for a Nature-level audience. The community needs to know exactly how AtlasFold-M performs relative to these established methods.
    - **Resolution test** Provide a table or figure showing AtlasFold-M's performance on a standard antibody–antigen benchmark (e.g., the Antibody Benchmark from the Protein Data Bank or a recent CASP complex prediction set) with metrics such as DockQ, iRMSD, and CDR-H3 RMSD, compared directly to AlphaFold3 and ESMFold2.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M3
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
    - **Claim pointer** "AtlasLM-3B ... outperforms the similarly sized ESM2-3B in unsupervised contact prediction."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** No quantitative results are given for this claim. The metric for contact prediction (e.g., precision at L/5, top-L/2, or top-L) is not specified, nor is the benchmark dataset (e.g., CASP14/15 contact prediction targets, or a standard set like the Protein Contact Prediction Benchmark).
    - **Why it matters** Unsupervised contact prediction is a key test of a PLM's structural knowledge. A clear, quantitative comparison is needed to establish that AtlasLM-3B's larger and more diverse training data translates into better structural representations.
    - **Resolution test** Provide a figure or table showing contact prediction precision (e.g., at L/5, L/2, L) on a standard benchmark (e.g., CASP14/15 targets) for AtlasLM-3B and ESM2-3B, with error bars.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M4
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** No
    - **Axis** Methodological clarity
    - **Claim pointer** "This protein-specific folding architecture enables fast, memory-efficient inference with AtlasFold and AtlasFold-M."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The abstract does not describe the architectural innovations that lead to "fast, memory-efficient inference." It is unclear whether this is due to a novel architecture, a distillation technique, or simply the use of a smaller model. Without any description of the architecture or inference speed/memory benchmarks, this claim is unverifiable.
    - **Why it matters** Speed and memory efficiency are important practical advantages, especially for high-throughput applications. The claim needs to be supported by quantitative benchmarks (e.g., inference time per protein, GPU memory usage) compared to existing methods.
    - **Resolution test** Provide a table or figure in the full manuscript showing inference time (seconds per residue) and peak GPU memory usage for AtlasFold, ESMFold, and OmegaFold on a set of proteins of varying lengths. Describe the architectural features that contribute to efficiency.
- **Minor Comments**
    - **Concern ID** R1-m1
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Clarity
    - **Affected element** Model naming
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract
    - **Issue** The abstract introduces "AtlasFold-Multimer (AtlasFold-M)" but the relationship between "AtlasFold" and "AtlasFold-M" is not fully clear. Is AtlasFold-M simply a fine-tuned version of the same architecture, or does it involve architectural changes?

    - **Concern ID** R1-m2

    - **Axis** Completeness
    - **Affected element** Training data description
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract

    - **Concern ID** R1-m3

    - **Affected element** Comparison scope
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract
    - **Issue** The abstract compares AtlasFold to "PLM-based folding models" and AtlasFold-M to AlphaFold3 and ESMFold2. It is unclear whether AlphaFold3 is considered a PLM-based model or a different category. This could confuse readers.
    - **Required correction** Clarify the categorization of models in the full manuscript. For example, state that AlphaFold3 uses a different architecture (e.g., diffusion-based) and is included as a top-performing baseline, not as a PLM-based model.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3. The core performance claims for monomer folding, complex prediction, and contact prediction are entirely unsupported by quantitative evidence in the abstract. These must be addressed with full benchmarking data in the manuscript.

The originality cannot be fully assessed without the full manuscript.
    - **Scientific importance**: Potentially high. If the performance claims are validated, a fast, open-source, and accurate PLM-based folding system would be a significant resource for the structural biology community. The inclusion of complex prediction and antibody–antigen modeling further increases potential impact.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership**: High. The work would be of interest to computational biologists, structural biologists, bioinformaticians, and researchers in drug discovery and protein engineering.
    - **Technical soundness**: Cannot be assessed from the abstract alone. The lack of any quantitative results, benchmark details, or methodological descriptions prevents evaluation of the technical rigor.
    - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The abstract is clear and well-structured for a specialist audience. Terms like "masked language modeling" and "unsupervised contact prediction" are standard in the field. A nonspecialist might need additional context, but this is acceptable for an abstract.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract makes strong, potentially impactful claims, but provides no quantitative data to support them. A full manuscript with rigorous benchmarking, clear methodological descriptions, and statistical comparisons is required before the case for publication in a high-impact journal can be made. The recommendation is supportive if the technical concerns are resolved with compelling evidence.
