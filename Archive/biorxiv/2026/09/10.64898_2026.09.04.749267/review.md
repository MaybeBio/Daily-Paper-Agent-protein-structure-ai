## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript (bioRxiv preprint)
- **Assessment boundary** Claims, methods, results, and conclusions as presented in the provided text
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report the generation of 5.6 million novel Rubisco-like sequences using protein language models (ProGen-2 and ESM-2), from which 21 candidates were predicted to be active. Six designs were soluble in *E. coli*, five produced quantifiable 3PGA, and one showed an apparent CO₂/O₂ specificity estimate beyond the range of natural Rubiscos assayed. A crystal structure of one design confirmed predicted dimer and active-site geometry with sub-angstrom Cα agreement. The authors conclude that sequence-only generation followed by structural filtering can recover active Rubiscos from previously unexplored sequence space.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript including methods, figures, tables, supplementary data, sequence alignments, experimental protocols, structural statistics, and specificity assay details.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially transformative approach to Rubisco engineering using protein language models. The concept of generating de novo Rubisco sequences that are soluble, active, and structurally validated is highly novel and could address a long-standing challenge in synthetic biology. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the robustness of the computational pipeline, the rigor of the experimental validation, and the validity of the specificity claim. Critical methodological and quantitative details are absent, preventing a full assessment of the work's technical soundness and reproducibility.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in synthetic biology, protein engineering, carbon fixation, and computational protein design would be interested. The work offers a scalable strategy for exploring sequence space beyond natural evolution, which could have implications for improving Rubisco and other recalcitrant enzymes. The use of protein language models for de novo design of a complex, multi-subunit enzyme is a significant methodological advance.
- **Major strengths** 1. The use of protein language models to generate sequences beyond natural diversity for a notoriously difficult enzyme is a conceptually novel and ambitious approach. 2. The combination of computational generation with experimental validation (solubility, activity, and crystal structure) provides a multi-pronged evidence base. 3. The claim of achieving a specificity estimate beyond natural Rubiscos, if validated, would represent a major breakthrough.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Experimental validation and data completeness
    - **Claim pointer** "One design produced an apparent CO₂/O₂ specificity estimate beyond the range of the natural representative Rubiscos assayed."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The abstract does not describe the assay used to determine CO₂/O₂ specificity, the number of replicates, the error margins, or the specific natural Rubiscos used as comparators. "Apparent" specificity is a critical qualifier that suggests the measurement may not be a true kinetic specificity (τ or Srel). Without details on the assay (e.g., radiometric, mass spectrometry, or coupled enzyme), the claim is unverifiable.
    - **Why it matters** This is the most impactful claim in the abstract. If the specificity measurement is not rigorous or is based on a non-standard assay, the central conclusion of the paper is undermined. The field has a history of over-interpreting apparent specificity values.
    - **Resolution test** Provide full details of the specificity assay, including the method, number of replicates, error bars, and a direct comparison to well-characterized natural Rubiscos (e.g., from spinach, *Rhodospirillum rubrum*, or *Synechococcus*) under identical conditions. The data must be presented in a figure with statistical analysis.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M2
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Computational pipeline validation
    - **Claim pointer** "We generated 5.6 million novel Rubisco-like sequences and identified 21 highly diverse candidates predicted to be active."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The abstract does not describe the criteria for "predicted to be active." The ESM-2 discriminator's training data, threshold, and performance metrics (e.g., precision, recall, false positive rate) are not provided. The diversity of the 21 candidates is asserted but not quantified (e.g., sequence identity to natural Rubiscos, phylogenetic distance).
    - **Why it matters** Without understanding the discriminator's reliability, the reader cannot assess whether the 21 candidates are truly promising or are artifacts of the model. The claim of exploring "unprecedented" sequence space requires a quantitative comparison to known Rubisco sequences.
    - **Resolution test** Provide the discriminator's architecture, training data, and performance on a held-out test set. Show a sequence identity matrix or phylogenetic tree comparing the 21 candidates to known Rubisco families. Define the threshold for "predicted to be active" and justify it.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M3
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Structural validation
    - **Claim pointer** "We also solved the crystal structure of one de novo design that reproduced the predicted dimer and active-site geometry with sub-angstrom Cα agreement."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The abstract does not state the resolution of the crystal structure, the R-factors (Rwork/Rfree), or the method used to calculate the Cα RMSD. "Sub-angstrom Cα agreement" is vague; a value of 0.9 Å is sub-angstrom but may not be convincing for a de novo design. The comparison is between the predicted model and the experimental structure, but the method for generating the predicted model is not described.
    - **Why it matters** Structural validation is a key strength of the paper. Without standard crystallographic statistics, the quality of the structure and the accuracy of the prediction cannot be evaluated. The field requires high-resolution structures (e.g., <2.5 Å) with good R-factors to support claims of accurate design.
    - **Resolution test** Provide the crystallographic data collection and refinement statistics in a table. Report the exact Cα RMSD value and the method used to align the predicted and experimental structures. Show a superposition figure of the active site.

    - **Concern ID** R1-m1

    - **Required correction** Specify the exact Rubisco types (e.g., Form II, Form III, or a specific set of sequences) used to fine-tune ProGen-2.
    - **Concern ID** R1-m2

    - **Axis** Data presentation

    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided

    - **Required correction** Report the specific activity or kcat values for the five active designs, with error bars, and compare them to a positive control (e.g., a natural Rubisco expressed under the same conditions).
    - **Concern ID** R1-m3
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Reproducibility
    - **Affected element** Abstract text
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided

    - **Required correction** State the number of replicates (e.g., "n=3 independent experiments") for all experimental data.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of a CO₂/O₂ specificity estimate beyond natural Rubiscos is unsupported without full assay details and statistical comparison.
- The claim that the 21 candidates occupy "regions of Rubisco phylogenetic space not previously observed in nature" is unsupported without a phylogenetic analysis.
- The claim of "sub-angstrom Cα agreement" is unsupported without the exact RMSD value and crystallographic statistics.
- The claim that the strategy is "scalable" and provides a "broadly accessible path" is a forward-looking statement that is not directly supported by the data presented in the abstract.
