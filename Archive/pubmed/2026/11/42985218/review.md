## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, computational methods, results and discussion, conclusion) for the article "When does a funnel become a trap? A Gomboc-inspired model of protein folding landscapes" by Shityakov, Skorb, and Nosonovsky.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological soundness, validity of claims, and internal consistency of the presented evidence. No assessment of editorial fit or journal-specific policy matters.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose a two-dimensional computational model inspired by the Gömböc shape (a convex body with one stable and one unstable equilibrium) to study how landscape ruggedness affects protein folding outcomes. They report a sharp transition from efficient folding to trapping as a ruggedness parameter r increases, and claim qualitative correspondence between model success rates and the performance of Rosetta and AlphaFold3 on the TPR1 domain.
- **Visible evidence base** Full text including equations, simulation parameters, results tables/figures referenced (Table 1, Figs 1–4, Fig S1), and benchmark comparisons against Rosetta and AlphaFold3 for the TPR1 domain (PDB 1ELW).
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures and Table 1 are referenced but not provided in the submitted text. Supplementary information (Fig S1A–F) is referenced but not available. The GitHub repository link is provided but its contents were not assessed. Exact numerical values for some reported statistics (e.g., success rates at each r value) are not fully specified in the text.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents an interesting conceptual analogy between the Gömböc shape and protein folding landscapes, and the computational model is clearly described. However, the central claim of qualitative correspondence between model success rates and Rosetta/AF3 performance rests on a comparison of non-equivalent metrics across different systems, and the manuscript does not establish that the Gömböc-inspired model captures features beyond what standard Gō-like models already provide. The sharp transition in success rate with increasing r is presented as a key finding, but the mechanistic interpretation is underdeveloped and the apparent recovery at high r is dismissed as an artifact without rigorous demonstration. The benchmark comparison is suggestive but not quantitatively grounded. The manuscript would benefit from a clearer articulation of what specific, testable prediction the model makes that distinguishes it from existing approaches.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in protein folding theory, energy landscape analysis, and computational biophysics may find the Gömböc analogy conceptually appealing. The model's simplicity and computational efficiency could interest those seeking coarse-grained or conceptual frameworks for teaching or for generating hypotheses about frustration effects. The bridging of topology and folding may also appeal to mathematicians working on shape optimization or dynamical systems with applications in biophysics.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript is clearly written and the Gömböc analogy is pedagogically attractive. The computational model is simple, transparent, and reproducible in principle. The systematic sweep of the ruggedness parameter across five orders of magnitude is a reasonable approach. The use of two independent structure prediction methods (Rosetta and AF3) as benchmarks for the TPR1 domain is appropriate, and the reported energy landscape features for TPR1 are consistent with its known behavior as a well-folded domain.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Claim validity
  - **Claim pointer** "These findings suggest that the Gömböc-inspired model captures essential features of folding landscape quality and may serve as a useful heuristic for comparing foldability across different systems."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and discussion, "Gömböc-inspired model reveals frustration-dependent folding behavior"; "Rosetta ab initio simulations"; "AF3 validates the TPR1 domain"
  - **Concern** The central claim of qualitative correspondence between model success rates and Rosetta/AF3 performance is based on comparing fundamentally different quantities. The Gömböc model reports the fraction of optimization trajectories reaching an RMSD threshold in a 2D toy landscape, while Rosetta and AF3 report structural prediction accuracy for a real 3D protein. The numerical values (93% vs 90%, 83% vs 77%) are presented as if they were comparable, but there is no theoretical or statistical framework linking these quantities. The model success rate at r = 0 is 93%, which is claimed to correspond to AF3's 90% coverage, yet the model at r = 0 has no frustration at all, while AF3 operates on a real protein with real sequence-specific interactions. The comparison is therefore not a validation of the model but an exercise in pattern matching.
  - **Why it matters** If the model is to serve as a heuristic for comparing foldability, the correspondence must be established on a principled basis, not by aligning two numbers that happen to be similar. Without a defined mapping between model parameters and physical quantities, or between model success rates and prediction confidence metrics, the claim is unfalsifiable and cannot be extended to other systems.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a quantitative framework linking model success rates to physical observables (e.g., folding rates, Φ-values, or prediction confidence scores) and demonstrate predictive validity on at least two additional proteins with known frustration properties. Alternatively, clearly reframe the claim as a purely conceptual analogy with no quantitative predictive content.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** "At minimal ruggedness (10−8), the model achieves 83% success in reaching native-like states" and "At zero ruggedness (r = 0.0), the landscape acts as a near-perfect funnel, yielding an average energy drop of 129.5 ± 21.7 arbitrary units, convergence in 21 optimization steps, and a 93% success rate."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Results and discussion, "Gömböc-inspired model reveals frustration-dependent folding behavior"; Table 1 (referenced, not provided)
  - **Concern** The manuscript reports success rates at r = 0 (93%) and r = 10−8 (83%) but does not explain why adding even infinitesimal noise (10−8) causes a 10 percentage point drop. The energy function includes both a stochastic term (Erug) and a frustration term (Efrust), both scaled by r. At r = 10−8, the magnitude of these terms is negligible compared to the native attraction term, yet the success rate drops substantially. This is not explained mechanistically. Additionally, the text states that "the stochastic noise at extreme ruggedness values (10−4 and 10−3) causes the gradient-based optimizer to terminate prematurely; thus, the apparent recovery in the success rate is merely an artifact." This interpretation is plausible but not demonstrated. The manuscript does not report the distribution of final RMSD values, the fraction of trajectories terminating by iteration limit versus gradient tolerance, or any diagnostic to distinguish genuine folding from premature termination near the starting point.
  - **Why it matters** The sharp transition between r = 10−8 and r = 10−7 is the central quantitative finding of the model. If the transition is an artifact of the optimizer terminating prematurely rather than a property of the landscape, the model's relevance to physical folding is undermined. The manuscript must distinguish between landscape trapping (a physical effect) and optimizer failure (a numerical artifact).
  - **Resolution test** Report the fraction of trajectories that terminate by gradient tolerance versus iteration limit at each r value. Show the distribution of final RMSD values and energy drops. Demonstrate that the transition is robust to changes in optimizer (e.g., using a stochastic optimizer or simulated annealing) and to the choice of convergence criteria. If the transition is an optimizer artifact, the claim of landscape trapping must be revised.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Claim pointer** "Unlike conventional Gō models that encode only native contacts, the present formulation combines a monomonostatic (single-stable-equilibrium) geometric target with an explicit, tunable Efrust that penalizes non-native long-range distances and a stochastic ruggedness term Erug that injects local energy noise."
  - **Evidence pointer** Conclusion; Computational methods, "Energy function"
  - **Concern** The manuscript claims novelty relative to Gō-like models, but the energy function described is a standard structure-based model with added noise and a non-native contact penalty. The Gömböc analogy is presented as the conceptual motivation, but the model itself does not appear to use any property specific to Gömböc geometry. The native state is a 2D curve generated by Fourier synthesis, not a Gömböc shape, and the energy function does not encode monomonostatic properties. The connection to Gömböc is therefore purely metaphorical. The manuscript does not demonstrate that the Gömböc analogy generates any specific, testable prediction that a generic Gō-like model with tunable frustration would not also make.
  - **Why it matters** If the model is functionally equivalent to existing structure-based models with added noise, the Gömböc framing does not constitute a scientific contribution beyond the analogy itself. The manuscript must either demonstrate that the Gömböc-inspired construction leads to qualitatively different behavior than a generic Gō-like model, or reposition the work as a pedagogical or conceptual contribution rather than a novel computational method.
  - **Resolution test** Compare the model's behavior to a standard Gō-like model with the same native state and the same frustration terms but without the Gömböc-inspired construction. If the behavior is identical, the Gömböc framing is decorative. Alternatively, identify a specific prediction that arises from the monomonostatic property and test it.

  - **Issue** The abstract states "the quantitative effects of landscape ruggedness on folding kinetics are not fully understood," but the manuscript does not measure kinetics. The model reports success rates of optimization trajectories, not folding rates or timescales. The term "kinetics" is used loosely and may mislead readers.

  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Statistical rigor
  - **Affected element** Results and discussion, "Gömböc-inspired model reveals frustration-dependent folding behavior"
  - **Evidence pointer** Table 1 (referenced, not provided)
  - **Issue** The manuscript reports means and standard deviations for energy drops (e.g., 129.5 ± 21.7) but does not state the number of independent simulations per condition beyond "n = 100 per condition" in the methods. It is unclear whether the reported uncertainties are standard deviations across the 100 runs or standard errors. The manuscript also does not report whether the differences between conditions are statistically significant.
  - **Required correction** Clarify the uncertainty metric and report the number of simulations. Consider adding a statistical test (e.g., ANOVA or permutation test) to support claims of significant differences between ruggedness conditions.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Interpretation
  - **Affected element** Results and discussion, "AF3 validates the TPR1 domain as a minimally frustrated benchmark"
  - **Evidence pointer** Section text
  - **Issue** The manuscript states that "High-confidence AF3 predictions (pLDdt ≥ 90) with a uniform PAE plot are characteristic of well-folded, minimally frustrated systems." This is an interpretive claim about what AF3 confidence metrics indicate about frustration. AF3 confidence reflects prediction certainty, not the physical frustration of the folding landscape. A protein with high frustration could still be predicted with high confidence if the training set contains similar folds. The claim conflates prediction confidence with biophysical properties.
  - **Required correction** Soften the claim or provide a citation supporting the link between AF3 confidence metrics and physical frustration. Alternatively, reframe the AF3 results as evidence of structural predictability rather than minimal frustration.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m5
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Completeness
  - **Affected element** Results and discussion, "Gömböc-inspired model reveals frustration-dependent folding behavior"
  - **Evidence pointer** Section text
  - **Issue** The manuscript mentions "our earlier topological bioscaling analysis of Trp-cage folding" (reference 20) and states that the two approaches "provide a quantitative bridge between monomonostatic topology and the dimensional properties of native state recovery." However, the connection between the two models is not elaborated. It is unclear how the fractional-dimensional analysis relates to the Gömböc-inspired model or what new insight the combination provides.
  - **Required correction** Expand the discussion of how the two approaches complement each other, or remove the reference if it is not essential to the current work.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m6
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Reproducibility
  - **Affected element** Computational methods, "Numerical optimization"
  - **Evidence pointer** Section text
  - **Issue** The manuscript states that the model is "pattern-reproducible but not bit-reproducible" and that "reruns give different individual trajectories but the same ensemble statistics." This is a useful clarification, but the manuscript does not specify the random seed policy or whether the reported results are from a single run or averaged over multiple runs with different seeds.
  - **Required correction** Specify the random seed policy and report whether the results are averaged over multiple independent runs.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (claim of qualitative correspondence without a principled link between model and benchmark metrics) and R1-M2 (incomplete reporting of simulation diagnostics to distinguish landscape trapping from optimizer failure) are the primary technical issues. The incomplete equations for Erug and Efrust (R1-m2) also need correction for reproducibility.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The Gömböc analogy is novel as a conceptual framing, but the computational model itself does not demonstrate new physics or methodology beyond standard structure-based models with tunable frustration. Scientific importance: The question of how landscape ruggedness affects folding is important, but the manuscript does not establish that the toy model provides insights beyond what is already known from Gō-like models and energy landscape theory. Interdisciplinary readership: The manuscript bridges topology and biophysics in a way that could appeal to a broad audience, but the lack of quantitative rigor in the central comparison limits its impact. Technical soundness: The model is simple and clearly described, but the incomplete reporting of equations and diagnostics, and the conflation of prediction confidence with physical frustration, undermine technical rigor. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, with the Gömböc analogy providing an intuitive entry point. However, the abrupt transition from model results to benchmark comparisons without a clear logical bridge may confuse nonspecialist readers.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The conceptual framing is appealing and the model is simple enough to be useful as a pedagogical tool or a starting point for more rigorous work. However, the central claim of correspondence with Rosetta/AF3 performance must be either quantitatively grounded or explicitly reframed as a conceptual analogy. The simulation diagnostics must be reported to distinguish physical trapping from optimizer artifacts. If these issues are addressed, the manuscript could make a modest but useful contribution to the conceptual toolkit of protein folding research.
