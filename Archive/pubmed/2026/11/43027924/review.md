## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a timely and conceptually interesting perspective on the role of computational methods in PCSK9 therapeutic development, with a clear and defensible central argument about the under-exploitation of the CHRD. However, the abstract is largely programmatic and lacks specific evidence, case examples, or critical evaluation of the methods discussed. The claim that computational methods are identifying candidate sites beyond the LDLR-binding interface is asserted without supporting examples. The review's value will depend entirely on the depth and rigor of the full manuscript, which cannot be assessed from the abstract alone.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists working on protein dynamics and allosteric regulation, structural biologists studying PCSK9 or related proprotein convertases, drug discovery researchers focused on cardiovascular targets, and AI practitioners developing generative models for protein design would find this review relevant. The integration of physics-based and AI approaches is a topic of broad interest across these communities.
- **Major strengths** The abstract identifies a genuine and important gap in PCSK9 research, namely the neglect of the disordered CHRD in therapeutic development. The framing around gain- and loss-of-function variants as an organizing principle is sensible. The call for integrating generative AI with enhanced sampling is forward-looking and aligns with current trends in computational drug discovery.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract provides no concrete examples of computational studies that have identified candidate sites beyond the LDLR interface, no discussion of specific variant dynamics, and no critical assessment of the limitations of the methods mentioned. The central claim about diverging directions between drug discovery and computational methods is stated but not substantiated. The abstract reads more as a proposal than a review of existing work.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract could benefit from naming specific computational studies or tools that exemplify the trends described. The phrase "premier model for precision medicine" is somewhat grandiose and could be tempered. The final argument about the next advance would be strengthened by acknowledging potential challenges or counterarguments.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** None can be identified from the abstract alone, as no technical details or results are presented.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The perspective is moderately original in its emphasis on the CHRD and the AI-enhanced sampling integration, though similar arguments exist in the broader computational drug discovery literature. Scientific importance: High, given the clinical relevance of PCSK9 and the identified gap. Interdisciplinary readership: The abstract is accessible to a broad audience but would need more specific examples to engage specialists. Technical soundness: Cannot be assessed from the abstract; no methods or results are presented. Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is clearly written and avoids excessive jargon, though terms like "cryptic-pocket detection" and "enhanced sampling" may need brief explanation in the full text.
