### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The claim that pH 5.0 treatment followed by neutralization induces partial unfolding, increased alpha-helix content, and enhanced AFB1 binding affinity.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; specific spectroscopic data and simulation results not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that pH 5.0 treatment increases alpha-helix content relative to native Ara, which is counterintuitive given that acid-induced unfolding typically reduces ordered secondary structure. The authors describe this as "partial unfolding" while also reporting increased alpha-helix content, which requires careful reconciliation. Without quantitative data from circular dichroism or other techniques, and without details on how secondary structure fractions were calculated, this claim is difficult to evaluate.  
- **Why it matters** The bidirectional mechanism hinges on the distinct conformational outcomes at pH 5.0 versus pH 3.0. If the secondary structure assignments are incorrect or the spectroscopic data are overinterpreted, the entire mechanistic framework could be compromised.  
- **Resolution test** Provide raw and fitted spectroscopic data (e.g., CD spectra with deconvolution results) and simulation trajectories showing secondary structure evolution over time. Clarify how "partial unfolding" coexists with increased alpha-helix content, and include statistical comparisons with native Ara.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The claim that pH 3.0 treatment causes dissociation that is not reversed upon neutralization, leading to decreased AFB1 binding capacity.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no data on protein oligomeric state or binding affinity provided  
- **Concern** The abstract reports irreversible dissociation at pH 3.0 but does not specify how dissociation was measured (e.g., size-exclusion chromatography, native PAGE, dynamic light scattering) or how "not reversed" was confirmed. Additionally, the relationship between dissociation and reduced AFB1 binding is asserted but not mechanistically detailed. It is unclear whether reduced binding is due to loss of binding sites, conformational changes, or aggregation effects.  
- **Why it matters** The claim of irreversibility is central to the bidirectional model. If the dissociation is partially reversible or if the reduced binding has an alternative explanation, the conclusions would need substantial revision.  
- **Resolution test** Provide experimental evidence of oligomeric state before and after neutralization, and include binding affinity measurements (e.g., fluorescence quenching, isothermal titration calorimetry) with appropriate controls. Demonstrate that the observed reduction in binding is specifically due to irreversible dissociation.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The claim that AFB1 may be physically entrapped within acid-induced protein aggregates, contributing to increased difficulty of detection and detoxification.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no data on aggregate formation or entrapment provided  
- **Concern** The entrapment mechanism is presented as a notable finding, but the abstract provides no direct evidence for physical entrapment. It is unclear whether this is a hypothesis or a conclusion supported by data. If it is a hypothesis, it should be framed as such. If it is a conclusion, the authors must provide evidence such as microscopy, light scattering, or release experiments demonstrating that AFB1 is trapped within aggregates rather than simply bound with lower affinity.  
- **Why it matters** This claim has significant practical implications for detection and detoxification strategies. Overstating the evidence could mislead applied research in food safety.  
- **Resolution test** Provide direct experimental evidence of entrapment, such as confocal microscopy with labeled AFB1, or comparative release kinetics from aggregates under different conditions. Clearly distinguish between binding and entrapment in the interpretation.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Originality  
- **Claim pointer** The claim that this study reveals "for the first time" a bidirectional regulatory mechanism by which pH-driven conformational remodeling of Ara modulates AFB1 binding.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no literature comparison provided  
- **Concern** The novelty claim is strong, but the abstract does not summarize prior work on arachin-AFB1 interactions or pH effects on mycotoxin binding to food proteins. Without a clear statement of what is known and how this study differs, the "first time" assertion cannot be evaluated.  
- **Why it matters** Novelty is a key criterion for publication in high-impact journals. An unsupported novelty claim could be challenged by reviewers or readers familiar with the literature.  
- **Resolution test** In the full manuscript, provide a thorough literature review demonstrating that no prior study has reported bidirectional pH-dependent modulation of AFB1 binding by arachin or similar proteins. Soften the claim if any related findings exist.
