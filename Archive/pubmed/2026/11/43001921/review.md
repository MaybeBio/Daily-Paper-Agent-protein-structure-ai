## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no access to full manuscript, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that AlphaFold3 (AF3) pseudo-ensembles, generated by sampling multiple models, can predict parallel versus antiparallel β-sheet organization in short amyloidogenic peptides, with 112 of 117 peptides correctly assigned. They further claim that simultaneous sampling of both organizations indicates structural diversity, exemplified by MVGGVVIA and LYIQWL. They acknowledge limitations in steric-zipper architecture prediction and in handling cosolvents and metal ions.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no quantitative data, methodological details, or validation statistics beyond the reported 112/117 accuracy
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full methods, model generation parameters, peptide dataset composition, definition of "clear preference," statistical analysis, comparison to baseline or alternative methods, and any figures or tables supporting the claims

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially useful application of AF3 pseudo-ensembles to a challenging problem in amyloid peptide structure prediction. The reported accuracy of 112/117 is promising, but the abstract lacks sufficient methodological detail and validation context to fully assess the robustness and generalizability of the approach. The claims regarding structural diversity and the limitations are plausible but require supporting evidence from the full manuscript.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in computational structural biology, amyloid biology, and peptide biophysics would find these results relevant. The work addresses a gap in applying deep learning-based structure prediction to polymorphic amyloid systems, which could aid in sequence-based screening for amyloidogenic peptides and in understanding β-sheet organization determinants.
- **Major strengths** The study tackles a biologically and clinically relevant problem with a state-of-the-art tool. The use of pseudo-ensembles to capture conformational diversity is conceptually sound and aligns with emerging uses of AlphaFold beyond single-structure prediction. The reported accuracy is high, and the acknowledgment of limitations adds credibility.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract does not provide details on how "clear preference" is defined, how the 117-peptide test set was curated, or how the accuracy was computed. The claim about structural diversity is based on only two examples, which is insufficient to establish a general signature. The limitations on steric-zipper and solvent/ion effects are stated but not quantified, leaving the practical utility unclear.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract would benefit from a brief statement on the computational cost or runtime of generating pseudo-ensembles. The term "pseudo-ensembles" is used without definition, which may confuse nonspecialist readers. The relevance of the findings to experimental amyloid studies could be more explicitly stated.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The definition of "clear preference" and the criteria for correct assignment must be specified. The statistical significance of the 112/117 accuracy relative to a null model or random assignment should be provided. The generalizability of the structural diversity signature beyond the two examples needs demonstration.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: Moderate to high, as applying AF3 pseudo-ensembles to amyloid β-sheet organization is a novel angle. Scientific importance: Moderate, with potential impact on amyloid prediction but limited by the acknowledged inaccuracies in detailed architecture. Interdisciplinary readership: Moderate, appealing to computational and experimental amyloid researchers but not broad enough for a general audience. Technical soundness: Not fully assessable from the abstract; requires full methods and validation. Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is accessible but uses jargon such as "pseudo-ensembles" and "steric-zipper" without sufficient context.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved, particularly regarding the definition of prediction criteria, dataset curation, and statistical validation. The core idea is promising, but the abstract alone does not establish the case fully.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Methodological clarity
- **Claim pointer** The claim that AF3 pseudo-ensembles correctly assign 112 of 117 peptides for which a clear preference is predicted.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract does not define what constitutes a "clear preference" for parallel versus antiparallel organization. It is unclear whether this is based on a confidence threshold, a majority vote across models, or another criterion. Without this definition, the accuracy metric is ambiguous and cannot be independently evaluated.
- **Why it matters** The central claim of predictive accuracy hinges on the assignment criteria. If the threshold for "clear preference" is lenient, the accuracy may be inflated; if stringent, the applicability may be limited. This ambiguity undermines the reproducibility and interpretability of the result.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript must specify the exact algorithm for determining "clear preference," including any thresholds, scoring functions, or model aggregation methods. A sensitivity analysis showing how accuracy varies with different thresholds would strengthen the claim.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Validation and generalizability
- **Claim pointer** The claim that simultaneous sampling of both β-sheet organizations provides a signature of structural diversity, as shown for MVGGVVIA and LYIQWL.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** The structural diversity claim is based on only two peptide examples. This is insufficient to establish a generalizable signature, especially given the acknowledged polymorphism of amyloid peptides. The abstract does not indicate whether this pattern was observed across a broader set or whether it is a post hoc observation.
- **Why it matters** If the diversity signature is not robust across multiple peptides, the claim may be overgeneralized. The utility of the approach for identifying polymorphic sequences depends on the reliability of this signature, which is not demonstrated.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should provide a systematic analysis of the frequency and consistency of dual-organization sampling across the full peptide set, with statistical comparison to peptides with a single predicted organization. Additional examples beyond the two cited would be necessary to support the claim.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Quantitative rigor
- **Claim pointer** The claim that AF3 pseudo-ensembles are "accurate predictors" of β-sheet organization, with 112 of 117 correct.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract reports a raw accuracy count but does not provide a baseline for comparison. It is unclear how this accuracy compares to random assignment, to simpler sequence-based predictors, or to other structure prediction tools. The absence of a null model or comparator makes it difficult to assess the added value of the AF3 approach.
- **Why it matters** Without a baseline, the reported accuracy may not reflect a meaningful improvement over existing methods. The claim of predictive utility requires context to establish that the approach is not merely performing at chance level or being outperformed by simpler alternatives.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should include a comparison to a suitable baseline, such as random assignment, a sequence-based predictor, or a simpler structural model. Confidence intervals or statistical tests for the accuracy should also be provided.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Terminology clarity
- **Affected element** "Pseudo-ensembles"
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Issue** The term "pseudo-ensembles" is introduced without definition. It is not clear whether this refers to a specific sampling strategy, a set of independently generated models, or a particular post-processing method.
- **Required correction** Define "pseudo-ensembles" in the abstract or introduction, specifying how the models are generated and aggregated.

- **Concern ID** R1-m2
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Practical relevance
- **Affected element** Limitations on steric-zipper and solvent/ion effects
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that steric-zipper prediction is biased and that cosolvent and metal ion effects are not reproduced, but it does not quantify the extent of these limitations or their practical impact on the method's utility.
- **Required Correction** Provide a brief quantitative summary of the limitations, such as the fraction of cases where steric-zipper prediction fails or the types of coordination geometries that are mispredicted, to help readers gauge the severity.

- **Concern ID** R1-m3
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Readability
- **Affected element** "Clear preference" criterion
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Issue** The phrase "for which a clear preference is predicted" is vague and may confuse readers about the scope of the accuracy claim.
- **Required Correction** Clarify whether the 112 of 117 accuracy applies to all peptides in the test set or only to a subset where the model made a confident prediction, and specify the size of that subset.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that AF3 pseudo-ensembles are "accurate predictors" of β-sheet organization is not fully supported without a defined prediction criterion and a baseline comparison.
- The claim that simultaneous sampling provides a "signature of structural diversity" is unsupported from the abstract alone, as it is based on only two examples.
- The statement that prediction in the presence of cosolvents and metal ions "does not reproduce solvent-mediated interfaces or noncanonical coordination geometries" is not quantifiable from the abstract and requires detailed evidence.
- The overall utility of the approach for "identifying amyloidogenic sequences with potential structural diversity" is not established without broader validation.
