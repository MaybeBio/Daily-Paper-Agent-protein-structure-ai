Prolines in the transmembrane (TM) region play a pivotal role in the function of G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs). In this molecular simulation study, 2 independent sets of trajectories are analyzed for 7 mutations of TM prolines of beta(2)-adrenergic receptor in agonist (Ag)- and inverse-agonist (IAg)-bound states and compared with the wild-type (WT) receptor. A set of structural and contact-based signatures that consistently separate activating from inactivating mutations is identified. While mutation does not substantially alter local protein structure, it leads to numerous structural changes in the protein that affect activity, largely consistent with results of earlier experiments. Comparing the distance between Leu47(2x46) and Leu215(6x37) for Ag- and IAg-bound states with the WT reference, we find activation-like behavior in both trajectories for the 3 mutants experimentally found to be activating-like, and inactivation-like behavior in at least one trajectory for the 4 mutants found experimentally to be inactivating. In addition to the well-recognized TM6 swing, activating-like mutations generate other interhelical rearrangements. Changes involving the TM3-TM6 hydrophobic lock, DRY-TM6 interface, and DRY-NPxxY coupling are important features distinguishing activation-like from inactivation-like responses. A functional shift score is introduced, which helps clarify connections between contact and functional changes due to mutation.
