How GH29 alpha-L-fucosidases balance hydrolysis against transfucosylation at the structural level remains poorly defined, limiting rational engineering of these enzymes for glycoside synthesis. We report Thermo-Fuc, a thermostable GH29A alpha-L-fucosidase from a Thermotogaceae bacterium with an unusually alkaline pH optimum (9.0), a temperature optimum of 50 ℃, and a half-life beyond 6 h. The enzyme hydrolyzes diverse fucosylated substrates, favoring branched Lewis antigens (Leᵃ, Leˣ) over linear HMO backbones such as 2'-FL and 3-FL (K(m) = 1.015 mM, kcat = 0.00232 s(-)(1) for pNP-alpha-L-fucopyranoside), and under optimized conditions converts substrate to 2'-fucosyllactose at a 72.1% (mol/mol) transfucosylation ratio with high regioselectivity, confirmed by LC-MS and (1)H-NMR, among the highest yields reported for a wild-type GH29A enzyme. Sequence and structural analyses identified a conserved Asp219-Glu261 catalytic dyad, while molecular dynamics simulations revealed substrate-dependent flexibility across five active-site loops. The most catalytically productive complex had the lowest PC1 variance (17.4%) of all those tested, suggesting its activity depends on a broad, distributed range of active-site motions rather than a single dominant conformational switch. Together, these findings offer a testable, flexibility-based model of catalytic selectivity in GH29 fucosidases and mark Thermo-Fuc as a promising biocatalyst for enzymatic HMO synthesis.
