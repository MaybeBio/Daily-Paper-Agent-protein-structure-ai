## Review setup

- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract and importance statement
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report cryo-electron microscopy structures of a pre-frameshift ribosomal complex and four translocation intermediates from West Nile virus programmed -1 ribosomal frameshifting, including a semi-rotated state with partial 40S rotation and near-canonical tRNA positions. They propose that frameshifting is coordinated by RNA conformational heterogeneity, peptidyl-transferase-center remodeling, and nascent peptide exit-tunnel interactions, supported by molecular dynamics simulations and functional assays.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text and importance statement only; no figures, tables, methods, or numerical data provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all structural figures and maps, simulation details, functional assay data, statistical analyses, and experimental protocols

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially significant structural investigation of WNV -1 PRF, with a novel intermediate state and a tripartite regulatory model. However, the evidence base is limited to the abstract, and several claims require detailed validation that cannot be assessed from the supplied material. The work is likely of interest to the virology and translation machinery communities, but the strength of the conclusions cannot be fully evaluated without the full manuscript.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying viral gene expression mechanisms, particularly flaviviruses; structural biologists investigating ribosome dynamics and translocation; and scientists interested in RNA pseudoknot function and recoding mechanisms. The proposed coordinated regulation model may also attract those studying nascent peptide-ribosome interactions.
- **Major strengths** The identification of a previously uncharacterized semi-rotated state with partial 40S rotation and near-canonical tRNA positions is a notable structural finding. The integration of cryo-EM, simulations, and functional assays provides a multidisciplinary approach. The proposal that frameshifting is governed by coordinated coupling rather than a single element offers a conceptually integrative framework.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract lacks quantitative data, resolution information, and statistical validation for functional claims. The causal relationship between the semi-rotated state and frameshifting efficiency is not established. The nascent peptide interaction claim requires demonstration of specificity and functional relevance. The RNA conformational ensemble claim is based on modeling and simulations without direct experimental validation.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract would benefit from specifying the number of independent experiments and replicates for functional assays. The importance statement repeats abstract content without adding new information. The term "coordinated coupling" is used descriptively but not operationally defined.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3, R1-M4
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality is high given the novel intermediate state and integrative model. Scientific importance is potentially substantial for virology and translation fields, but the abstract does not provide sufficient evidence to confirm broad impact. Interdisciplinary readership is plausible given the combination of structural, computational, and functional approaches. Technical soundness cannot be assessed from the abstract alone. Readability for nonspecialists is adequate, though some terms such as "semi-rotated state" and "peptidyl-transferase-center" may require additional context for a general audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. Supportive if technical concerns are resolved in the full manuscript.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim to have resolved a pre-frameshift ribosomal complex and four translocation intermediates, including a previously uncharacterized semi-rotated state.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, structural results section; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract does not provide any resolution metrics, map quality indicators, or validation statistics for the cryo-EM structures. Without these, the assignment of a "semi-rotated state" and the distinction from known translocation intermediates cannot be verified.
- **Why it matters** Structural claims of novel states require rigorous validation to rule out artifacts from sample heterogeneity or refinement biases. The novelty of the semi-rotated state is central to the paper's contribution.
- **Resolution test** Provide Fourier shell correlation curves, local resolution maps, and validation statistics for each state. Demonstrate that the semi-rotated state is reproducibly observed across independent datasets and is distinct from previously published intermediates.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that disruption of the tunnel-occupying peptide reduced frameshifting efficiency.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, functional analyses; location not provided
- **Concern** No quantitative data, effect sizes, or statistical significance are reported. It is unclear how the peptide was disrupted, whether controls were included, and whether the effect is specific to the frameshift signal or reflects general translation defects.
- **Why it matters** The nascent peptide interaction is a key component of the proposed coordinated regulation model. Without robust functional evidence, the causal role of the peptide remains speculative.
- **Resolution test** Report frameshifting efficiencies with and without peptide disruption, including replicate numbers, statistical tests, and appropriate controls such as synonymous mutations or unrelated peptide substitutions.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The authors suggest that the WNV frameshift-stimulatory RNA exists as a compact but conformationally heterogeneous ensemble capable of adopting multiple stable or metastable pseudoknot architectures.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, structural modeling and simulations; location not provided
- **Concern** This claim is based on modeling and molecular dynamics simulations, but no experimental validation such as chemical probing, single-molecule FRET, or mutational analysis is described. The biological relevance of the predicted ensemble is not demonstrated.
- **Why it matters** The RNA conformational heterogeneity is a central pillar of the proposed model. Without experimental support, the claim remains computational speculation.
- **Resolution test** Provide experimental evidence for conformational heterogeneity, such as selective 2'-hydroxyl acylation analyzed by primer extension or single-molecule measurements, and correlate predicted states with functional outcomes.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Scientific importance
- **Claim pointer** The authors conclude that WNV frameshifting is governed by coordinated coupling of RNA dynamics, ribosome remodeling, and nascent-chain interactions.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, conclusions; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract presents each component as a separate observation, but the "coordinated coupling" claim implies a mechanistic integration that is not directly demonstrated. No experiments are described that test the interdependence of these elements.
- **Why it matters** The integrative model is the main conceptual advance claimed. Without evidence for coordination, the conclusion overreaches the presented data.
- **Resolution test** Design experiments that perturb one component and measure effects on the others, such as mutations in the RNA that alter ribosome remodeling or peptide interactions, and show correlated changes in frameshifting.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m3

- **Required correction** Define what is meant by coordination, such as temporal ordering or mutual dependence, and specify the predicted relationship among the three components.

## Risk / unsupported claims

- The existence and functional relevance of the semi-rotated state as a distinct intermediate cannot be evaluated without structural validation data.
- The claim that nascent peptide exit-tunnel interactions influence frameshifting is unsupported without quantitative functional data and specificity controls.
- The predicted RNA conformational ensemble is unsupported without experimental validation.
- The coordinated regulation model is not established from the abstract alone, as no interdependence experiments are described.
- The generalizability of the findings to other flaviviruses or -1 PRF systems is not addressed and should not be inferred.
