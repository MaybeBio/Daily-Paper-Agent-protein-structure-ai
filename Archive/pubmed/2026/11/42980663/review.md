## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a high-level narrative connecting evolutionary biology and computational protein science, culminating in AlphaFold. The historical and conceptual framing is plausible and potentially of interest, but the abstract provides no technical detail, no evidence, and no specific claims that can be evaluated. As submitted, the material is insufficient to assess the scientific contribution of the full paper.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Evolutionary biologists studying protein sequence-structure relationships, computational biologists working on protein structure prediction, and researchers interested in the conceptual history of AlphaFold. The interdisciplinary bridge between molecular evolution and machine learning could attract readers from both communities.
- **Major strengths** The abstract identifies a genuinely important conceptual thread, namely the evolutionary rationale underlying coevolution-based structure prediction. The connection between mutual information, direct coupling analysis, and deep learning is historically accurate and intellectually compelling. The symposium context lends a community perspective that could add value.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract is entirely descriptive and contains no testable claims, no data, and no methodological content. The scientific contribution of the paper itself is not stated. The claim that coevolution "revolutionized" the field is asserted without supporting evidence or qualification. The relationship between the symposium and the paper's content is unclear.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract reads more like an editorial or meeting report than a research article. The phrase "evolutionary intelligence" is introduced without definition. The list of authors is long relative to the apparent scope of the abstract, raising questions about individual contributions.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** None can be identified from the abstract alone, as no technical content is presented.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: the conceptual synthesis is not new, though the specific framing may be. Scientific importance: potentially high if the full paper provides novel analysis or synthesis, but not demonstrable from the abstract. Interdisciplinary readership: the topic bridges evolution and computational biology, which is promising. Technical soundness: not assessable from the abstract. Readability for nonspecialists: the abstract is accessible, though terms like "direct couplings" and "deep neural networks" are used without explanation.

## Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the inference of evolutionary correlations "produced very powerful developments that revolutionized the field of protein structure and function prediction."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract asserts a revolutionary impact without presenting any data, benchmarks, comparisons, or specific examples that substantiate this claim. No evidence is provided for the magnitude of the advance, the scope of the impact, or the causal link between coevolution inference and AlphaFold's success.
- **Why it matters** A claim of revolution requires evidence of transformative change. Without quantitative or qualitative support, the statement remains an opinion rather than a scientific finding. For a paper that presumably contains substantive content, the abstract should signal the evidence base.
- **Resolution test** The full paper should provide specific examples, performance metrics, historical documentation, or comparative analyses that demonstrate the claimed impact. The abstract should summarize at least one concrete finding or conclusion.
