## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no access to full manuscript, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose a LASSO-based feature selection framework applied to 145 training proteins, identifying six structural features from 12 parameters, and use these to build four machine learning models (RR, RF, SVM, SSA-BP) for protein folding rate prediction. Performance is evaluated on 29 test proteins with bootstrap resampling. RF achieves lowest RMSE and MAE; SSA-BP achieves highest correlation coefficient (R = 0.9282). Folding-type-specific analysis and SHAP interpretation are reported.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no numerical results beyond R = 0.9282, no figures, tables, or methodological details
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all quantitative performance metrics (RMSE, MAE values), dataset composition and source, hyperparameter settings, bootstrap details, SHAP analysis outputs, and any validation or comparison against existing methods

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a plausible and potentially useful framework for protein folding rate prediction, combining LASSO feature selection with multiple machine learning models and interpretability analysis. However, the evidence provided in the abstract is insufficient to evaluate the robustness, novelty, or generalizability of the claims. Key quantitative results are missing, and the comparative claims about model performance cannot be verified from the supplied material. The work may be of interest to the protein biophysics and computational biology communities, but the case is not established from the abstract alone.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in computational biology, protein biophysics, and machine learning applied to biological problems would be interested. The framework addresses a practical need for interpretable and accurate prediction of protein folding rates, which is relevant to understanding protein dynamics and folding mechanisms. The use of SHAP for model interpretation may appeal to those seeking explainable AI approaches in bioinformatics.
- **Major strengths** The abstract clearly identifies a specific problem (limited generalizability and multicollinearity of existing structural parameters) and proposes a coherent pipeline (feature selection, modeling, interpretation). The use of bootstrap resampling for uncertainty assessment is a positive methodological choice. The inclusion of folding-type-specific analysis and SHAP interpretation suggests attention to model behavior beyond aggregate metrics.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The abstract reports only one performance metric (R = 0.9282 for SSA-BP) and does not provide the actual RMSE and MAE values for any model. Without these numbers, the comparative claim that "RF achieved the lowest RMSE and MAE" cannot be assessed.
  - R1-M2: The claim that LASSO identified six features "from 12 structural parameters" is presented without any detail on the selection procedure, stability of selection, or validation that these features are indeed the most informative. The abstract does not state whether the same features were used across all models or whether feature selection was performed within cross-validation, which is a common source of bias.
  - R1-M3: The abstract claims "distinct model performance patterns between two-state and multi-state proteins" but provides no quantitative support. The reader cannot determine the magnitude or direction of these differences, nor whether they are statistically significant.
  - R1-M4: The SHAP analysis claim that "relative contributions of structural features varied across models and folding types" is not supported by any specific results. The abstract does not report which features dominated for which model or folding type, nor how these contributions were quantified.
  - R1-M5: The training set of 145 proteins and test set of 29 proteins are mentioned, but the abstract does not state how the split was performed, whether the test set is truly independent, or whether the dataset is publicly available. This limits the reproducibility assessment.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract does not specify the units or definition of "folding rate" used, which is important for interpreting the RMSE and MAE values.
  - R1-m2: The choice of the four models (RR, RF, SVM, SSA-BP) is not justified. The abstract does not explain why these particular models were selected or how they complement each other.
  - R1-m3: The abstract mentions "bootstrap resampling used to assess performance uncertainty" but does not report any confidence intervals or uncertainty measures. The reader cannot evaluate the stability of the reported performance.
  - R1-m4: The term "SSA-BP" is not defined in the abstract. If this is a hybrid model (e.g., sparrow search algorithm optimized backpropagation), it should be stated explicitly.
  - R1-m5: The abstract does not compare the proposed framework against existing protein folding rate prediction methods. Without such a comparison, the added value of the approach is unclear.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3, R1-M4, R1-M5. The core comparative and interpretability claims are not supported by the evidence provided in the abstract.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The combination of LASSO feature selection with multiple ML models and SHAP interpretation for protein folding rate prediction is not clearly novel from the abstract alone. Similar approaches have been used in other bioinformatics prediction tasks. The abstract does not articulate what is new compared to prior work.
  - Scientific importance: Protein folding rate prediction is a relevant problem, but the abstract does not demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves substantially better performance or provides new biological insight beyond existing methods.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work sits at the intersection of biophysics and machine learning, which could attract a broad readership. However, the abstract is written in a way that assumes familiarity with both fields, and the lack of quantitative detail limits accessibility.
  - Technical soundness: The methodological choices (LASSO, bootstrap, SHAP) are reasonable, but the abstract does not provide enough detail to assess whether they were implemented correctly. The absence of key performance metrics is a significant technical shortcoming.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is concise but uses field-specific terms (e.g., L(eff), LRO, ACO, Sheet, H(nc), P(beta)) without definition. A nonspecialist would struggle to understand the significance of these features.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract presents a reasonable framework, but the lack of quantitative results and methodological detail prevents a supportive recommendation. The authors should provide the full manuscript with complete performance metrics, feature selection details, and SHAP outputs for a proper evaluation.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "RF achieved the lowest RMSE and MAE" is unsupported because the actual values are not reported.
- The claim that "SSA-BP showed the highest correlation coefficient (R = 0.9282)" is supported only by a single number with no context (e.g., confidence interval, comparison to other models' R values).
- The claim of "distinct model performance patterns between two-state and multi-state proteins" is unsupported by any quantitative evidence.
- The claim that "SHAP analysis further showed that the relative contributions of structural features varied across models and folding types" is unsupported by specific results.
- The generalizability claim ("providing an interpretable approach for protein folding rate prediction") is not established because the abstract does not demonstrate performance on external or independent datasets beyond the 29-protein test set.
