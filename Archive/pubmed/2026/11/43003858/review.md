## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, figure legends) provided as plain text. No figures, tables, or supplementary files were available for direct inspection.
- **Assessment boundary** Assessment is limited to the scientific content, methodological soundness, and internal consistency of the claims as presented in the text. Statistical analyses, simulation details, and experimental validations are evaluated based on the descriptions provided.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use multi-microsecond all-atom molecular dynamics simulations of a 225-residue "mini-tropoelastin" construct (domains 10-19 of human tropoelastin) to quantify protein conformational entropy, hydration-water entropy, and interaction enthalpies in free and fully extended states. They report that hydration-water entropy loss dominates the unfavorable entropic contribution to elastin stretching, exceeding the protein conformational entropy loss, and that opposing enthalpic contributions from disrupted intra-protein interactions and strengthened protein-water interactions largely compensate, yielding a small net free-energy change.
- **Visible evidence base** Text descriptions of Figures 1-7, Supplementary Figures S1-S10, Supplementary Tables, and Methods section. Numerical values for key thermodynamic quantities are reported in the text.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Actual figures, figure panels, supplementary figures, source data files, simulation parameter files, and convergence analyses were not available for direct examination. This limits verification of the quantitative results and the robustness of the statistical analyses.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a long-standing and important question in elastin biophysics: the molecular origin of entropic elasticity. The authors present a sophisticated computational approach that separately quantifies protein conformational and hydration-water entropy changes during stretching, which is a significant methodological advance over previous studies that treated the system as a whole. The central claim that hydration water provides the dominant unfavorable entropic contribution is clearly stated and supported by the reported numbers. However, several technical concerns regarding the interpretation of the fully extended state, the sensitivity of the entropy calculations to simulation parameters, and the generalizability of the mini-tropoelastin model to full-length elastin need to be addressed before the case is fully established. The manuscript is well-written and the thermodynamic decomposition framework is a valuable contribution to the field.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of elastin biophysics, protein thermodynamics, intrinsically disordered proteins, and biomaterial design would be interested. The work provides a molecular-level resolution of the long-standing debate between rubber-elasticity and hydrophobic-hydration models of elastin, which has implications for understanding age-related elastin degradation and for designing elastin-mimetic biomaterials. The methodological framework for decomposing entropy into conformational and hydration contributions is also broadly applicable to other disordered proteins and hydrogels.
- **Major strengths**
  1. The study directly addresses a 50-year-old controversy in elastin biophysics with a state-of-the-art computational approach.
  2. The separation of protein conformational entropy and hydration-water entropy using established methods (dihedral-angle distributions and 2PT) is methodologically rigorous.
  3. The use of multi-microsecond simulations represents a substantial improvement in sampling over previous computational studies of elastin.
  4. The thermodynamic decomposition, including both entropic and enthalpic contributions, provides a more complete picture than entropy-only analyses.
  5. The identification of sequence- and structure-dependent heterogeneity in the entropic response is a novel and informative finding.
- **Major Concerns**

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the fully extended state serves as an appropriate reference state for isolating the thermodynamic consequences of chain extension, and that the free and fully extended states represent the endpoints needed to compute entropy and enthalpy changes.
- **Evidence pointer** Results and discussion, "Conformational entropy" section; Methods, "Molecular dynamics simulations of mini-tropoelastin" section; Figure 2b.
- **Concern** The fully extended state is generated by restraining only the terminal C-alpha atoms and stretching the chain to its contour length. This is an artificial construct that may not represent a physically relevant state. The authors acknowledge this is an "upper-bound molecular reference state," but the thermodynamic quantities derived from this state are used to draw conclusions about the mechanism of elastin elasticity. The choice of the fully extended state as an endpoint is critical because the entropy and enthalpy changes are computed as differences between the two states. If the fully extended state is not representative of the actual stretched state of elastin in tissue (where chains are cross-linked and not fully extended), the quantitative conclusions may not be directly applicable to the physiological mechanism. The authors should justify this choice more thoroughly and discuss how the results would change if a partially extended state were used instead.
- **Why it matters** The central conclusion that hydration water dominates the entropic penalty depends on the difference between the free and fully extended states. If the reference state is not physically meaningful, the quantitative decomposition of entropy and enthalpy changes is called into question, and the conclusions about the mechanism of elastin elasticity may not hold under physiological conditions.
- **Resolution test** The authors should provide a sensitivity analysis using partially extended states (e.g., 50%, 75% of full extension) to demonstrate that the qualitative conclusion (hydration water dominance) is robust to the choice of reference state. Alternatively, they should provide evidence that the fully extended state is representative of the stretched state of elastin in tissue, such as comparison with experimental force-extension curves or small-angle X-ray scattering data.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The authors report a protein-wide hydration entropy penalty of approximately 2024 kJ/mol (1583 kJ/mol translational + 441 kJ/mol rotational) and a conformational entropy penalty of 0.36 kJ/mol-res, concluding that hydration water is the dominant entropic contributor.
- **Evidence pointer** Results and discussion, "Hydration entropy" section; Figure 5a; Figure 7a.
- **Concern** The hydration entropy calculation relies on the 2PT method applied to 20-ps simulations with protein atoms position-restrained. The authors state that 2PT entropies converge within ~20 ps, but this is a very short timescale for capturing the full thermodynamic response of water to protein stretching. The position restraints on all protein atoms freeze the protein conformation, which may not allow the water structure to fully equilibrate to the stretched state. Additionally, the choice of the first hydration layer (FHL) cutoff at 0.41 nm is a critical parameter, and while the authors state the results are robust to variations (0.37-0.49 nm), the actual data for this sensitivity analysis is not shown. The 2PT method itself has known limitations for highly confined water, and the authors do not discuss potential artifacts.
- **Why it matters** The quantitative claim that hydration water dominates the entropic penalty is the central finding of the paper. If the 2PT calculations are not converged or are sensitive to the FHL definition, the quantitative conclusion could be significantly altered. The use of position restraints may also bias the water entropy calculation by preventing protein-water coupled fluctuations that could contribute to the entropy.
- **Resolution test** The authors should provide convergence tests for the 2PT calculations (e.g., 50 ps, 100 ps simulations) and show the sensitivity of the results to the FHL cutoff in a supplementary figure. They should also discuss the potential artifacts of position restraints on the water entropy calculation and, if possible, perform a test with harmonically restrained (rather than fixed) protein atoms to allow some protein flexibility.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Scientific importance
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that "hydration water provides the dominant unfavorable entropic contribution" to elastin stretching, and that this supports a "hydration-coupled thermodynamic mechanism" for elastin elasticity.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Results and discussion, "Thermodynamic summary" section; Figure 7.
- **Concern** The authors compare their results to experimental studies that report system-level entropy changes, but the connection between the simulated mini-tropoelastin and the macroscopic elastin fiber is not fully established. The mini-tropoelastin is a monomeric fragment, whereas elastin in tissue is a highly cross-linked polymer network. The authors acknowledge this limitation but do not discuss how cross-linking might alter the relative contributions of conformational and hydration entropy. Cross-linking could restrict conformational entropy more than hydration entropy, potentially changing the balance. Additionally, the fully extended state of a single chain may not be representative of the deformation of a cross-linked network, where chains are stretched to varying degrees.
- **Why it matters** The claim that hydration water is the dominant entropic contributor is presented as a general mechanism for elastin elasticity. If this conclusion is specific to the monomeric, non-cross-linked system studied here, its relevance to the physiological function of elastin is limited. The authors should more carefully qualify their conclusions and discuss how cross-linking might affect the thermodynamic balance.
- **Resolution test** The authors should add a discussion section on the implications of cross-linking for their conclusions. They could also perform simulations of a cross-linked mini-tropoelastin dimer or trimer to test whether the relative contributions of conformational and hydration entropy change upon cross-linking.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The authors report that the fully extended state has a more homogeneous water environment, with narrower distributions of molar entropies, and that this is due to the loss of secondary structure and the uniform exposure of the protein surface.
- **Evidence pointer** Results and discussion, "Hydration entropy" section; Figure 5a insets; Figure 6.
- **Concern** The interpretation of the water dynamics data (C2(t) and MSD) is somewhat superficial. The authors report reorientation times and diffusion coefficients but do not provide a detailed analysis of the spatial heterogeneity of these dynamical properties. The claim that the extended state has a "more homogeneous" water environment is based on the width of the entropy distributions, but this could be an artifact of the FHL definition or the limited sampling in the extended state. A more detailed spatial analysis (e.g., water dynamics near specific residue types or secondary structure elements) would strengthen the interpretation.
- **Why it matters** The structural and dynamical analysis of water is presented as supporting evidence for the hydration entropy calculations. If the interpretation of the water dynamics is not rigorous, the supporting evidence is weakened, and the reader may question the robustness of the main conclusions.
- **Resolution test** The authors should provide a more detailed spatial analysis of water dynamics, such as residue-specific or domain-specific water entropy maps, to support the claim of increased homogeneity in the extended state. They should also discuss the potential limitations of the static indexing approach for the C2(t) and MSD calculations.

- **Concern ID** R1-m2
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Affected element** Methods, "Conformational entropy of protein" section
- **Evidence pointer** Methods section, equation 4
- **Issue** The description of the conformational entropy calculation is brief. The authors state they use the method of ref. 28 but do not provide sufficient detail for a reader to reproduce the calculation without consulting the original reference. In particular, the treatment of side-chain rotamers and the handling of residues with multiple chi angles could be clarified.
- **Required correction** Expand the Methods section to include more detail on the entropy calculation, such as the exact binning scheme for side-chain chi angles, the treatment of terminal residues, and the number of frames used for convergence.

- **Concern ID** R1-m4
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Affected element** Methods, "Error analysis and statistical testing" section
- **Evidence pointer** Methods section
- **Issue** The statistical analysis is limited to t-tests for selected comparisons. Given the complexity of the simulation data and the multiple comparisons made across domains and residue types, a more comprehensive statistical framework (e.g., correction for multiple comparisons) would be appropriate.
- **Required correction** The authors should either apply a multiple-comparison correction to their t-tests or justify why this is not necessary. They should also report effect sizes or confidence intervals for the key comparisons.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that hydration water is the "dominant" entropic contributor is supported by the reported numbers, but the sensitivity of this conclusion to the choice of reference state (fully extended) and to the 2PT parameters is not fully established. This is a risk if the fully extended state is not representative of physiological stretching.
- The claim that the findings support a "hydration-coupled thermodynamic mechanism" for elastin elasticity is an interpretation that goes beyond the direct simulation results. The simulations show correlations between hydration entropy and surface exposure, but do not establish causality.
- The generalizability of the findings from mini-tropoelastin to full-length, cross-linked elastin is not demonstrated and is acknowledged as a limitation. The claim that the mechanism is relevant to elastin's physiological function is therefore not fully supported.
- The comparison to experimental NMR data (ref. 13) is qualitative and relies on the assumption that the simulated system captures the same physics as the experimental system. The authors should be more careful in drawing direct comparisons between simulation and experiment.
- The statement that "no study has provided an atomistic, direct, and quantitative comparison" of the relevant thermodynamic quantities is a strong claim that is difficult to verify without a comprehensive literature search. The authors should soften this claim or provide more evidence that such a comparison has not been made.
