Artificial intelligence-based protein structure prediction provides new opportunities for structure-based drug discovery, especially for targets lacking experimental structures. Determining whether AlphaFold2- and AlphaFold3-derived models can reliably support structure-based virtual screening remains a critical issue. In this study, we systematically evaluated the applicability of AlphaFold2- and AlphaFold3-derived models for structure-based virtual screening and assessed the performance of different VS pipeline configurations that integrate AlphaFold models with diverse docking and scoring combinations. Based on our benchmarking results, we established a hierarchical VS methodology and applied this approach to CLIP1-LTK, an oncogenic fusion protein lacking an experimentally resolved structure. Using this pipeline, a library of approximately three million compounds was virtually screened, leading to the identification of potential inhibitor candidates. Similarity-based analog searching identified QL07, which exhibited an IC(50) value of 524.27 nM. QL07 inhibited CLIP1-LTK downstream signaling pathways and induced apoptosis in transformed Ba/F3-CLIP1-LTK cells. Furthermore, QL07 suppressed the growth of CLIP1-LTK-positive tumors in xenograft models. These findings underscore the utility of AlphaFold models in overcoming structural limitations in drug discovery and demonstrate their feasibility in identifying promising small-molecule inhibitors against protein targets without experimentally-determined structures.
