# Phyfold: environment aware mathematical modeling for protein folding dynamics integrated with physics informed neural network


## Abstract
  Protein folding remains a fundamental problem in computational biology, where understanding folding trajectories and dynamic behavior under physiological conditions is critical for linking structure to function. Recent advances such as AlphaFold have revolutionized sequence to structure prediction, establishing a powerful and robust foundation for computational protein modeling. Building upon this success, we introduce a novel physics informed computational framework that expands static structure prediction toward dynamic and physically consistent modeling of protein folding processes. The proposed framework unifies a mathematically grounded reaction diffusion model of folding kinetics with deep sequence embeddings through a physics informed neural network (PINN), ensuring consistency with governing physical laws while remaining biologically grounded. We refer to this unified framework as PhyFold. Our results illustrate that the proposed approach effectively captures environment dependent folding dynamics and shows strong agreement with both numerical reference solutions and experimental fluorescence measurements under physiologically relevant conditions. By explicitly integrating environmental factors such as temperature, pressure, and pH, PhyFold enables realistic, physics aware simulation of protein folding dynamics. This approach offers a natural extension of modern structure prediction methods and opens new directions for protein design, structure guided drug discovery, and personalized medicine. The code to reproduce the work is available at https://github.com/jamshaidwarraich/PhyFold.git.

  Scientific contribution

  PhyFold introduces a hybrid Bio-Physics Informed Neural Network framework that models protein folding kinetics as a reaction diffusion partial differential equation with environmentally modulated kinetic coefficients, integrated with ProtT5 sequence embeddings for sequence specific spatiotemporal prediction an approach not previously demonstrated in the literature. Unlike purely data driven or static structure prediction methods, PhyFold explicitly encodes temperature, pH, and pressure dependence into governing kinetic coefficients, enabling physically interpretable simulation of cooperative folding transitions across diverse physiological conditions. Dual validation against finite difference reference solutions and experimental GFP fluorescence measurements confirms mathematical consistency and biological relevance, establishing PhyFold as a computationally efficient surrogate for environment aware protein folding dynamics.


## Introduction
  Indeed, the protein molecules, the classic molecular machines of life, mediate almost all biological phenomena such as catalytic activity, structural function, immunity, and signaling because of the unique folding abilities of these molecules to achieve a specific three-dimensional structure [1]. This complex folding mechanism converts a linear arrangement of amino acids into an accurate spatial form so that the protein may assume its functional form. The ability of the most complex of molecular structures that make up a living system to assemble with precision and fidelity is one of the signatures of a living system, which has since been observed in Nature as noted by Dobson [2]. It is important to note that even the slightest deviation in the folding dynamics [3], which can be caused by the presence of foreign substances and/or other biological agents, can have disastrous effects on the transformation, as demonstrated in the case of aggregation, misfolding, and degradation, underpinning the causes of numerous diseases, including, but not limited to, Alzheimer’s, Parkinson’s [4], and other prion diseases [5].

  Protein folding as a fundamental mechanism has been focused on under the approach of energy landscape [6] theory extensively so as to understand its mechanics as a fundamental mechanism of protein folding itself has been the subject of study under the energy landscape theory approach. Known as the new view of protein folding, this viewpoint holds that folding is not a series of compulsory steps between particular partially folded states but is rather a stochastic search of the numerous conformations available to a polypeptide chain [7]. The energy landscape is encoded in the amino acids sequence that allows proteins to evolve in a way that allows them to fold fast and efficiently in a trial and error process. This theoretical framework has transformed our views on the formation of correct folds out of underlying structural transitions.

  It has been a key problem in computational biology and biophysics [8] to understand and predict the dynamics of protein folding processes under conditions of strong dilution, which may be facilitated by the repulsive long range forces the protein must resist without collisions . Simulations A wealth of information on folding pathways and energy landscapes [9, 10] has been obtained with classical molecular dynamics (MD) [11] simulations. These atomistic methods are able to describe the fine details of folding mechanisms and some simulations have shown that local structural units such as the alpha-Helices can be formed in less than 100 nanoseconds. Nevertheless, the computational cost of these techniques is prohibitive, limiting its time and space resolution to large biomolecules and folding pathways with timescales ranging between micro and seconds long [12, 13] and respectively, which demand deep learning in science and biotechnology .

  Such constraints have triggered the development of mathematical and machine learning methods that can fill the gap between atomistic and computational efficiency. The recent advances in the area of deep learning have transformed protein structure prediction field [14]. AlphaFold2 proved to be highly accurate at predicting tertiary structures directly off amino acid sequences [15] with unprecedented accuracy, and RoseTTAFold [16] built upon these achievements with three track neural architecture. Equally, based models such as ProtT5 [17], and ESM-2 [18] generate syntactic forms of evolutionary and biophysical data using large protein sequence corpora as potent priors to downstream prediction tasks [19]. Such frameworks as DeepProtein offer detailed benchmarks of learning protein sequences that allow to systematically analyze different architectural methods of learning protein sequences [20, 21].

  Even with these impressive developments, the vast majority of data driven methods are merely correlative and are concerned with the structure of a static equilibrium of folding pathways as opposed to dynamic ones, which are the required structures in nature [22]. As stressed by Dill and MacCallum, it is not only the protein folding problem but also an understanding of how the structure actually folds, not only the prediction of structure but also the folding of a protein structure [23]. The limitation is especially important given the fact that folding is also related to a lot of biological processes such as molecular homeostasis and cell regulation. In addition, traditional machine learning methods do not explicitly incorporate physical laws that guarantee that the variables interact in biophysical plausible ways within the changing environmental conditions [24–26].

  To overcome these, we propose a new hybrid framework, namely, PhyFold, that fills the gap between data driven learning and physics based modeling that uses reaction diffusion physics [27] and deep learning embeddings [28], which combine reaction diffusion physics with environmental parameters. Adding to the classical data driven models, physics based models simulate the folding by RDEs and energy landscape theory use of the folding problem in the physical language of RDEs and energy landscapes [29, 30]. They give mesoscopic images of the kinetics of folds, where the conformational population is a diffusive process with temperature, pH, and solvent interactions acting as modulation factors, as illustrated in the literature [24, 25]. These models are simple to use because they include key features such as nucleation, cooperative folding, and spatial diffusion yet can be constrained by simple potential functions and boundary conditions.

  PINNs have become a disruptive computational model to bring together these complementary paradigms and unify them in a cohesive framework for computing physics and chemistry processes and properties through a single computational system and algorithmic approach [31, 32]. Based on this, our PhyFold framework directly incorporates partial differential equations (PDEs) into its architecture and restricts biophysical modeling to obey the known physics while also being able to learn using minimal experimental information provided by the data [33, 34]. The hybridization allows simultaneous enactment of PDE residual minimization, fulfillment of boundary conditions, and the fitting of empirical data [35] and practically turns the model into a physics consistent surrogate solver. The latest comparative assessment of PINN based techniques and classical numerical methods, including finite difference techniques, have undergone a thorough test in solving nonlinear reaction diffusion problems applicable in biological systems and have proven to have a great capacity to predict the solution to such problems [36].

  The achievements of this neural network in epidemiological simulation and modeling [37, 38] are strong incentives to implement the method in protein folding dynamics: it has been demonstrated to be successful and effective in epidemiological applications and in the analysis of molecular systems in general, including lipid molecules and their dynamics, among others, where this method can be utilized to gain an advantage. Moreover, the latest developments in the sphere of the graph neural networks to predict molecular properties, such as Enhancing Molecular Property Prediction via Hybrid Deep Learning and Domain Knowledge prove the possibility of the hybrid methods that will combine the domain knowledge with data driven learning. This paper builds on these ideas by creating a physics informed model that is particularly adapted to study spatiotemporal protein folding in the many different environmental conditions with a critical transition between physical modeling and biological verification.

  The PINN method allows the study of the dynamics of conformational change in space and time without using atomistic simulation only in the context of protein folding. The PhyFold model provides the mapping between spatial location, temporal dynamics and folding dynamics using a reaction diffusion [39] mathematical model that describes cooperative folding dynamics as well as environmental modulation. This model combines diffusion with spatial propagation and nonlinear reaction terms in its formulation and is effective in the description of the process of unfolded to folded states and includes environmental parameters like temperature, pH, and pressure in its formulation [40].

  Coupled with experimental or embedding derived datasets, PhyFold can offer an essential interface between mathematical modeling and biological validation [41] by combining this physics based framework with such data. Not only do the predictions presented by the framework provide numerically consistent predictions, but it is also possible to interpret them biologically, especially when compared to fluorescence based folding metrics benchmarked against real biology data structure measurements [42, 43]. The recent developments in AI techniques of protein interaction modeling only emphasize the usefulness of such hybrid strategies, which the paradigm that PhyFold directly illustrates is an example of multistate protein folding via AI technologies [44]. Conventional PINN models tend to be based on idealized PDEs [45] having fixed coefficients (whereas these should be more comprehensively modeled with probability distributions) and have a resemblance to coarse grating mechanisms in complex systems [46, 47]. Also, there is no empirical connection between sequence derived features and features of physical states to restrict their biological validity and generalization power, as well as their biological validity and generalization power, respectively [11].

  Moreover, prior research has seldom tried to directly validate PINN learned folding dynamics with a test of experimental fluorescence measurements or large scale sequence embeddings. This gap shows the need for hybrid approaches that combine the mathematical strength of physics based PDEs with the biological richness of deep sequence embeddings.

## Related works
  Protein folding is one of the most basic processes in the field of molecular biology, and has remained a central subject in the field of structural bioinformatics and biophysics [1]. Previously developed theoretical models explained the basic energy landscape theory which demonstrates that proteins are folded in a pathway mediated by thermodynamic [49] stability and kinetic accessibility [24] to the foldamers. These initial systems related the physicochemical [50] properties of folding with chaperone assisted models and pathological misfolding of neurodegenerative diseases [3, 51].

  Atomistic insights of folding transitions are available only through traditional computational approaches like MD simulations, which is limited by the cost of calculation and temporal scaling, as well as by scale and computational expense [17]. These limitations inspired the introduction of machine learning into protein structure prediction, dynamics models [26] and prediction of protein structure. Models such as AlphaFold have shown that deep learning models can predict residue level 3D structures of primary sequences with exquisite accuracy [15], but usually predict equilibrium conformations and not kinetic folding pathways [30].

  Later studies have investigated the dynamics of protein folding by applying ML-based generative models to protein folding [32]. Simultaneously, self supervised protein language models have become a reality resulting in biologically meaningful sequence embeddings [43]. These embeddings deliver high dimensional representations which are able to capture evolutionary and structural dependencies, which are reflected in protein sequences and structures, respectively [52].

  PINNs [53] was propsed in order to add physical consistency to data driven frameworks. The strategy offers a means of deep learning governing PDEs into neural loss functions. Later works have used this neural network to address other complicated biophysical systems [16]. This neural network can simulate kinetics of folding when perturbed by the environment under reaction diffusion dynamics and still exhibit physical interpretability [13], by constraining learning: this capability is known as folding kinetics modeling of physical processes, given that reaction diffusion dynamics can be interpreted as physical processes that can be learned by running simulations and understood through physical laws governing equilibrium driven processes.

  However, although much has been achieved, the majority lack dynamic prediction of the folding in real time, or do not incorporate the adaptability of the environment, by not considering the influence of differing physiological conditions, which has been accomplished by most current models. Contrarily, the given study will address this issue by co-training a reaction diffusion based PINN model with bioinformatics derived fluorescence measurements as a biological proxy. The hybrid methodology does not only maintain thermodynamic fidelity but also can be tailored to the different physiological conditions to provide a single methodology to model protein folding and stability under environmental state variation.


## PhyFold framework

### Proposed model

The PhyFold model becomes dynamical since the biological drive to understand the underlying structural dynamics of proteins and the transitions between states requires the model to be informed by environmental changes, i.e. temperature, pH and pressure. This method is essential because it includes the shortcomings of the static analyses, which is the dynamic folding kinetics that is experienced when subjected to real cellular conditions. Combining reaction diffusion equation (RDE) and a PINN, we create a predictive model that is validated with experimental fluorescence data and this represents a considerable improvement as it describes the ongoing changes of the folding landscape. ### Biological interpretation and implications
  The PhyFold frame can capture the dynamics of folding of a protein, which observe major biophysical principles of the formation of protein structures. The sigmoidal folding curves and the propagation of the spatial structure are representative of the cooperative folding process and are preceded by a rapid structural consolidation due to the nucleation processes. These conditions are perturbed and the kinetics of folding are delayed, the steady state folding fractions are reduced, which agrees with previous experimental measurements and indicates that there are increased energetic barriers and destabilisation of native conformations.

  The sensitivity analysis on the environmental condition also shows that physio-chemical conditions play a crucial role in determining folding behavior. Optimal temperature and pH also maximize the rates of effective folding whereas atypical alkaline stress specifically disrupts electrostatic interactions and hydrogen bonding network that provide stability to the structure. The extreme loss of predictive accuracy in high pH regimes demonstrates the complexity inherent in the folding landscapes under chemically perturbed conditions, and the necessity to consider the effects of the environment on the folding landscape models.

  PhyFold uses explicitly physical limitations to a data driven learning model, so that biologically interpretable folding predictions are being made by PhyFold beyond structure determination. This allows modeling of physiologically relevant and stress induced conditions of folding pathways more realistically, which is of use in protein engineering, structure guided drug discovery, and in the study of folding related dysfunctions.

## Conclusion
  This study presents PhyFold, a hybrid framework that unifies PINN with deep learning embeddings to model protein folding dynamics under varying environmental conditions. By coupling data-driven learning with physical constraints, PhyFold bridges the gap between empirical fluorescence data and the underlying biophysical processes governing folding kinetics. The framework explicitly incorporates environmental parameters temperature (T), pH, and pressure (P) into the RDE governing protein folding, enabling physically consistent simulation of environmental influences on folding stability. Mathematical validation against finite difference solutions confirmed close adherence to the governing physics, establishing the reliability of the PINN based solver. Optimization with the Adam optimizer ensured smooth and stable convergence while maintaining the balance between data loss and physical consistency. Using fluorescence data as a biologically grounded validation proxy, the model achieved a Pearson correlation of r = 0.822r=0.822 and MAE = 0.101=0.101 across the held out test set of 4,289 GFP variant sequences, demonstrating strong agreement with experimentally observed biological trends. These results highlight that integrating physics informed constraints into neural computation enhances both predictive accuracy and interpretability. The framework reproduces the spatiotemporal dynamics of protein folding and captures environmentally modulated folding behavior in a physically interpretable manner. The framework demonstrates its strongest predictive reliability under physiological and near physiological conditions, including mild sub optimal, thermal stress, and acidic stress environments. The one dimensional reaction coordinate is based on energy landscape theory and Kramers’ diffusion formalism and is appropriate for two state folders, like GFP. However, it cannot resolve the three dimensional structural complexity and nonlocal contacts. In order to apply the one dimensional reaction coordinate to multi domain and intrinsically disordered proteins, an important future direction is to develop higher dimensional collective variable spaces. Future extensions incorporating asymmetric pH response functions, sequence dependent diffusion coefficients, higher dimensional representations, and validation across diverse protein families are identified as important directions for advancing the framework.
