BACKGROUND: Pharmaceutical Research and Development (R&D) is characterized by high costs, lengthy timelines, and frequent clinical failure. Eroom's Law describes the historical decline in new drug approvals per unit of R&D expenditure. Artificial Intelligence-driven Drug Discovery (AIDD) offers new approaches to target identification, molecular design, and property prediction, but improved computational performance alone does not establish clinical benefit. OBJECTIVE: This narrative review examines how AIDD can facilitate transitioning from target identification to molecular design and clinical evaluation. METHODS: It discusses graph neural networks, structure prediction, and generative models alongside pharmacological and clinical evidence. Four dimensions structure the discussion: target structure and dynamics; subcellular localization and target accessibility; pharmacokinetics and binding kinetics; and clinical factors. They provide a qualitative framework for identifying evidence gaps, not a mathematical model or predictive tool for drug efficacy. A complementary target-molecule-clinical framework illustrates how experimental and clinical evidence can inform earlier stages of drug development. RESULTS: Clinical examples, including rentosertib and GTAEXS617, emphasize distinguishing design objectives, preclinical findings, and clinical observations. Rentosertib's phase IIa trial provides an early clinical signal, although its limited sample size and duration preclude conclusions regarding sustained benefit. Training-data bias, data leakage, limited interpretability, synthetic accessibility, and uncertain generalizability beyond the training domain remain important concerns. This review acknowledges selective coverage of the literature and study-design heterogeneity. CONCLUSION: Assessing whether AIDD can improve overall drug-development efficiency requires comparative evidence on development time, cost, safety, and clinical success. Experimental validation and longitudinal clinical follow-up remain essential for evaluating these outcomes.
