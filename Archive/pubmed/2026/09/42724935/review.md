## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript (protocol paper)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific and technical content as presented in the manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The manuscript presents a step-by-step protocol for using HuDiff, an autoregressive diffusion model, to humanize antibodies and nanobodies from scratch using only CDR sequences as input, without requiring preexisting human templates.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Background, Procedure (including tables of generated sequences), Data analysis (including humanness score tables), Validation of protocol, General notes and troubleshooting
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** No original research data or code is provided for independent verification; the protocol references a separate original study [3] for validation; no raw training data, model weights, or evaluation scripts are included in the manuscript; no experimental validation data (e.g., binding affinity, immunogenicity assays) is presented.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This protocol describes a computational method for antibody and nanobody humanization that is potentially useful for the therapeutic antibody community. The step-by-step instructions are detailed and appear technically sound. However, the manuscript lacks any original validation data or performance benchmarks, relying entirely on a separate publication for evidence of efficacy. As a protocol paper, this may be acceptable for a methods-focused journal, but the claims of "humanizing from scratch" and "eliminating the need for preexisting human templates" are not substantiated within this manuscript itself.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in antibody engineering, therapeutic protein development, and computational biology who need practical guidance for implementing template-free humanization. The protocol's detailed instructions for installation, data preparation, training, and inference would be valuable for practitioners seeking to apply the method.
- **Major strengths** 1. The protocol provides extremely detailed, step-by-step instructions with specific command-line examples, making it highly reproducible. 2. The two-stage training strategy (pretraining on human sequences, fine-tuning on target species) is a well-motivated approach. 3. The inclusion of both antibody (HuDiff-Ab) and nanobody (HuDiff-Nb) workflows, with the inpainting mode for nanobodies, addresses two important use cases. 4. The troubleshooting section covers common practical issues (CUDA OOM, file path errors, IMGT numbering) that users will encounter.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Scientific importance / Technical soundness
    - **Claim pointer** "HuDiff is an adaptive autoregressive diffusion approach that generates humanized antibodies and nanobodies from scratch using only complementarity-determining region sequences as input, eliminating the need for preexisting human templates."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Background, Validation of protocol
    - **Concern** The manuscript provides no original evidence that HuDiff actually works. The "Validation of protocol" section states "This protocol has been validated in the original study [3] through both in silico benchmarks and wet-lab experiments," but no data, metrics, or results from that study are presented here. The humanness scores in Tables 3 and 4 are presented without any comparison to baseline methods (e.g., CDR grafting, Sapiens, AbNatiV) or to the original non-humanized sequences. There is no demonstration that the humanized sequences retain binding affinity or structural integrity beyond AlphaFold2 predictions.
    - **Why it matters** A protocol for a method whose efficacy cannot be assessed from the manuscript itself has limited value. Readers cannot determine whether HuDiff outperforms existing approaches, whether the generated sequences are truly functional, or whether the protocol is worth implementing. The central claim of the method—template-free humanization that preserves function—remains unsubstantiated.
    - **Resolution test** Provide comparative benchmarks against at least one established humanization method (e.g., CDR grafting, Sapiens) using the same input sequences, showing humanness scores, structural metrics, and ideally experimental binding data. Alternatively, include a summary table from the original study [3] with key performance metrics.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M2
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Technical soundness
    - **Claim pointer** "HuDiff-Ab processes paired heavy and light chains for conventional antibodies, while HuDiff-Nb can incorporate a specialized inpainting mode to preserve critical nanobody framework residues."
    - **Evidence pointer** Procedure B2, B3, Tables 1 and 2
    - **Concern** The manuscript does not explain how the inpainting mode works, what residues are preserved, or how this affects the humanization process. The inpainting mode is described as "essential for preserving nanobody stability and expression" but no justification or mechanism is provided. Additionally, the selection of specific variants (2B04-hAb4, hAb7, hAb9; 3-2A2-4-hNb3, hNb10, hNb11) for structural analysis is stated without any criteria or rationale.
    - **Why it matters** The inpainting mode is a key differentiator of HuDiff-Nb and critical for nanobody humanization. Without understanding what it does or why it works, users cannot assess whether it is appropriate for their application or how to interpret results. The arbitrary selection of variants undermines confidence in the analysis.
    - **Resolution test** Provide a clear description of the inpainting mechanism, including which framework residues are preserved and why. Specify the selection criteria for choosing representative variants (e.g., based on humanness scores, diversity, structural metrics).

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
    - **Originality**: The method itself (autoregressive diffusion for antibody humanization) may be novel, but this protocol does not demonstrate originality beyond the referenced study [3]. The protocol format is standard.
    - **Scientific importance**: Antibody humanization is an important problem, but the manuscript does not provide evidence that HuDiff advances the state of the art. Without comparative benchmarks, the importance cannot be assessed.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The protocol is targeted at computational biologists and antibody engineers. It is too specialized for a broad interdisciplinary audience.
    - **Technical soundness**: The protocol steps appear technically sound, but the lack of validation data and unclear inpainting mechanism raise concerns. The reliance on a separate publication for validation is a significant weakness.
    - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The protocol is well-written with clear step-by-step instructions, but the Background and Data analysis sections assume familiarity with antibody humanization concepts and metrics.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The protocol may be suitable for a methods journal with appropriate revisions, but it does not meet the standards for a Nature journal due to the lack of original validation data and comparative benchmarks. The authors should either include key results from the original study [3] or reframe the manuscript as a pure protocol without claims of efficacy.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- "HuDiff generates humanized antibodies and nanobodies from scratch using only CDR sequences as input, eliminating the need for preexisting human templates" – Unsupported within this manuscript; no evidence provided.
- "All variants achieved H T20 > 76, confirming that HuDiff-Ab produces humanized antibodies with humanness levels comparable to experimentally validated antibodies" – Unsupported; no comparison to experimentally validated antibodies or original sequences.
- "The selected variants maintain high humanness scores and structural integrity" – Unsupported; selection criteria not provided, and structural integrity is only assessed by AlphaFold2 predictions, not experimental validation.
- "This protocol has been validated in the original study [3] through both in silico benchmarks and wet-lab experiments" – Not assessable from the provided material.
