- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report a protein-supported single-atom catalyst platform in which structurally invariant Pt(Glu)2 units are covalently anchored to protein scaffolds. They claim that protein conformational transitions modulate the second-shell microenvironment without altering the metal coordination environment, producing reversible activity modulation of approximately 65% solely through conformational cycling. Kinetic analysis attributes activity changes to substrate affinity (Km) rather than intrinsic turnover, supported by similar activation energies from Arrhenius analysis. Fluorescence tracking and molecular dynamics simulations are cited as evidence for enhanced substrate accessibility and spatial preorganization in the folded state.

The central claim, that conformational cycling modulates catalytic activity by approximately 65% without perturbing the metal coordination environment, is potentially significant for the field of artificial metalloenzyme design. However, the evidence base available in the abstract is insufficient to verify the structural invariance claim, the reversibility of modulation, or the kinetic interpretation. The work may be of interest to researchers in single-atom catalysis, metalloenzyme engineering, and biocatalysis, but the current abstract does not provide enough detail to assess technical soundness or establish the case.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** "Structurally invariant Pt(Glu)2 units were covalently anchored onto protein scaffolds, while protein conformational transitions selectively modulated the surrounding spatial microenvironment without perturbing the metal active site."
- **Evidence pointer** Location not provided
- **Concern** The claim of structural invariance of the Pt(Glu)2 unit and the absence of perturbation to the metal coordination environment is central to the entire decoupling strategy. However, the abstract provides no spectroscopic or structural data, such as X-ray absorption spectroscopy, EXAFS, XANES, or XPS, to support this claim. Without such evidence, it is not possible to verify that the metal center remains unchanged during conformational cycling.
- **Why it matters** If the metal coordination environment is altered during conformational transitions, the observed activity changes could be attributed to first-shell effects rather than second-shell contributions, which would invalidate the core conclusion of orthogonal decoupling.
- **Resolution test** Provide spectroscopic characterization of the Pt center in both folded and unfolded states, including EXAFS fitting parameters, coordination numbers, bond distances, and any relevant spectra, demonstrating no statistically significant differences.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** "Reversible catalytic modulation spanning approximately 65% activity range was achieved solely through conformational cycling, without any detectable change to the metal coordination environment."
- **Evidence pointer** Location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract claims reversibility of catalytic modulation over multiple conformational cycles, but no data on cycling experiments, number of cycles, reproducibility, or potential hysteresis are presented. The phrase "without any detectable change" requires a defined detection limit and appropriate control experiments.
- **Why it matters** Reversibility is essential to demonstrate that the modulation is due to conformational switching rather than irreversible structural changes or catalyst degradation. Without cycling data, the claim of reversible modulation is unsubstantiated.
- **Resolution test** Provide data from multiple conformational cycling experiments showing consistent activity values across cycles, with error bars and statistical analysis, along with characterization confirming no structural or compositional changes after cycling.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** "Kinetic analysis revealed that activity changes were dominated by variations in substrate affinity (Km) rather than intrinsic turnover, as confirmed by nearly identical activation energies between folded and unfolded states from Arrhenius analysis."
- **Evidence pointer** Location not provided
- **Concern** The kinetic interpretation relies on Km and activation energy comparisons, but the abstract provides no kinetic parameters, fitting procedures, substrate concentration ranges, or statistical comparisons. The claim of "nearly identical" activation energies requires quantitative values and error estimates to be meaningful. Additionally, Km changes alone do not necessarily imply changes in substrate affinity without considering enzyme concentration and other factors.
- **Why it matters** The mechanistic conclusion that second-shell effects operate through substrate affinity rather than turnover is a key finding. If the kinetic analysis is flawed or underpowered, the mechanistic interpretation would be compromised.
- **Resolution test** Provide full kinetic data, including Michaelis-Menten plots, fitted Km and kcat values with standard errors for both folded and unfolded states, Arrhenius plots with activation energies and confidence intervals, and a statistical test demonstrating equivalence of activation energies.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** "Fluorescence tracking and molecular dynamics simulations further supported that protein folding enhances substrate accessibility and spatial preorganization around the catalytic center."
- **Evidence pointer** Location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract cites fluorescence tracking and molecular dynamics simulations as supporting evidence, but provides no details on the fluorescence probes, labeling sites, simulation force fields, system setup, simulation timescales, or analysis methods. Without these details, the reliability and relevance of the computational and fluorescence data cannot be evaluated.
- **Why it matters** The molecular dynamics simulations are used to support the proposed mechanism of enhanced substrate accessibility and preorganization. If the simulations are not properly validated or the fluorescence data are not directly linked to substrate accessibility, the mechanistic interpretation would be weakened.
- **Resolution test** Provide details of fluorescence labeling and control experiments, simulation parameters, convergence criteria, and analysis of substrate access channels or binding poses in folded versus unfolded states, with appropriate validation against experimental data.
