## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, main body sections, concluding remarks, author contributions, conflict of interest, generative AI statement, publisher's note, supplementary material description). No figures, tables, or supplementary files were provided for review.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, structural logic, evidence quality, and internal consistency of the manuscript as submitted. Claims are assessed against the evidence presented within the text itself. No external literature verification was performed.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The manuscript is a review article that synthesizes structural biology knowledge of DEAD-box RNA helicases with multi-omics data from cold-stressed rubber tree (Hevea brasiliensis). It identifies nine differentially abundant DDX proteins under cold stress, highlights DDX39B as the most responsive, and uses AlphaFold2 predictions to argue for strong structural and evolutionary conservation of HbDDX39B. The authors propose that cold-induced downregulation of DDX39B may underlie cold sensitivity in tropical plants and suggest translational applications for crop improvement.
- **Visible evidence base** Text descriptions of Table 1 (structural data from PDB Europe), Table 2 (nine differentially abundant DDX proteins), Figure 1 (classification and domain architecture), Figures 2-5 (structural details of DDX3X, DDX21, DHX36, RIG-I-like receptors), Figure 6 (AlphaFold2 comparison of HbDDX39B with human ortholog), Figure 7 (conceptual framework), Supplementary Figures S1-S3, Supplementary Table S1. None of these figures, tables, or supplementary materials were actually provided to the reviewer.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures, tables, and supplementary files are referenced but not included. The multi-omics datasets (proteomics, transcriptomics) are cited via prior publications (Mao et al., 2022; Gao et al., 2025) but not described in sufficient detail to evaluate experimental design, statistical thresholds, or replication. The Ka/Ks analysis methodology is described only briefly. The AlphaFold2 prediction parameters, confidence metrics (pLDDT, PAE), and validation steps are not reported.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a potentially useful integrative review that connects structural biology of DEAD-box helicases with cold stress responses in a non-model crop species. The central observation, that DDX39B is strongly downregulated under cold stress in rubber tree and is highly conserved across tropical species, is interesting and could have translational relevance. However, the manuscript suffers from a fundamental mismatch between its stated scope and its actual content. The title and abstract promise "structural and functional perspectives," but the structural sections are largely a summary of previously published work on human and yeast systems, with no new structural data presented. The rubber tree-specific content is limited to a single AlphaFold2 prediction and a Ka/Ks analysis, both of which are described with insufficient methodological detail. The interpretive leap from sequence conservation to a mechanistic explanation of tropical plant cold sensitivity is not adequately supported. The manuscript reads more like a perspective or hypothesis paper than a comprehensive review, and the evidence base for its central claims is thin.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Plant stress biologists studying cold tolerance mechanisms in tropical crops would find the identification of DDX39B as a candidate cold-responsive factor of interest. Researchers working on RNA metabolism in plants, particularly those studying splicing and mRNA export under abiotic stress, may find the conceptual framework useful. Structural biologists studying DEAD-box helicases might be interested in the comparative analysis, though they would find little new information. Agricultural biotechnologists focused on rubber tree improvement or engineering cold tolerance in tropical crops could see translational potential. The broader readership of a plant science journal would find the topic relevant, but the depth of analysis may not satisfy specialists in any single discipline.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript addresses a genuinely understudied area, namely DEAD-box helicase function in tropical crop stress responses. The integration of structural biology concepts with plant stress physiology is a worthwhile framing. The identification of DDX39B as a consistently cold-responsive helicase across multiple comparison groups is a concrete, useful observation. The Ka/Ks analysis, while briefly described, provides quantitative evidence for purifying selection. The authors are appropriately cautious in noting that their structural predictions require experimental validation. The writing is generally clear and accessible.

  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that "nine differentially abundant DDX family proteins were identified" in cold-stressed rubber tree and that "DDX39B shows the most pronounced response."
  - **Evidence pointer** Table 2, Section 4.1, location not provided
  - **Concern** The multi-omics data underpinning the central claim of this review are not presented in sufficient detail. The manuscript refers to "a previous quantitative analysis" (Mao et al., 2022; Gao et al., 2025) but does not describe the experimental design, the cold stress treatment conditions, the time points sampled, the tissue types analyzed, the proteomics methodology, the statistical thresholds for differential abundance, or the number of biological replicates. Without this information, the reader cannot assess the robustness of the identification of these nine proteins or the claim that DDX39B is the most pronounced responder. The table itself is not provided.
  - **Why it matters** The entire rubber tree-specific contribution of this review rests on these multi-omics data. If the underlying data are weak, underpowered, or derived from a single condition, the interpretive framework built on them collapses. A review article should either present the primary data with full methodological transparency or clearly direct readers to the original studies with a summary of key parameters.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a detailed description of the experimental design, including cold stress conditions, time points, tissues, replication, statistical thresholds, and the magnitude of changes for each of the nine proteins. Include the full Table 2 with quantitative values and statistical measures. If the data are from published studies, summarize the key methodological parameters in the text or a supplementary table.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Claim-evidence alignment
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that "AlphaFold2 predictions reveal near-identical core helicase folds" between rubber tree and human DDX39B, and that the RMSD across the core helicase domain is "only 0.299 Å."
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 4.3, Figure 6, location not provided
  - **Concern** The AlphaFold2 analysis is presented as a central piece of evidence, but the methodological details are almost entirely absent. The manuscript does not state which AlphaFold2 version was used, whether the predictions were run locally or obtained from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database, what confidence metrics were obtained (pLDDT, PAE), how the structures were aligned, which residues were included in the RMSD calculation, or whether the comparison was performed on full-length proteins or isolated domains. The claim of "near-identical" folds based on a single RMSD value of 0.299 Å is difficult to evaluate without knowing the alignment procedure and the structural context. Furthermore, the comparison to the crystal structure of human DDX39B (PDB: 1XTI) is mentioned but not shown quantitatively.
  - **Why it matters** The structural comparison is used to argue for strong functional constraint and to support the translational relevance of the findings. If the RMSD calculation is misleading, for example by excluding flexible regions or comparing only a small conserved core, the conclusion of "near-identical" folds would be overstated. The manuscript itself acknowledges that these are computational predictions, but the presentation still implies a level of precision that the described methods cannot support.
  - **Resolution test** Report the AlphaFold2 version, the source of the models, the pLDDT and PAE scores for the regions compared, the exact residues included in the RMSD calculation, and the alignment method. Provide a figure showing the structural superposition with clearly labeled domains and confidence annotations. If possible, include a quantitative comparison of the rubber tree model to the human crystal structure using multiple metrics (e.g., TM-score, GDT-TS).
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Logical inference
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript proposes that "the cold sensitivity of tropical plants may be partially attributable to the inherent vulnerability of their RNA metabolic machinery to low temperatures" and that "reduced DDX39B abundance could lead to impaired splicing and nuclear export of cold-responsive transcripts."
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 4.3, Section 5, location not provided
  - **Concern** The causal chain from DDX39B downregulation to cold sensitivity is speculative and presented without adequate consideration of alternative explanations. The observation that DDX39B is downregulated under cold stress does not establish that this downregulation causes cold sensitivity. It could be a protective response, a downstream consequence of cellular damage, or unrelated to the cold sensitivity phenotype. The manuscript acknowledges this distinction in the concluding remarks but then proceeds to build a translational framework on the assumption that DDX39B modulation could enhance cold tolerance. The comparison to temperate plants with CBF/DREB-mediated cold acclimation is presented as if it were established that DDX39B is a limiting factor in tropical plants, which is not supported by the data shown.
  - **Why it matters** The translational claims in Section 5.4, including the suggestion that DDX39B could be a target for marker-assisted selection or transgenic approaches, depend on the causal role of DDX39B in cold sensitivity. If the downregulation is a consequence rather than a cause, these applications would not be justified. The manuscript should clearly distinguish between hypothesis and established fact.
  - **Resolution test** Reframe the discussion to explicitly label the causal model as a hypothesis requiring experimental testing. Describe the specific experiments needed to establish causality, such as overexpression or knockdown of HbDDX39B in rubber tree or a model system, followed by cold stress phenotyping. Alternatively, present additional evidence from the multi-omics data that supports a causal role, such as correlation with known cold tolerance regulators or enrichment of DDX39B targets among cold-responsive genes.

For each helicase discussed, state what specific insight it provides for understanding HbDDX39B function or cold stress responses. Alternatively, condense Sections 3.1-3.4 into a single section that focuses on the structural principles most relevant to the rubber tree analysis.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M5
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Novelty and contribution
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims to provide "a conceptual framework for dissecting DDX helicase roles in plant abiotic stress tolerance" and a "generalizable strategy for mechanistic investigations in non-model plant species."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Section 5, Section 5.5, location not provided

Combining multi-omics data with AlphaFold2 structure prediction is now a standard approach in plant biology and is not inherently novel. The manuscript does not demonstrate that this integration has produced insights that could not have been obtained by simpler analyses. The "conceptual framework" presented in Figure 7 is described only in text and appears to be a general schematic rather than a specific, testable model. The authors do not articulate what is genuinely new about their approach or what specific hypotheses it generates that would not arise from standard sequence analysis alone.
  - **Why it matters** For a review article to be valuable, it must either synthesize existing knowledge in a way that generates new insights or identify specific gaps and opportunities for future research. The current manuscript does neither in a compelling way. The reader is left with a general sense that DEAD-box helicases are important in cold stress, but no clear agenda for future research.
  - **Resolution test** Articulate specific, testable hypotheses that arise from the integration of structural and multi-omics data. For example, predict which specific residues in HbDDX39B might be responsible for cold sensitivity based on comparison with cold-tolerant orthologs, or identify specific RNA targets whose splicing or export might be affected by DDX39B downregulation. Describe how the proposed framework differs from existing approaches in the literature.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that nine differentially abundant DDX proteins were identified in cold-stressed rubber tree is not supported by the provided text, as the underlying data and methodology are not described.
- The claim that DDX39B shows the "most pronounced response" to cold stress is not supported by quantitative data.
- The claim that rubber tree DDX39B shares ">95% sequence identity" with orthologs from other tropical species and "~73% identity" with human DDX39B is presented without showing the alignment or specifying the sequences compared.
- The claim that AlphaFold2 predictions reveal "near-identical core helicase folds" with an RMSD of 0.299 Å is not supported by methodological detail or a figure.
- The claim that "the cold sensitivity of tropical plants may be partially attributable to the inherent vulnerability of their RNA metabolic machinery to low temperatures" is a hypothesis presented without direct evidence.
- The claim that DDX39B downregulation "could lead to impaired splicing and nuclear export of cold-responsive transcripts" is speculative and not supported by functional data.
- The claim that the predicted LysM and protein kinase domains in isoform A0A6A6KEA8 represent "a potentially novel domain architecture not previously reported" is not verifiable from the provided text.
- The translational claims regarding marker-assisted selection or transgenic approaches for cold tolerance are not supported by the evidence presented.
- The statement that "recent multi-omics investigations have identified multiple DDX family proteins that are differentially abundant under cold stress" is not verifiable without access to the cited primary studies.
