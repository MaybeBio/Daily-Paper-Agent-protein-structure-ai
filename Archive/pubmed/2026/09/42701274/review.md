## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no access to full manuscript, figures, tables, or supplementary information
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that lactone ring-opening in macrocyclic peptides can be used to generate distinct atropisomeric conformations. Under kinetic control, the process is highly atroposelective; under thermodynamic control, reversible ring-opening equilibrates atropisomers. NMR and molecular dynamics studies show that the resulting atropisomers adopt markedly different conformations, from a 310-helix to noncanonical structures. In an RGD-containing macrocycle, the kinetic atropisomer reproduces the integrin-binding geometry, while the thermodynamic atropisomer adopts a previously undescribed conformation. The authors conclude that this constitutes a new strategy for controlling macrocyclic peptide conformation.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental procedures, characterization data, NMR spectra, MD simulation parameters, or structural coordinates provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, experimental methods, compound characterization, NMR assignment details, MD simulation methodology and validation, crystallographic or structural data, biological assay data for integrin binding, and any control experiments

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a conceptually interesting approach to conformational control of macrocyclic peptides via lactone ring-opening, with a clear distinction between kinetic and thermodynamic atropisomer outcomes. The claim of achieving atroposelective ring-opening and the observation of divergent conformations, including a 310-helix, are potentially significant. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient evidence to evaluate the robustness of the synthetic methodology, the rigor of the conformational assignments, or the biological relevance of the RGD-containing system. The central claims are plausible but not verifiable from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in peptide chemistry, macrocycle synthesis, conformational analysis, and drug discovery would be interested. The work addresses a long-standing challenge in controlling the 3D structure of macrocyclic peptides, which is relevant to the design of peptide-based therapeutics targeting protein-protein interactions. The atroposelective ring-opening strategy could offer a practical route to conformationally defined macrocycles, and the RGD-integrin example provides a direct application context.
- **Major strengths** The conceptual distinction between kinetic and thermodynamic control of atropisomer formation is clear and potentially generalizable. The use of lactone ring-opening as a late-stage modification is an elegant approach that avoids early-stage conformational bias. The inclusion of an RGD-containing macrocycle with a known biological target (integrin) strengthens the translational relevance. The combination of NMR and MD to characterize conformations is appropriate for the question posed.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that lactone ring-opening is "highly atroposelective across macrocyclic precursors tested" under kinetic control.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; no experimental data provided
  - **Concern** The abstract states that atroposelectivity is high across multiple precursors, but no quantitative data (e.g., diastereomeric ratios, yields, substrate scope) are provided. Without these, the generality of the selectivity claim cannot be assessed.
  - **Why it matters** Atroposelectivity is the central enabling feature of the kinetic control strategy. If selectivity is substrate-dependent or modest, the utility of the method is substantially diminished.
  - **Resolution test** Provide diastereomeric ratios and yields for all tested macrocyclic precursors, including any failed or low-selectivity cases, in the full manuscript.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that NMR and molecular dynamics studies reveal "markedly different conformations, ranging from a 310-helix to various noncanonical conformations."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; no NMR spectra, NOE assignments, or MD simulation details provided
  - **Concern** The conformational assignments are central to the paper's significance, yet no evidence is presented to support the identification of a 310-helix or the noncanonical conformations. NMR-based conformational analysis requires careful assignment of NOEs, chemical shift deviations, and coupling constants, and MD requires validation of force field parameters and sampling convergence.
  - **Why it matters** If the conformational assignments are incorrect or overinterpreted, the central claim of "differentiated conformations" collapses. The 310-helix assignment in particular is often ambiguous without high-resolution structural data.
  - **Resolution test** Provide full NMR assignment tables, key NOE correlations, and MD simulation details (force field, water model, simulation length, convergence metrics) in the full manuscript. If available, include X-ray or cryo-EM structures for at least one pair of atropisomers.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that the kinetic atropisomer of the RGD-containing macrocycle "recapitulates the 3D geometry involved in integrin binding."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; no biological or structural data provided
  - **Concern** The abstract implies a functional or structural match with integrin binding, but no binding assay data, co-crystal structure, or comparison with known integrin-bound RGD conformations is presented. The phrase "recapitulates" is ambiguous and could mean either a computational overlay or an experimental binding measurement.
  - **Why it matters** The RGD example is the key translational demonstration. Without direct evidence of integrin binding or a structural comparison, the biological relevance of the kinetic atropisomer is unsubstantiated.
  - **Resolution test** Provide integrin binding affinity data (e.g., IC50 or Kd) for both atropisomers and a structural overlay with a known integrin-bound RGD conformation, or a co-crystal structure.

- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The concept of using lactone ring-opening for atroposelective conformational control of macrocyclic peptides appears novel and distinct from existing strategies such as N-methylation or backbone rigidification. This is a potential strength.
  - Scientific importance: If fully validated, the work would address a significant challenge in macrocyclic peptide drug design. However, the importance is contingent on the generality of the selectivity and the biological relevance of the RGD example, neither of which is established from the abstract.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges synthetic organic chemistry, conformational analysis, and peptide therapeutics, which would appeal to a broad readership. The abstract is written in a way that is accessible to nonspecialists.
  - Technical soundness: Cannot be assessed from the abstract. The NMR and MD methods are appropriate in principle, but the lack of detail prevents evaluation of their rigor.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is clear and concise, with appropriate use of terminology. The key concepts are explained sufficiently for a general scientific audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The conceptual framework is promising, and the work could be suitable for a high-impact venue if the full manuscript provides quantitative atroposelectivity data, rigorous conformational evidence, and direct biological validation. I would be supportive if these technical concerns are resolved in the full manuscript.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of "highly atroposelective" ring-opening is unsupported without quantitative selectivity data.
- The identification of a 310-helix and "various noncanonical conformations" is unsupported without NMR or structural evidence.
- The claim that the kinetic atropisomer "recapitulates the 3D geometry involved in integrin binding" is unsupported without binding data or structural comparison.
- The statement that the thermodynamic atropisomer adopts a "distinct conformation that was not previously described" is unverifiable without structural data or a literature comparison.
- The generalizability of the strategy "across macrocyclic precursors tested" is unverifiable without a defined substrate scope.
