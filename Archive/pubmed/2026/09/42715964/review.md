## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that engineering the hydrophobic microenvironment of the active site in ZEN lactonases enhances proton transfer and hydrolytic activity, based on structural analysis, QM calculations, and MD simulations. They demonstrate this in ZENM and transfer the strategy to ZHD101.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or data are provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, including all figures, tables, experimental methods, computational details, raw data, and statistical analyses. The abstract alone is insufficient to evaluate the robustness of the claims.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents an interesting and potentially impactful strategy for enzyme engineering. However, the evidence base is too limited to assess the validity of the core claims. The mechanistic link between hydrophobic microenvironment engineering, proton transfer, and activity enhancement is asserted but not demonstrated with quantitative data. The transferability to ZHD101 is mentioned but lacks detail. The work is at a preliminary stage of evaluation from the abstract alone.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in enzyme engineering, food safety, and mycotoxin detoxification would be interested. The proposed strategy of hydrophobic microenvironment engineering for enhancing proton transfer could be a generalizable approach for improving lactonase activity, which is relevant for industrial applications in food and feed processing.
- **Major strengths** The combination of structural analysis, QM calculations, and MD simulations to guide enzyme engineering is a rigorous approach. The identification of a near-attack conformation of His245 as a key catalytic step provides a mechanistic target. The demonstration of transferability to another lactonase (ZHD101) suggests potential general applicability.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
    - **Claim pointer** "Engineering the hydrophobic microenvironment significantly enhanced the activity of ZENM toward multiple substrates."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The abstract does not provide any quantitative data (e.g., kcat, KM, kcat/KM, fold-improvement) to support the claim of "significantly enhanced activity." Without numerical values and statistical comparisons to the wild-type enzyme, the magnitude and significance of the improvement cannot be assessed.
    - **Why it matters** The central claim of the paper rests on demonstrating that the engineered mutations lead to a meaningful increase in catalytic activity. The absence of quantitative data makes this claim unverifiable from the abstract.
    - **Resolution test** Provide specific kinetic parameters (e.g., kcat, KM, kcat/KM) for wild-type and mutant ZENM against multiple ZEN substrates, with appropriate error bars and statistical tests (e.g., t-tests or ANOVA).
    - **Concern ID** R1-M2
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Mechanistic validation
    - **Claim pointer** "QM and MD analyses identified a near-attack conformation of the catalytic His245 as essential for proton transfer."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The abstract states that a near-attack conformation (NAC) of His245 is essential, but it does not describe how this was identified, what the energetic or geometric criteria for the NAC were, or how the mutations specifically optimize this conformation. The link between the hydrophobic microenvironment and the NAC is asserted but not explained.
    - **Why it matters** The mechanistic claim is the foundation for the engineering strategy. Without a clear description of the NAC and how it is modulated by the mutations, the rationale for the engineering is weak, and the claim of "enhanced proton transfer" remains unsupported.
    - **Resolution test** Provide QM-calculated energy profiles for proton transfer in wild-type and mutant enzymes, showing the NAC geometry and its population from MD simulations. Show that the mutations increase the population or stability of the NAC.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M3
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Generalizability
    - **Claim pointer** "Transfer of the engineered region to another ZEN lactonase, ZHD101, also significantly improved the hydrolytic activity, supporting the potential general applicability of this strategy."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The abstract does not specify which residues were transferred, how the transfer was performed (e.g., site-directed mutagenesis, chimeric construction), or the magnitude of the activity improvement in ZHD101. Without these details, the claim of general applicability is premature.
    - **Why it matters** The broader impact of the study hinges on the transferability of the strategy. The current description is too vague to evaluate whether the improvement in ZHD101 is comparable to that in ZENM or is an artifact of a specific mutation.
    - **Resolution test** Provide sequence alignment showing the transferred region, kinetic data for wild-type and engineered ZHD101, and a discussion of sequence conservation to explain why the strategy works in a different enzyme.
- **Minor Comments**
    - **Concern ID** R1-m1
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Clarity
    - **Affected element** Claim about "hydrophobic microenvironment"
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Issue** The term "hydrophobic microenvironment" is used but not defined. It is unclear whether this refers to the entire active site, a specific pocket, or a set of residues. The abstract does not specify which mutations were made or how they altered hydrophobicity.
    - **Required correction** Define the hydrophobic microenvironment in terms of specific residues or structural features. Provide a table or figure in the full manuscript showing the mutations and their effect on local hydrophobicity (e.g., using molecular surface calculations or water occupancy from MD).
    - **Concern ID** R1-m2
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Reproducibility
    - **Affected element** Computational methods
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Issue** The abstract mentions QM calculations and MD simulations but provides no details on the level of theory (e.g., DFT functional, basis set), force field, simulation length, or software used. This is insufficient for reproducibility.
    - **Required correction** Include a brief description of computational methods in the abstract or ensure the full manuscript provides all necessary details (e.g., "QM calculations at the B3LYP/6-31G* level" and "MD simulations of 100 ns using the AMBER ff14SB force field").
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3. The core claims of activity enhancement, mechanistic understanding, and generalizability are not supported by the abstract alone.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
    - **Originality**: The concept of engineering the hydrophobic microenvironment to enhance proton transfer in lactonases is novel and potentially original. However, the abstract does not provide enough context to distinguish it from prior work on active-site hydrophobicity in other enzyme classes.
    - **Scientific importance**: If validated, the strategy could be important for improving ZEN detoxification, a significant food safety issue. The potential generalizability to other lactonases adds to its importance.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The work bridges structural biology, computational chemistry, and enzyme engineering, which could appeal to a broad audience in biochemistry and biotechnology.
    - **Technical soundness**: Cannot be assessed from the abstract. The claims rely on computational and experimental data that are not presented.
    - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The abstract is clear and well-structured, but terms like "near-attack conformation" and "hydrophobic microenvironment" may require brief explanation for a general audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract presents an interesting hypothesis, but the lack of quantitative data and mechanistic details prevents any meaningful evaluation. A full manuscript with kinetic data, computational validation, and transferability evidence is required to assess the work's validity and impact.
