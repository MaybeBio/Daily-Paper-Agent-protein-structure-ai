## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a methodologically ambitious integration of population-genetic and protein-structural approaches to prioritize PTGS paralog variants. The authors are appropriately cautious in distinguishing descriptive population differentiation from functional evidence, and they explicitly acknowledge the absence of experimental validation. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the rigor of the analyses, the validity of the prioritization framework, or the significance of the findings. The claim of a "calibrated prioritisation framework" is plausible but not verifiable from the supplied material. The work may interest pharmacogenomics and evolutionary genetics communities, but its current evidentiary base is too thin to assess technical soundness or broader impact.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Pharmacogenomics researchers studying NSAID response variability, population geneticists interested in selection signals in drug-target genes, and structural biologists examining cyclooxygenase channel architecture. The integration of population differentiation with protein-contextual analysis offers a template for variant prioritization that could be applied to other drug-target gene families.

- **Major strengths** The authors explicitly separate descriptive population differentiation (FST) from functional claims, avoiding overinterpretation. The acknowledgment that no signal-peptide predictor output was used and that functional effects were not demonstrated reflects scientific restraint. The multi-layered integration of allele frequency, transcript consequence, splice prediction, and structural context is conceptually sound. The retrospective comparison of Val511Ala against other missense candidates shows an attempt at internal benchmarking.

- **Major Concerns** None identified from the supplied material, as the abstract provides insufficient detail to substantiate or refute the core claims. However, the absence of verifiable evidence means the case is not established from the provided material.

- **Minor Comments** None identified from the supplied material, as the abstract is too brief to identify specific minor issues.

Without access to the full methods and results, the technical soundness of the FST calculations, splice predictions, docking analyses, and leader-sequence property computations cannot be assessed. The claim of "controlled structural analyses" requires detailed description of controls and parameters.

Scientific importance: Potentially relevant to NSAID pharmacogenomics, but the abstract does not demonstrate a clear advance over existing knowledge. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges population genetics, protein structure, and pharmacology, which could appeal to a broad audience, but the abstract is too technical and dense for nonspecialists. Technical soundness: Not assessable from the abstract alone; no methods or data are provided. ## Risk / unsupported claims

- The claim of a "calibrated prioritisation framework" is unsupported, as no calibration procedure or validation metrics are described.
- The statement that Val511Ala "ranked first by allele frequency, allele-frequency range and maximum pairwise FST within four channel-context candidates" is unverifiable without the underlying data.
- The claim that PTGS1 p.Trp8Arg and p.Pro17Leu produced "distinct directly calculated changes" in leader-sequence properties is unsupported, as no numerical values or statistical comparisons are provided.
- The assertion that "controlled docking-score, contact and direct-frame pose analyses detected no systematic variant-associated shift" is unverifiable without details of the docking protocol, controls, and statistical thresholds.
- The overall conclusion that the results "support a calibrated prioritisation framework" is not established from the abstract, as no evidence of calibration or validation is presented.
