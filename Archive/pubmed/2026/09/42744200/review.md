## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no access to full manuscript, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report modification of albumin-bound paclitaxel (Nab-PTX) by fusion with de novo designed protein binders targeting EGFR or HER2. They claim the resulting particles retain physicochemical properties of Nab-PTX, acquire receptor-specific binding, show increased uptake and inhibition in receptor-high cancer cell lines, and enhance tumor accumulation and suppression with possibly reduced off-target exposure in a xenograft model. They propose this as an adaptable modular platform for targeted albumin-based chemotherapeutics.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental details, numerical data, statistical analyses, or figure/table references are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, methods section, statistical analysis details, raw data, supplementary information, and any validation of the de novo designed binders

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a conceptually interesting approach that combines de novo protein design with an established clinical drug formulation. The modular platform concept has potential appeal. However, the abstract provides only qualitative claims without any quantitative data, statistical support, or experimental details. The central claims of enhanced targeting and therapeutic efficacy cannot be evaluated from the supplied material. The work may be of interest to the drug delivery and protein design communities, but the evidence base is insufficient to assess technical soundness or establish the stated conclusions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in nanomedicine and drug delivery, particularly those working on albumin-based formulations and targeted chemotherapy. The protein design community may also be interested in the application of de novo binders to functionalize existing drug carriers. Clinicians involved in cancer therapy could find the potential for reduced off-target toxicity relevant, though clinical translation is not demonstrated.
- **Major strengths** The conceptual integration of AI-based protein design with a widely used chemotherapeutic formulation is timely and potentially impactful. The modularity of the approach, allowing customization with different target binders, is a notable strength. The choice of EGFR and HER2 as targets is clinically relevant.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that binder-modified Nab-PTX particles "have increased uptake and inhibitory effects significantly" in receptor-high cell lines and "enhances tumor accumulation, improves tumor suppression, and may reduce off-target exposure" in a xenograft model.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
  - **Concern** No quantitative data, effect sizes, statistical significance values, or experimental replicates are reported. The terms "significantly" and "enhances" are used without supporting numerical evidence. No information is given on the magnitude of improvement over conventional Nab-PTX, the number of animals used, or the statistical methods applied.
  - **Why it matters** Without quantitative results and statistical analysis, the core claims of enhanced targeting and therapeutic efficacy cannot be verified. The abstract does not allow assessment of whether the observed effects are biologically meaningful or merely marginal.
  - **Resolution test** Provide numerical data with confidence intervals or p-values for in vitro uptake and inhibition assays, tumor growth curves, tumor weights, and biodistribution measurements. Include details on sample sizes and statistical tests used.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical validation
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract states that the de novo designed protein binders target human EGFR or HER2 and that the resulting particles "retain the similar physicochemical properties of Nab-PTX while acquiring the receptor-specific binding capacities."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
  - **Concern** No information is provided on how the de novo binders were designed, their binding affinities, specificity validation, or how fusion to albumin was achieved and confirmed. The claim that physicochemical properties are retained is unsupported by any data on particle size, polydispersity, drug loading, stability, or release profiles.
  - **Why it matters** The entire premise of the platform depends on the binders functioning as intended and the modified particles maintaining the favorable properties of Nab-PTX. Without validation of binder specificity and particle characteristics, the approach cannot be distinguished from nonspecific effects or formulation artifacts.
  - **Resolution test** Provide binding affinity measurements (e.g., KD values), specificity assays against off-target receptors, and characterization data for particle size, zeta potential, drug loading efficiency, and in vitro release kinetics.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Reproducibility and completeness
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the strategy "provides an adaptable modular platform" and that the particles "may have promising clinical potential."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract does not describe the manufacturing process, batch-to-batch variability, stability of the fusion proteins or the modified particles, or any assessment of immunogenicity or pharmacokinetics. The claim of clinical potential is speculative without these data.
  - **Why it matters** For a platform claim to be credible, the production and handling of the modified particles must be reproducible and scalable. Lack of stability and pharmacokinetic data prevents assessment of whether the approach is viable beyond the specific experimental conditions described.
  - **Resolution test** Include data on manufacturing reproducibility, storage stability, serum stability, pharmacokinetic profiles, and any immunogenicity assessment in the full manuscript.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3. The abstract lacks quantitative data, binder validation, particle characterization, and pharmacokinetic or biodistribution evidence. These are essential to establish the claimed targeting and efficacy.

  Scientific importance: If the claims are substantiated, the work could have implications for targeted chemotherapy. However, the importance is not yet established from the abstract alone.  
  Interdisciplinary readership: The topic bridges protein design, nanomedicine, and oncology, which could attract a broad readership. The abstract is written in accessible language.  
  Technical soundness: Cannot be assessed from the supplied material. No experimental details, controls, or statistical analyses are provided.  
  Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is generally clear and avoids excessive jargon, though the naming conventions could be clarified.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The conceptual approach is promising, but the abstract does not provide sufficient data to support the central claims. A supportive stance would require access to the full manuscript with quantitative results, binder validation, and particle characterization.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of "increased uptake and inhibitory effects significantly" is unsupported without numerical data or statistical analysis.
- The claim of "enhances tumor accumulation, improves tumor suppression, and may reduce off-target exposure" is unsupported without quantitative imaging, biodistribution, and tumor growth data.
- The claim that particles "retain the similar physicochemical properties of Nab-PTX" is unsupported without characterization data.
- The claim of "receptor-specific binding capacities" is unsupported without affinity or specificity measurements.
- The claim of "promising clinical potential" is speculative and not supported by the abstract-level evidence.
- The "adaptable modular platform" claim is not assessable without demonstration of generalizability to other binders or targets.
