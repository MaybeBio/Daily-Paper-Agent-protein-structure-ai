- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present AASIA, a database of predicted protein-protein interactions (PPIs) and their 3D complex structures for five agricultural animal species, built using an integrative pipeline combining interolog mapping, domain-domain interaction inference, and deep learning, with structures generated by ESMFold. The database contains 410,421 high-confidence PPIs and is claimed to be the first proteome-scale structural interactome resource for these species.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; database URL provided; no quantitative validation metrics, benchmark results, or comparison with existing resources are shown
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full methods, validation statistics, database architecture details, case studies, comparison with existing interactome resources, and any supplementary figures or tables

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract describes a potentially valuable community resource, but the evidence provided is insufficient to evaluate the technical soundness of the prediction pipeline, the confidence of the PPIs, or the quality of the structural models. The claims of "high-confidence" and "first proteome-scale" require substantiation with quantitative benchmarks and comparisons. The work is relevant to agricultural genomics and structural biology, but the current abstract alone does not establish the case.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in agricultural genomics, animal breeding, and veterinary science would benefit from a species-specific interactome resource. Structural biologists and computational biologists interested in PPI prediction and AI-driven structure modeling would also find the resource relevant. The database could support variant interpretation and multi-omics integration in livestock species, appealing to applied genetics groups.
- **Major strengths** The resource addresses a clear gap in species-specific interactome coverage for agricultural animals. The integration of multiple prediction methods with structural modeling is a sensible approach. The database is made publicly available, which supports community use and reproducibility.

  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
