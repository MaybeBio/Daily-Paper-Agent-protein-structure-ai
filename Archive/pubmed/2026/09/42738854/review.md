## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, main text, tables referenced but not provided in full)
- **Assessment boundary** Conceptual framework, evidentiary logic, operationalization, and validation design; not experimental data or novel empirical results
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The manuscript proposes the Dynamic Protein Structure Paradox (DPSP) as an integrative framework for assessing whether a given protein structure provides sufficient evidence for a specified functional endpoint. It distinguishes structural accuracy from functional sufficiency, defines four coupled assessment dimensions (relevant state, context, ensemble/kinetics, chemistry), and offers a scoring rubric with three modes of utilization (geometry-led, conditional, function-measured). The framework is explicitly unvalidated, with prospective studies proposed.
- **Visible evidence base** Literature citations (structural genomics, allostery, disorder, SKEMPI, AlphaFold), illustrative examples (KaiB, GPCR, kinase inhibitor program), proposed validation study designs (Study 1 and Study 2), and a scoring rubric (Tables 4-6 referenced but not fully visible)
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full tables (especially Tables 4-6 with rubric criteria and worked examples), the published scoring rubric referenced as prior work, and any supplementary materials. Without these, the operational details of the rubric cannot be independently assessed.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a genuine and important problem: the conflation of structural accuracy with functional evidence in the era of high-throughput structure prediction. The proposed framework is conceptually sound, clearly articulated, and appropriately scoped as an integrative tool rather than a new theory. The distinction between state accuracy and functional sufficiency is well motivated by established literature. However, the manuscript's value hinges on the operational rubric, which is not fully visible in the provided material, and on the proposed validation studies, which are described but not executed. The framework's practical utility, inter-rater reliability, and incremental validity over existing approaches remain unestablished. The writing is clear and accessible, though somewhat repetitive in places.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists using cryo-EM, X-ray crystallography, or NMR to support functional claims; computational biologists applying AlphaFold or other predictors to annotate function; drug discovery scientists using structures for hit-to-lead optimization; journal reviewers and editors evaluating structure-to-function manuscripts; and methodologists interested in evidentiary standards for structural biology.
- **Major strengths** (1) The core distinction between structural accuracy and functional sufficiency is important, timely, and clearly articulated. (2) The framework is explicitly integrative, drawing on established findings rather than overclaiming novelty. (3) The four dimensions are well chosen and the coupling between them is acknowledged. (4) The manuscript is unusually honest about its own limitations and validation status. (5) The proposed validation studies are thoughtfully designed with attention to blinding, preregistration, and refutation criteria.
- **Major Concerns** See detailed items below.
- **Minor Comments** See detailed items below.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (rubric not visible), R1-M2 (no reliability data), R1-M3 (validation studies not executed), R1-M4 (comparison to existing frameworks incomplete).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** **Originality**: Moderate. The problem is well known, and the framework is an integration rather than a conceptual breakthrough. The specific operationalization is novel but incremental. **Scientific importance**: High. If validated, the framework could improve the rigor of structure-to-function claims across structural biology and drug discovery. **Interdisciplinary readership**: Moderate. The manuscript is accessible to structural biologists, computational biologists, and drug discovery scientists, but may be less engaging for broader biological audiences. **Technical soundness**: The conceptual logic is sound, but the operational rubric and validation design cannot be fully assessed from the provided material. **Readability for nonspecialists**: Good. The writing is clear, with helpful examples and explicit scoping of claims.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The conceptual framework is valuable and well presented, but the manuscript's central claim of practical utility requires the full rubric, reliability data, and at least pilot validation results to be convincing.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Operationalization
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims the framework is "operationalized as a published scoring rubric with explicit adequate, uncertain, and missing criteria; worked examples; a materiality test; three mutually exclusive modes of use."
- **Evidence pointer** Section 5, Tables 4-6 (referenced but not provided in full)
- **Concern** The central operational claim of the manuscript cannot be evaluated because the rubric itself is not visible in the provided material. Tables 4-6 are referenced as containing the criteria, examples, and materiality test, but their content is not available. Without these, the reader cannot assess whether the rubric is clear, complete, or consistently applicable.
- **Why it matters** The manuscript's value proposition is that it provides a practical, reproducible tool. If the rubric is not visible, the reader cannot verify this claim or apply the framework. The distinction between "adequate," "uncertain," and "missing" is the core of the framework, and its operational definition is essential.
- **Resolution test** Provide the full rubric (Tables 4-6) in the manuscript or supplementary materials. The rubric should be self-contained, with criteria that are specific enough for independent raters to apply consistently.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Reliability
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript states the framework's value "relies on the reliability and incremental validity studies as outlined, rather than on the argument's plausibility."
- **Evidence pointer** Section 8.6 (Reliability Pilot), Section 9 (Limitations)
- **Concern** The manuscript explicitly acknowledges that no reliability pilot has been conducted and reports no reliability data. The framework's utility depends on inter-rater agreement, but this is entirely unmeasured. The proposed pilot design is reasonable, but it is a proposal, not evidence.
- **Why it matters** A framework that cannot be applied consistently by independent assessors has no practical value, as the manuscript itself states. Without reliability data, the framework remains an untested proposal, and the manuscript's claim to provide a usable tool is unsupported.
- **Resolution test** Conduct the proposed reliability pilot and report the results, including kappa coefficients and confidence intervals. If the pilot has not been conducted, the manuscript should be reframed as a proposal for a framework, not a validated tool.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Validation
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript proposes two validation studies (Study 1 and Study 2) with specific designs, endpoints, and refutation criteria.
- **Evidence pointer** Section 8.1-8.4
- **Concern** The validation studies are described in detail but not executed. The manuscript provides no results, no pilot data, and no evidence that the proposed designs are feasible. The studies are well designed on paper, but their execution is essential to establish the framework's validity.
- **Why it matters** The manuscript's central claim is that the framework improves evidentiary assessment. This claim requires empirical support. Without validation results, the framework's predictive value and practical utility are entirely speculative.
- **Resolution test** Execute at least one of the proposed studies, or a pilot version, and report the results. If this is not possible, the manuscript should be explicitly framed as a proposal for validation, not a validated framework.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Comparative positioning
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims DPSP "complements, rather than replaces, existing structural, ensemble, and computational approaches" and distinguishes itself from frameworks like FDA, ASME V, CAFA, and SKEMPI.
- **Evidence pointer** Section 1.4, Table 1
- **Concern** The comparison with existing frameworks is presented in Table 1, which is referenced but not fully visible. The manuscript's claim of complementarity is plausible, but the specific distinctions from each framework are not fully articulated in the visible text. The relationship to other evidentiary frameworks (e.g., those used in structural biology for validation of cryo-EM maps) is not discussed.
- **Why it matters** The manuscript's contribution is integrative, so its value depends on clear positioning relative to existing tools. If the distinctions are not clear, readers may not understand when to use DPSP versus existing approaches.
- **Resolution test** Expand the comparison in the text, not just in the table, and explicitly state what DPSP adds beyond each existing framework. Discuss the relationship to structural biology validation standards (e.g., map-model validation criteria).

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Terminology
- **Affected element** Section 1.3, term "paradox"
- **Evidence pointer** Section 1.3
- **Issue** The term "paradox" is used in a "constrained sense" and the manuscript acknowledges it is not a true paradox. The comparison with Anfinsen and Levinthal is explicitly "purely expository." The term may mislead readers and overstate the novelty of the framework.
- **Required correction** Consider using a more precise term such as "apparent paradox" or "evidentiary gap" throughout, or justify the term more strongly in the introduction.

- **Concern ID** R1-m2
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Repetition
- **Affected element** Sections 2-4
- **Evidence pointer** Sections 2.2, 3.1, 4.1
- **Issue** The core distinction between structural accuracy and functional sufficiency is restated multiple times across sections, which makes the manuscript feel repetitive. The examples (KaiB, GPCR, antibody) are used in multiple places with similar points.
- **Required correction** Consolidate the repeated explanations and move the worked examples to a single, dedicated section.

- **Concern ID** R1-m3
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Scope
- **Affected element** Section 8.3
- **Evidence pointer** Section 8.3
- **Issue** The proposed Study 1 uses SKEMPI 2.0 data for binding free energy changes, but the framework is intended to be general. The choice of this specific dataset and endpoint may limit the generalizability of the validation.
- **Required correction** Acknowledge this limitation explicitly and discuss how the framework might be validated for other endpoints (e.g., catalysis, signaling) in future work.

- **Concern ID** R1-m4
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** References
- **Affected element** Section 1.1
- **Evidence pointer** Section 1.1
- **Issue** The introduction cites AlphaFold, RoseTTAFold, ESMFold, and AlphaFold 3 but does not cite the original papers for these tools, only the review context. This makes it difficult for readers to find the primary sources.
- **Required correction** Add citations to the original papers for each tool mentioned.

- **Concern ID** R1-m5
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Clarity
- **Affected element** Section 8.4
- **Evidence pointer** Section 8.4
- **Issue** The description of Study 2's noninferiority margin is vague. The manuscript states the margin will be "estimated from a calibration set and fixed prior to unblinding," but does not specify how the margin will be chosen or what constitutes a meaningful difference.
- **Required correction** Provide more detail on how the noninferiority margin will be determined, or reference a standard approach for margin selection.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the framework is "operationalized as a published scoring rubric" cannot be verified from the provided material, as the rubric is not visible.
- The claim that the framework's value "relies on the reliability and incremental validity studies" is an admission that the framework is unvalidated; this is honest but means the framework's utility is unsupported.
- The claim that DPSF "complements, rather than replaces, existing approaches" is plausible but not fully supported by the visible text, as the comparison table is not provided.
- The illustrative scoring in Table 6 is described as conducted by a single unblinded assessor, and the manuscript correctly states this is not evidence of reliability or validity; however, the inclusion of such examples without reliability data may be misleading to readers.
- The manuscript's claim that the framework is "integrative" is supported by the literature review, but the specific value added beyond existing frameworks is not fully demonstrated without the comparison table.
