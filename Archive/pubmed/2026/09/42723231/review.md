- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that the N-terminal domain (NTD) of the farnesoid X receptor (FXR) engages in interdomain contacts with other FXR domains and interacts directly with coregulator proteins. They identify a novel SXXLF motif in the NTD that mediates these interactions, based on mutagenesis, mammalian two-hybrid assays, mammalian one-hybrid assay, and molecular dynamics simulations. They further claim that mutation of this motif induces large changes in conformational and allosteric coupling in FXR, and that the motif modulates FXR transcriptional activity.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the NTD "engages in interdomain contact with other FXR domains" and "interacts directly with coregulator proteins."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that these interactions were observed, but no experimental data, binding affinities, or statistical significance are reported. It is unclear which assays produced these observations and whether the interactions were validated under physiologically relevant conditions.  
- **Why it matters** Direct interaction claims require quantitative or at least semi-quantitative evidence, such as binding curves, co-immunoprecipitation with appropriate controls, or two-hybrid data with proper positive and negative controls. Without such data, the interaction claims are unverifiable.  
- **Resolution test** Provide representative data from the interaction assays, including positive and negative controls, replicate numbers, and statistical analysis. If binding affinities are available, report them with confidence intervals.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Functional significance  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "mutation of the motif induces large changes in conformational and allosteric coupling in FXR" and that the motif "modulates the transcriptional activity of FXR."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not report the magnitude or direction of the transcriptional changes, the assay conditions, or the specific readouts used to assess allosteric coupling. The term "large changes" is qualitative and not supported by any numerical data.  
- **Why it matters** The functional relevance of the motif is central to the paper's significance. Without quantitative transcriptional activity data and a clear demonstration of allosteric effects, the claim that this motif modulates FXR function is not established.  
- **Resolution test** Report transcriptional activity values for wild-type and mutant FXR in the one-hybrid assay, with statistical comparisons. For allosteric coupling, provide quantitative metrics from MD simulations, such as correlation coefficients or free energy differences, with error estimates.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Novelty and contextualization  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim to identify "a novel SXXLF motif" that mediates interactions with coregulators and the ligand binding domain.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not compare the SXXLF motif to known NR interaction motifs, such as LXXLL in coregulators or FXXLF in androgen receptor. It is unclear whether SXXLF is truly novel or a variant of an existing motif class.  
- **Why it matters** The novelty claim is a key selling point. If the motif is a variant of a known motif, the finding is incremental rather than novel. A sequence alignment or functional comparison with known motifs is necessary to support the novelty claim.  
- **Resolution test** Provide a sequence alignment of the SXXLF motif with known NR interaction motifs and demonstrate differential binding or functional specificity through mutagenesis or competitive binding assays.
