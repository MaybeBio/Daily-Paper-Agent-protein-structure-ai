## Review setup

- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract; no evaluation of full-text data, figures, or methods
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The abstract claims that thermostable carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes) can be engineered to operate at 60-90 degrees C, that multiscale molecular mechanisms can be linked to process-relevant performance, and that representative engineered alpha-amylases and cellulases achieve DeltaT(m) improvements of approximately 10-20 degrees C with >80-85% residual activity, translating into 15-30% reductions in enzyme dosing and decreased steam consumption.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript text, experimental data, case study details, quantitative process metrics, and any statistical analyses are not available. The abstract cites "representative case studies" without specifying sources or providing verifiable data.

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a well-structured and timely review topic that bridges molecular enzymology and bioprocess engineering. The scope is coherent and the proposed integration of molecular mechanisms with process-level outcomes is conceptually valuable. However, the abstract relies on broad quantitative claims that cannot be verified from the supplied material. The absence of specific citations, data tables, or methodological details limits the ability to assess the robustness of the stated performance improvements. The review appears to be a useful synthesis, but the evidence base for the headline numbers is not established from the abstract alone.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers and engineers in industrial biotechnology, biofuel production, enzyme engineering, and biorefinery process development would find this review relevant. The focus on thermostable CAZymes for high-temperature biomass conversion addresses a practical bottleneck in lignocellulosic and starch-based biorefineries. The integration of molecular design strategies with process metrics could inform both academic research directions and industrial enzyme development programs.
- **Major strengths** The abstract clearly identifies a gap in existing literature, namely the lack of integrated multiscale perspectives linking molecular thermostability determinants to process-level outcomes. The structure is logical, moving from molecular mechanisms to engineering strategies and then to case studies and process benefits. The inclusion of modern approaches such as machine learning-assisted workflows and DBTL cycles reflects current trends in enzyme engineering. The stated goal of providing a "pragmatic roadmap" is appropriate for a review article.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that engineered thermophilic alpha-amylases and cellulases achieve DeltaT(m) improvements of approximately 10-20 degrees C and sustain >80-85% residual activity after prolonged exposure at process-relevant temperatures, with 15-30% reductions in enzyme dosing and measurable decreases in steam consumption.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, "Representative case studies" section, location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract presents specific quantitative ranges for DeltaT(m) improvements, residual activity, enzyme dosing reductions, and steam consumption without identifying the source studies, the number of cases, the enzyme variants, or the assay conditions. These figures are presented as generalizable outcomes, but no data, figures, or references are visible in the supplied material.
  - **Why it matters** Quantitative claims of this specificity carry weight in a review and may be cited by others. Without verifiable sources or methodological context, the numbers could be misinterpreted as consensus values or as representative of all engineered CAZymes, which would be misleading. The credibility of the review depends on the accuracy and traceability of these performance metrics.
  - **Resolution test** The full manuscript should provide a table or figure listing each case study with the specific enzyme, engineering strategy, DeltaT(m) value, residual activity assay conditions, and the process-level metrics. Each entry should be linked to a primary citation. The abstract should then either retain the ranges with explicit reference to the number of cases or soften the language to indicate that these are illustrative examples.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scope coherence
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract states that existing reviews "rarely integrate multiscale molecular mechanisms with process-relevant engineering strategies" and that this review "systematically examines" this integration.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, introductory and concluding sections, location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract does not specify the literature search strategy, inclusion criteria, or the time frame of the reviewed studies. It is unclear whether the claim of rarity is based on a systematic survey or on the authors' qualitative impression. The scope of "multiscale" is listed as covering hydrophobic packing, electrostatic networks, disulfide bonds, conformational dynamics, and modular architectures, but the depth of coverage for each is not indicated.
  - **Why it matters** A review's value depends on its comprehensiveness and reproducibility. If the literature coverage is selective or non-systematic, the claim of filling a gap may be overstated. Readers need to know how the authors identified the gap and how they selected the studies to trust the synthesis.
  - **Resolution test** The full manuscript should include a methods subsection describing the search databases, keywords, inclusion and exclusion criteria, and the number of screened and included studies. The abstract should either state that the review is systematic or explicitly frame it as a narrative synthesis with defined scope boundaries.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Generalizability
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract states that the engineering strategies are "critically compared" and that the review provides a "pragmatic roadmap for deploying thermostable CAZymes as robust biocatalysts in sustainable high-temperature biomass conversion."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, "engineering strategies" and "concluding" sections, location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract lists six engineering strategies but does not indicate the criteria used for comparison, the number of studies per strategy, or whether any strategy emerged as superior in specific contexts. The "roadmap" language implies actionable guidance, but the abstract does not specify decision points, such as which strategy to choose for a given enzyme class or process condition.
  - **Why it matters** A critical comparison should offer more than a list of pros and cons. Without explicit comparison criteria or a decision framework, the "roadmap" may be too generic to guide practitioners. The review's practical utility depends on the specificity of its recommendations.
  - **Resolution test** The full manuscript should include a comparative table or framework that scores each strategy against defined criteria such as success rate, resource intensity, applicability to different CAZyme families, and compatibility with high-throughput screening. The concluding section should translate these comparisons into explicit recommendations with conditions of applicability.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity
  - **Affected element** Terminology
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, "DeltaT(m)" usage, location not provided
  - **Issue** The term "DeltaT(m)" is used without definition. While specialists will recognize it as the change in melting temperature, nonspecialist readers in process engineering may not.
  - **Required correction** Define DeltaT(m) at first use, for example as "the change in protein melting temperature, a common proxy for thermostability."
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Precision
  - **Affected element** Quantitative ranges
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, "approximately 10-20 degrees C" and ">80-85%", location not provided
  - **Issue** The ranges "approximately 10-20" and ">80-85%" are imprecise. The first mixes approximation with a range, and the second combines an inequality with a range, which is ambiguous.
  - **Required correction** Use precise ranges or point values, for example "DeltaT(m) improvements of 10-20 degrees C" and "residual activity above 80% in most cases, with some exceeding 85%." Alternatively, report median and range if the full data are available.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Completeness
  - **Affected element** Process metrics
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, "15-30% reductions in enzyme dosing and measurable decreases in steam consumption", location not provided
  - **Issue** The abstract mentions "measurable decreases in steam consumption" without a quantitative range or a reference to the process configuration. This is vague and could be interpreted as anecdotal.
  - **Required correction** Provide a quantitative range or a specific example with process conditions, or state that these metrics are qualitative observations from selected case studies.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 is the primary blocking concern. The quantitative performance claims must be traceable to specific studies with defined conditions. Without this, the central assertion that engineered CAZymes deliver the stated process benefits is not verifiable from the supplied material. R1-M2 and R1-M3 are non-blocking but should be addressed to strengthen the review's methodological rigor and practical utility.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**  
  - Originality: The abstract proposes an integrated multiscale perspective that is not commonly found in existing reviews. This is a moderate strength, though the individual components are well established.  
  - Scientific importance: The topic is relevant to sustainable bioprocessing and enzyme engineering. The potential impact on biorefinery efficiency is significant, but the importance is not fully demonstrated without verifiable case study data.  
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The abstract bridges molecular biology, biochemistry, and chemical engineering. The language is accessible to specialists in each field, though some terms may need definition for broader audiences.  
  - Technical soundness: The conceptual framework is sound, but the quantitative claims cannot be assessed for accuracy from the abstract alone. The lack of methodological detail for the literature review also weakens technical confidence.  
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is generally clear and logically organized. The use of technical terms such as "DBTL cycles" and "DeltaT(m)" may require explanation for readers outside the immediate field.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The review topic is valuable and the proposed integration is timely. However, the abstract's quantitative claims must be substantiated with traceable data in the full manuscript. The authors should also clarify the literature review methodology and provide a more explicit comparative framework for the engineering strategies. Once these issues are addressed, the review would be a useful contribution to the field.
