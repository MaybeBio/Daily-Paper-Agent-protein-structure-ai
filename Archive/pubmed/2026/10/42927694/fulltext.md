Controlling thermal deformation of 4D printing gels remains a significant challenge for achieving 3D structural stability of printing products. This study focused on the regulation of tapioca starch (TS) and its acetylated derivative (ATS) on thermal deformation (4D) of 3D-printed "crown" models based on surimi inks. The 4D printing images showed that the "crown" underwent a "flowering" deformation during steam heating. The COMSOL simulation revealed that greater thermal deformation on inner side than on outer one of model, resulted from heat and stress accumulation on inner side near the base of model. Both TS and ATS (particularly ATS) hindered the thermal deformation degree (from 27.8% to 16.5% and 11.7%), due to the improvement of heat conduction uniformity as evidenced by heat maps. Additionally, compared with single surimi gel, the TS/ATS-incorporated surimi gels exhibited higher gel strength and denser structure, suggesting an increasing resistance to thermal deformation. Analysis of protein secondary and tertiary structure and molecular force indicated that TS/ATS promoted the transformation from alpha-helix into beta-sheets, exposing more hydrophobic amino acid residues and forming more disulfide bonds in proteins. In case of ATS, hydrophobic interactions formed between its acetyl groups and the hydrophobic residues of proteins promoted protein to unfold and cross-link, thus suppressing moisture evaporation and thermal deformation. Overall, disulfide bond promoted by starch was the key for regulating network strength, and further thermal deformation of surimi gel. This study offered a new insight into regulating the thermal deformation of 4D-printed products via molecular forces by incorporating starch materials.
