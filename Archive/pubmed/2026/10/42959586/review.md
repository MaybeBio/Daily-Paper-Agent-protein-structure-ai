## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and conclusion. Figures, tables, and supplementary material are referenced but not provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific soundness, methodological rigor, validity of claims relative to provided evidence, and suitability for the stated aims. No assessment of formatting or editorial style.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present a nonredundant, resolution-stratified benchmark dataset of 972 protein-chain samples from 495 cryo-EM structures resolved at 4.5–8.0 Å, with voxel-level annotations for helix, sheet, and non-periodic secondary structure (NPSS). They evaluate five deep learning architectures (U-Net, Residual U-Net, Attention U-Net, UNETR, Swin-UNETR) under standardized conditions and report that Residual U-Net achieves the highest macro-averaged foreground F1 score in all three resolution bins, with helices generally segmented more accurately than sheets or NPSS.
- **Visible evidence base** Text descriptions of dataset construction, annotation, training, and evaluation procedures. Reported numerical results in the text (e.g., F1 scores for architectures across bins, CC score distribution). References to Figures 1–5 and Tables 1–2, none of which were provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures and tables, including the dataset overview figure, CC score distribution figure, resolution-wise dataset composition table, training configuration table, and all results figures. No code, trained models, or supplementary material. No statistical significance testing or variance reporting.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a genuine and timely need: standardized benchmarking for secondary-structure segmentation in intermediate-resolution cryo-EM maps. The dataset construction pipeline is described in considerable detail and appears methodologically sound. However, the scientific value of the contribution cannot be fully assessed without the actual results figures and tables, which are referenced but not provided. The reported numerical results are sparse and lack statistical context. The claim that Residual U-Net is the best-performing architecture is based on mean scores without variance or significance testing. The manuscript would benefit from clearer articulation of what is genuinely novel relative to existing benchmarks and from a more critical discussion of the relatively low absolute performance values.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational structural biologists developing or applying deep learning methods for cryo-EM map interpretation. Researchers working on protein structure prediction, model building, and secondary-structure assignment from density maps. The benchmark dataset and evaluation framework would be of practical use to this community for standardized method comparison.
- **Major strengths** The dataset construction pipeline is described with unusual care, including explicit selection criteria, redundancy removal via MMseqs2 clustering, and map-to-model agreement filtering with a conservative CC threshold. The resolution-stratified design (three bins within 4.5–8.0 Å) is a thoughtful and useful contribution that addresses a real gap in the literature. The controlled evaluation framework, with fixed partitions, standardized preprocessing, and common metrics, is exactly what the field needs. The inclusion of both CNN-based and transformer-based architectures provides a reasonable initial comparison across architectural paradigms.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that "Residual U-Net achieved the highest macro-averaged foreground (Macro-FG) F1 score in all three resolution bins" and that "All five architectures achieve their highest Macro-FG F1 scores in the highest-resolution bin."
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 2, Figure 3, Figure 4, location not provided
  - **Concern** The central quantitative results of the paper are presented only as references to figures that were not provided for review. The only numerical values given in the text are a few example F1 scores from the qualitative examples (e.g., Attention U-Net achieving 0.46 Macro-FG F1 on one Bin 1 chain). Without the actual results figures and tables, it is impossible to verify the claims about architecture ranking, resolution-dependent trends, or class-specific performance. The reported mean scores lack any measure of variance, confidence intervals, or statistical significance testing, making it impossible to determine whether the observed differences between architectures are meaningful.
  - **Why it matters** The primary scientific contribution of this paper is the empirical comparison of architectures under controlled conditions. If the results figures and tables are not available, the evidence base for the central claims is absent. Furthermore, without variance or significance testing, even the provided means cannot be interpreted reliably. The difference between Residual U-Net (0.386) and U-Net (0.373) in Bin 1, for example, may or may not be meaningful depending on the spread of per-chain scores.
  - **Resolution test** Provide all results figures and tables. Report per-chain score distributions, standard deviations, or confidence intervals for all reported means. Conduct and report statistical significance testing (e.g., paired tests across chains) for architecture comparisons within each bin.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Methodological validity
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that "voxels within a 3 Å radius of a Cα atom were assigned the secondary-structure class of the corresponding residue" and that the dataset includes "voxel-level annotations for helices, sheets, and non-periodic secondary-structure (NPSS) regions."
  - **Evidence pointer** Materials and Methods, Secondary Structure Annotation, location not provided
  - **Concern** The annotation procedure assigns secondary-structure labels based solely on distance to Cα atoms. This is a substantial simplification that may not accurately represent the true spatial extent of secondary-structure elements in the density map. A 3 Å fixed radius around Cα atoms will systematically under-label the volume occupied by helices and sheets, which have finite cross-sectional dimensions. The manuscript does not discuss how this annotation strategy compares to alternatives, such as using all backbone atoms or side-chain atoms, or how sensitive the results are to the choice of radius. The authors also do not report any validation of the annotation quality, such as comparison with manually curated segmentations or alternative labeling schemes.
  - **Why it matters** The quality and validity of the ground-truth labels are foundational to the entire benchmark. If the labels are systematically biased or inaccurate, all subsequent performance measurements are compromised. The choice of a 3 Å Cα-based radius is a critical methodological decision that could substantially affect the measured performance of all architectures, and the manuscript does not justify this choice or assess its consequences.
  - **Resolution test** Justify the 3 Å Cα-based annotation radius with reference to prior work or physical considerations. Report sensitivity analyses with different radii (e.g., 2.5 Å, 3.5 Å, 4 Å) or alternative annotation strategies. Validate the generated labels against an independent source, such as manual expert annotation on a subset of chains or comparison with segmentations from established tools.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific context
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that "there remains a lack of standardized evaluation protocols and systematic resolution-stratified analyses" and that existing methods "were primarily designed to introduce and evaluate specific deep-learning frameworks using study-specific datasets and experimental protocols."
  - **Evidence pointer** Introduction, Related Work and Research Gaps, location not provided
  - **Concern** The manuscript does not provide a systematic comparison with existing benchmark datasets or evaluation protocols in the cryo-EM secondary-structure segmentation literature. While the authors correctly note that prior studies used different datasets and protocols, they do not quantify or characterize these differences in a way that would allow readers to understand what specific gap this benchmark fills. For example, the EMNIST dataset mentioned in the Related Work section is not described in terms of its size, resolution range, or annotation strategy, so the reader cannot assess how the present benchmark differs. The claim of novelty for the resolution-stratified design is plausible but not substantiated by a thorough review of existing resources.
  - **Why it matters** The scientific contribution of a benchmark dataset depends on its novelty and utility relative to existing resources. Without a clear and detailed comparison to prior datasets and evaluation protocols, the reader cannot assess whether this benchmark fills a genuine gap or duplicates existing efforts with minor modifications.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a detailed comparison table or section characterizing existing benchmark datasets and evaluation protocols for cryo-EM secondary-structure segmentation, including dataset size, resolution range, annotation strategy, and evaluation metrics. Explicitly state what the present benchmark adds beyond these existing resources.

  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Affected element** Materials and Methods, Training Configuration
  - **Evidence pointer** Table 2, location not provided
  - **Issue** The manuscript states that "all five architectures were trained using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 3 × 10–4" and that "Swin-UNETR was trained with a batch size of 4 because of its higher GPU-memory requirement." The difference in batch size between Swin-UNETR and the other architectures is a potential confound, as batch size can affect optimization dynamics and final performance. The manuscript does not discuss this limitation.
  - **Required correction** Acknowledge the batch size difference as a potential confound and discuss its possible impact on the comparison. Consider reporting results with Swin-UNETR trained at a smaller batch size for the other architectures as a sensitivity check, or justify why the batch size difference is unlikely to affect the conclusions.

  - **Issue** The discussion of why performance decreases from Bin 1 to Bin 2 but not necessarily from Bin 2 to Bin 3 is somewhat speculative. - **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (results figures and tables not provided, no variance or significance testing) and R1-M2 (annotation strategy not validated or justified) are blocking. R1-M3 (insufficient comparison with existing benchmarks) is important for establishing novelty but may be addressable with additional text rather than new experiments.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: Moderate. The resolution-stratified design is a useful contribution, but the overall approach of deep learning for secondary-structure segmentation is well established. Scientific importance: Moderate. The benchmark has clear utility for the cryo-EM community, but the manuscript does not demonstrate that the benchmark enables new scientific insights beyond what existing datasets provide. Interdisciplinary readership: Limited. The manuscript is written for a specialized audience of computational structural biologists and may not be accessible to a broader readership. Technical soundness: The dataset construction pipeline is carefully described, but the annotation strategy is insufficiently justified and the results lack statistical rigor. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is clearly written but assumes substantial background in both cryo-EM and deep learning. The abstract and introduction are accessible, but the methods and results sections would be challenging for nonspecialists.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The dataset and evaluation framework are potentially valuable contributions, and the manuscript addresses a genuine need in the field. However, the blocking concerns regarding missing results evidence and annotation validation must be addressed before the scientific claims can be considered established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that Residual U-Net achieves the highest Macro-FG F1 score in all three resolution bins is unsupported without the actual results figures and tables, and without statistical significance testing.
- The claim that "all five architectures achieve their highest Macro-FG F1 scores in the highest-resolution bin" is unsupported for the same reason.
- The claim that the dataset enables "fine-grained analysis of resolution-dependent segmentation performance" is plausible but not demonstrated without the results figures.
- The claim that the annotation procedure produces reliable ground-truth labels is unsupported without validation against alternative labeling schemes or manual annotation.
- The claim that the benchmark is "nonredundant" is supported by the described MMseqs2 clustering procedure, but the specific parameters and resulting redundancy levels are not fully reported.
- The claim that the dataset reflects "contemporary" cryo-EM practices is supported by the 2020–2026 release date range, but the potential impact of this restricted range on generalizability is not discussed.
