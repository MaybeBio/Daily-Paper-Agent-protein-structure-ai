## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, results and discussion, conclusions, and materials and methods. No figures, tables, or supplementary materials were provided for inspection.
- **Assessment boundary** Assessment is limited to the textual content of the manuscript. Claims regarding simulation results, structural analyses, and methodological outcomes are evaluated based on the descriptions provided in the text alone. No raw data, trajectory files, or structural coordinates were available for independent verification.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that (i) conventional MD simulations of the apo HuR tandem RRM1-RRM2 construct show extensive interdomain motions while the RNA-bound complex remains stable; (ii) a novel application of Supervised MD (SuMD) to an intramolecular conformational transition, using a multistep supervision protocol based on selected interdomain residue pairs, reproduces a closed conformation resembling the experimental RNA-bound structure; (iii) per-residue interaction analyses identify key contributions from the linker region, particularly Arg97, Arg136, and Arg147, consistent with published mutagenesis data; and (iv) the reclosure pathway transiently exposes putative druggable pockets not detectable in endpoint conformations.
- **Visible evidence base** Textual descriptions of simulation protocols, RMSD and RMSF values, interaction energy analyses, residue-level contact patterns, and pocket detection results. Specific numerical values are reported for some quantities (e.g., RMSD values of 9.04 Å, 4.33 Å, 14.57 Å; interaction energy of −560.60 kcal/mol), but the corresponding figures, tables, and supplementary materials are not available for inspection.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures (Figures 1-7), tables (Tables 1-3, S1, S2), supplementary figures (Figures S1-S10), and Video S1 were not provided. These materials are essential for evaluating the validity of the reported structural analyses, the quality of the SuMD trajectories, the convergence assessments, and the pocket detection results. Without these, several key claims cannot be independently verified.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a methodologically interesting application of SuMD to an intramolecular conformational transition in HuR, a target of therapeutic relevance. The authors are transparent about the limitations of their approach, including the stochastic nature of SuMD and the lack of a validated metric to distinguish productive from unproductive trajectories. However, the manuscript has several significant weaknesses that currently prevent a full endorsement. The central claim of reproducing a "closed conformation resembling the experimental RNA-bound structure" rests on a single successful trajectory out of multiple attempts, and the reported RMSD values (9.04 Å for the full construct) are high by structural biology standards. The mechanistic interpretation, while plausible, is derived from a single trajectory and lacks statistical support. The pocket detection analysis is explicitly preliminary and the authors appropriately caution against overinterpretation, but the inclusion of this material without more rigorous validation adds limited value. The manuscript would benefit from a more critical discussion of what constitutes "success" in this context, additional validation of the SuMD-derived pathway, and a clearer articulation of the practical utility of the identified transient pockets. The writing is generally clear, though some methodological details require clarification.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational chemists and biophysicists working on enhanced sampling methods, particularly those interested in extending SuMD-type approaches beyond intermolecular recognition to intramolecular conformational transitions. Researchers studying RNA-binding proteins, especially HuR, as a therapeutic target in oncology and neurodegeneration, may find the mechanistic insights into domain closure and the identification of transient pockets of interest for structure-based drug design efforts. Methodologists developing supervised or directed sampling techniques would also be interested in the multistep supervision protocol described here.
- **Major strengths** (1) The manuscript addresses a biologically and therapeutically relevant question: the conformational transition of HuR between open and closed states, which is central to its RNA-binding function. (2) The extension of SuMD to an intramolecular transition is a novel methodological contribution, and the authors are appropriately cautious in framing this as a first application. (3) The multistep supervision protocol, inspired by prior work on protein-nucleic acid recognition, is a thoughtful adaptation that addresses the failure of single-step supervision. (4) The authors are transparent about the limitations of their approach, including the stochastic nature of SuMD, the lack of a validated success metric, and the preliminary nature of the pocket analysis. (5) The per-residue interaction analysis recovers experimentally validated key residues (Arg97, Arg136, Arg147), providing some confidence in the biological relevance of the simulated pathway.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness / Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the multistep SuMD protocol "succeeded in reproducing a nearly crystal-like conformation of closed RNA-bound HuR, with an RMSD of 9.04 Å for the RRM2 backbone."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion, "Simulations of HuR Conformational Transition" section; Figure 5; Figure S7; location not provided for specific figure panels
  - **Concern** The central success claim rests on a single trajectory (run1 of Supervision Step-2) out of four attempts. An RMSD of 9.04 Å for the RRM2 backbone relative to the crystal structure is substantial and does not constitute a "nearly crystal-like" conformation by conventional structural biology standards. The authors note that when aligning on the entire backbone the RMSD drops to 4.33 Å, but this is a global measure that may mask local structural deviations. The manuscript does not report whether the key interdomain contacts, hydrogen bonds, and salt bridges observed in the crystal structure are faithfully reproduced in the SuMD-derived closed state. Without a residue-level comparison of the simulated closed state to the experimental structure, the claim of "reproducing" the closed conformation is not adequately supported. Furthermore, the persistence assessment is based on a single 100 ns unbiased MD simulation, which is too short to establish stability.
  - **Why it matters** The entire methodological contribution hinges on the ability of the multistep SuMD protocol to generate a conformation that is meaningfully similar to the experimentally determined closed state. If the 9.04 Å RMSD reflects a conformation that is only superficially similar to the crystal structure, with incorrect interdomain contacts or an improperly formed RNA-binding cleft, then the claimed success is overstated and the mechanistic interpretations derived from this trajectory are unreliable.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide a detailed residue-level comparison between the SuMD-generated closed state and the crystal structure, including: (a) a per-residue RMSD plot with the secondary structure elements annotated; (b) a contact map comparison highlighting which native interdomain contacts are reproduced and which are not; (c) an analysis of the RNA-binding cleft geometry (e.g., width, depth, electrostatic potential) in the simulated closed state versus the crystal structure; and (d) a longer persistence simulation (at least 500 ns, ideally multiple replicates) demonstrating that the closed state is stable. If the simulated closed state deviates significantly from the crystal structure in functionally important regions, the claims should be revised accordingly.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness / Methodological rigor
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that "the identified pathway should be regarded as one plausible route among potentially multiple closure mechanisms" and that the per-residue interaction analysis "recovered several residues previously reported in the literature as key residues for RNA recognition."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion, "Simulations of HuR Conformational Transition" section; Figure 6; location not provided for specific figure panels
  - **Concern** The mechanistic interpretation of the reclosure pathway is derived from a single successful trajectory. The authors acknowledge this limitation in the conclusions, but the Results section presents the pathway and the residue-level interactions as if they are robust findings. The per-residue interaction energy analysis is performed on one trajectory, and no error bars, replicate analyses, or statistical measures are reported. The claim that the analysis "confirmed the prominent role of Arg97" is based on a single simulation and cannot be considered a confirmation in any meaningful sense. Additionally, the selection of the "most productive" trajectory for further analysis introduces a selection bias that is not addressed.
  - **Why it matters** The mechanistic insights into HuR domain closure are presented as a key outcome of the study. If these insights are derived from a single stochastic trajectory, they may not be reproducible or representative of the ensemble of possible closure pathways. The authors' own acknowledgment that SuMD is stochastic and that different initial velocities can lead to markedly different trajectories undermines the confidence that can be placed in the specific pathway and residue interactions described.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) demonstrate that the key mechanistic features (e.g., the role of Arg97, the sequence of interdomain contact formation) are reproducible across multiple independent SuMD runs that yield productive closure, or (b) explicitly reframe the findings as observations from a single trajectory that require experimental validation, with appropriate caveats throughout the Results section rather than only in the Conclusions. If multiple productive trajectories are available, a statistical analysis of the conserved versus variable features of the closure pathway should be provided.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance / Translational relevance
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that "the pool of the collected trajectories also revealed novel conformational states accessible to the protein, which may represent valuable structural templates for future computer-aided drug design studies and for the identification of transiently druggable conformational states."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion, "Simulations of HuR Conformational Transition" section; Figure 7; location not provided for specific figure panels
  - **Concern** The pocket detection analysis is explicitly preliminary, and the authors appropriately note that the druggability score is a geometric and physicochemical estimator, not a demonstration of genuine druggability. However, the claim that these pockets "may represent valuable structural templates" is speculative and not supported by any functional validation. The manuscript does not assess whether the identified pockets could accommodate drug-like molecules in a manner that would disrupt HuR-RNA interactions, nor does it compare these pockets to known small-molecule binding sites on HuR. The inclusion of this analysis without more rigorous validation (e.g., molecular docking of known HuR ligands, comparison to experimental binding data) weakens the translational claims.
  - **Why it matters** The potential for targeting transient conformational states of HuR is a significant motivation for this work. If the identified pockets are artifacts of the simulation protocol or are not functionally relevant, the translational value of the study is diminished. Overstating the significance of preliminary computational predictions could mislead future drug discovery efforts.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) provide additional computational validation, such as docking known HuR ligands or drug-like fragments into the identified pockets and assessing their predicted binding affinities, or (b) temper the claims about the translational value of the pockets, explicitly stating that these are preliminary observations requiring experimental validation. If the pockets are not conserved across multiple trajectories or are not stable in longer simulations, this should be reported.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity / Reproducibility
  - **Affected element** Methods, "SuMD" section
  - **Evidence pointer** Materials and Methods, "SuMD" section; location not provided
  - **Issue** The description of the SuMD supervision protocol lacks specific details on how the "distance between the centers of mass" was computed for residue pairs. It is unclear whether this refers to the distance between the Cα atoms, the side-chain centroids, or the centers of mass of the entire residues. Additionally, the criteria for accepting or rejecting a SuMD step are described only in terms of the slope of the fitted line, but the specific threshold values and the length of each SuMD step are not clearly stated.
  - **Required correction** Provide a precise definition of the distance metric used for supervision, including the atoms or groups considered. Specify the exact acceptance criteria for SuMD steps (e.g., the slope threshold) and the duration of each SuMD step in simulation time. This information is essential for reproducibility.

- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The application of SuMD to an intramolecular conformational transition is a novel methodological contribution. While the approach is an incremental extension of existing SuMD methodology, the specific application to HuR domain closure has not been previously reported. Scientific importance: The study addresses a biologically relevant question with therapeutic implications, but the significance of the findings is limited by the preliminary nature of the results and the lack of experimental validation. The mechanistic insights, while plausible, are derived from a single trajectory and require further support. Interdisciplinary readership: The manuscript is likely to appeal primarily to computational chemists and biophysicists specializing in enhanced sampling methods. The broader structural biology and drug discovery communities may find the results of interest, but the technical nature of the study limits its accessibility. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is clearly written and the authors make an effort to explain the SuMD methodology. - **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a novel methodological application with potential value for the computational biophysics community. However, the central claims require substantial additional analysis and validation before they can be fully endorsed. Specifically, the authors must provide a rigorous residue-level comparison of the SuMD-generated closed state to the crystal structure, demonstrate reproducibility of the key mechanistic findings across multiple trajectories, and either strengthen or temper the claims regarding druggable pockets. The manuscript would also benefit from a more critical discussion of what constitutes "success" in reproducing a conformational transition and the limitations of using a single trajectory for mechanistic interpretation.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the multistep SuMD protocol "succeeded in reproducing a nearly crystal-like conformation" is not fully supported by the reported RMSD value of 9.04 Å, which is high by structural biology standards, and the lack of a residue-level comparison to the crystal structure.
- The claim that the per-residue interaction analysis "confirmed" the role of Arg97, Arg136, and Arg147 is unsupported, as the analysis is based on a single trajectory and no statistical measures are provided.
- The claim that the identified pockets "may represent valuable structural templates for future computer-aided drug design studies" is speculative and not supported by any functional validation or comparison to known HuR ligand binding data.
- The statement that the closed state "persisted without undergoing a major reopening" during the 100 ns unbiased MD simulation is based on a single short simulation and cannot be considered evidence of stability.
- The claim that the identified pathway is "a plausible reclosure pathway" is reasonable as a hypothesis, but the manuscript does not provide sufficient evidence to distinguish this pathway from other possible mechanisms.
