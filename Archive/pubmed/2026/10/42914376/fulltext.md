This study investigates the effect of heat treatment (25-95  degrees C) on the interaction between beta-lactoglobulin (beta-LG) and capsaicin (CAP) through multi-spectroscopic, physicochemical, and molecular dynamics (MD) simulation approaches. We reveal a non-monotonic, temperature-dependent binding profile governed by beta-LG's multistage conformational transitions. Binding affinity increases from 25 to 65  degrees C, driven by a molten globule-like transition that exposes hydrophobic binding sites. A sharp decline at 75  degrees C, due to vigorous aggregation and site burial, marks a critical temperature threshold. Notably, partial binding recovery occurs at 85-95  degrees C, resulting from aggregate rearrangement. Beyond its role as a ligand, CAP actively modulates beta-LG aggregation by occupying hydrophobic patches, as evidenced by consistently smaller particle sizes and lower turbidity in CAP-supplemented groups. Molecular docking and MD simulations, including MM-PBSA free energy calculations and free energy landscape analysis, confirm stable binding within beta-LG's hydrophobic cavity and reveal that temperature-driven reshaping of conformational substates directly governs binding thermodynamics, with optimal affinity at 65  degrees C. These findings establish 65  degrees C as the optimal processing temperature for CAP retention, while suggesting that prolonged heating at 75  degrees C should be avoided. The strong correlations between simple physicochemical indicators (turbidity, solubility) and binding affinity offer a practical pathway for low-cost, real-time quality monitoring in industrial dairy processing.
