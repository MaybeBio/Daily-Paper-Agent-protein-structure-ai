## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no access to methods, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose TINA, a geometry-aware post-hoc scoring framework for ranking protein complex conformations generated by AlphaFold3. TINA uses atom-level interface graphs and many-body tensor modeling to capture two-body, three-body, and four-body geometric interactions. It is trained on ZDOCK decoys with joint ranking and regression objectives, fine-tuned for protein-peptide complexes, and evaluated on AF3-generated candidate pools across four benchmarks. The authors report superior or competitive top-1 success rate, enrichment factor, and top-1 DockQ, with pronounced benefits in low-confidence and antibody-related scenarios, and support this with case studies and ablation experiments.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no quantitative results, benchmark details, or methodological specifics are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full methods, benchmark definitions, decoy generation protocols, evaluation metrics, statistical significance tests, case study details, ablation results, and any figures or tables

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a plausible and potentially useful idea: a post-hoc scoring framework that explicitly models many-body geometric interactions at protein interfaces to complement AlphaFold3's internal confidence metrics. The motivation is clear and the problem is relevant. However, the abstract provides no quantitative evidence, no benchmark performance numbers, no comparison baselines beyond AF3 internal metrics, and no methodological detail sufficient to assess technical soundness. The claims of superiority and pronounced benefit in specific scenarios are therefore currently unsupported. The work may be of interest to the structural biology and protein modeling community, but the case is not established from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational structural biologists, developers of protein-protein interaction prediction and scoring methods, and researchers using AlphaFold3 for complex structure prediction who need improved model selection tools. The focus on antibody-antigen and protein-peptide complexes also targets the therapeutic antibody and peptide design communities.
- **Major strengths** The problem addressed is timely and practically important. The proposed approach of modeling many-body geometric interactions beyond pairwise contacts is conceptually novel relative to typical interface scoring functions. The evaluation strategy of using AF3-generated decoy pools is appropriate and directly relevant to the intended use case. The focus on low-confidence and antibody-related scenarios is a sensible and potentially valuable niche.
