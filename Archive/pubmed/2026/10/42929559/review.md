## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and concluding remarks. Figures, tables, and supplementary material referenced but not provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, methodological soundness, clarity of claims, and alignment with stated evidence. Assessment limited to the supplied text; figures, tables, and supplementary files were not available for direct inspection.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present gEDES-HM, a protocol that transfers conformational ensembles generated by enhanced sampling simulations of a single reference protein (E. coli adenylate kinase) to homologous family members via homology modeling using a multitemplate library. They report recovery of native-like binding modes for 92% of 26 protein-ligand complexes across 22 homologues, with performance approaching that of redocking into experimental holo structures.
- **Visible evidence base** Text descriptions of methods and results, including quantitative success rates, RMSD thresholds, correlation coefficients, and comparative performance across model sets. Specific numerical values for docking success rates and statistical correlations are reported in the text.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures, tables (including Tables S2, S4, S5, S6, S7), and supplementary figures (S1, S2, S3) are referenced but not provided. These contain the primary quantitative data supporting the claims. Without them, the accuracy of reported numbers and the validity of statistical analyses cannot be independently verified.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript describes a conceptually appealing and potentially useful extension of the authors' prior gEDES methodology, addressing a real bottleneck in structure-based modeling of protein families. The core idea, transferring conformational ensembles from a single reference to homologues via homology modeling, is logical and the validation design is thoughtful, including appropriate controls (single-template apo and holo models, and redocking into experimental structures). However, the manuscript as presented has several significant issues. The most critical is the absence of the actual data: all figures and tables are missing from the provided material, making it impossible to verify the quantitative claims. Beyond this, there are conceptual concerns about the interpretation of the results, particularly the claim that the approach "transfers functional transitions" when the validation is based on reproducing static bound structures. The discussion of limitations is honest and well-developed, which is a strength. The writing is generally clear, though some methodological details require clarification. Overall, the idea has merit, but the evidence base as presented is insufficient to fully establish the claims.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational structural biologists, particularly those working on ensemble docking, homology modeling, and structure-based drug discovery. The protocol offers a practical way to generate conformational libraries for protein families without running expensive simulations for each member, which could be valuable for virtual screening campaigns targeting multiple homologues. Researchers studying conformational transitions in enzyme families, especially those interested in adenylate kinases, would also find the results relevant. The work may also interest method developers in the enhanced sampling community, as it demonstrates a downstream application of gEDES.

- **Major strengths**
  1. The core concept is elegant and addresses a genuine practical need: reducing computational cost of generating conformational ensembles for multiple homologues.
  2. The validation design is rigorous, with multiple control sets (apo-only, holo-only templates, and experimental redocking) that allow clear attribution of performance to the ensemble transfer approach.
  3. The honest and detailed discussion of limitations, including the analysis of unsuccessful cases, is commendable and adds credibility.
  4. The use of multiple collective coordinate systems (distances and angles) to characterize the conformational space is thorough.
  5. The statistical analysis (Spearman correlations with p-values) is appropriate for the non-linear relationships being examined.

- **Major Concerns**

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence completeness
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims 92.3% success in reproducing native-like binding modes across 26 complexes, and 84.6% success in generating native-like binding-site conformations.
- **Evidence pointer** Figures 1-4, Tables S2, S4, S5, S6, S7; location not provided
- **Concern** All quantitative results are presented in figures and tables that were not included in the provided material. The text alone is insufficient to verify the accuracy of the reported percentages, the distribution of RMSD values, the cluster characteristics, or the statistical correlations. Without access to the actual data, the central claims of the paper cannot be independently assessed.
- **Why it matters** The core contribution of this work is empirical: the demonstration that gEDES-HM achieves high success rates across a diverse set of homologues. If the underlying data contain errors, outliers, or are less favorable than summarized, the conclusions would be substantially weakened. The absence of the data makes the manuscript impossible to evaluate as a scientific contribution.
- **Resolution test** Provide all figures and tables with full data. The reviewer must be able to inspect the distribution of RMSD values per complex, the cluster rankings, and the correlation plots to verify the reported summary statistics.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Claim-evidence alignment
- **Claim pointer** The title and abstract state that the method enables "mapping functional transitions across protein homologues from a single simulation."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Introduction, Results and Discussion; location not provided
- **Concern** The validation presented in the manuscript demonstrates that gEDES-HM can generate conformations resembling bound states, as measured by RMSD to experimental holo structures. However, this is not equivalent to mapping functional transitions. A transition implies a dynamical process or at least a characterization of the pathway between states. The manuscript does not present any analysis of transition pathways, intermediate states along a defined reaction coordinate, or kinetic/thermodynamic information about the interconversion between open and closed states. The claim of "mapping functional transitions" is therefore overstated relative to what is actually demonstrated.
- **Why it matters** The title and framing of the work promise more than the validation delivers. If the contribution is about generating binding-competent conformations for docking, that is a valuable but more modest claim. Overstating the scope could mislead readers about the method's capabilities and may invite inappropriate applications.
- **Resolution test** Either revise the title and abstract to accurately reflect that the method generates conformational libraries that include bound-like states, or add analyses that explicitly characterize transitions (e.g., transition paths, free energy profiles, or dynamical information) between the open and closed states in the transferred ensembles.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Methodological clarity
- **Claim pointer** The methods state that 101 conformational clusters from gEDES simulations were used as templates for homology modeling, and that 10 models per template were generated, yielding 1010 models per target, from which the best-scoring model per template was selected by DOPE score.
- **Evidence pointer** Methods, Homology Modeling, Model Generation; location not provided
- **Concern** The selection of the "best-scoring model" by DOPE score for each of the 101 conformations is a critical step, but the manuscript does not discuss the variability of DOPE scores across the 10 models generated per template. If the DOPE score varies substantially between models generated from the same template, the selection of a single model may introduce stochastic bias. Conversely, if the scores are tightly clustered, the selection is robust but the generation of 10 models is wasteful. The manuscript does not address this.
- **Why it matters** The reproducibility and robustness of the model selection step directly affect the reliability of the downstream docking results. If the selection is noisy, the reported success rates may not be reproducible across independent runs. This is particularly important given the authors' own acknowledgment that success depends on a small number of productive conformers surviving the selection process.
- **Resolution test** Provide an analysis of DOPE score distributions across the 10 models per template for a representative subset of targets. If variability is high, discuss the implications for reproducibility. Alternatively, demonstrate that the docking results are insensitive to the specific model selected per template.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Generalizability
- **Claim pointer** The abstract and conclusion suggest that gEDES-HM "could provide a practical route to generate binding-competent structural libraries for homologues lacking experimental structures."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Concluding Remarks; location not provided
- **Concern** The validation is performed exclusively on adenylate kinases, a family with a well-characterized, conserved domain motion. The authors acknowledge this limitation in the discussion, but the framing in the abstract and conclusion is more general. The key question is whether the approach would work for families with different types of conformational changes, such as loop rearrangements, domain rotations, or allosteric transitions that do not involve a simple open-to-closed motion. The manuscript provides no evidence or argument for why the approach would generalize.
- **Why it matters** The practical value of the method depends on its applicability beyond the test case. If the approach only works for families with large, well-defined domain motions similar to ADK, its utility is more limited than suggested. The authors should either temper the generalizability claims or provide a reasoned argument, based on the mechanism of the method, for why it should work more broadly.
- **Resolution test** Add a discussion of the types of conformational changes for which the approach is likely to be applicable, based on the nature of the gEDES bias and the homology modeling transfer. If possible, include a preliminary test on a family with a different type of conformational change.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of "mapping functional transitions" (title, abstract) is not supported by the presented evidence, which demonstrates generation of bound-like conformations rather than characterization of transitions.
- The generalizability claim that gEDES-HM "could provide a practical route" for families beyond ADK is speculative, as validation is limited to a single family with a conserved domain motion.
- The reported success rates (92.3% for docking, 84.6% for binding-site generation) cannot be verified without access to the figures and tables containing the underlying data.
- The statement that "the accuracy obtained in docking calculations using HMgEDES is marginally higher than that recorded at the homology modeling stage" is presented as a meaningful result, but the difference (84.6% vs 92.3%) is based on a small number of cases (2 additional targets recovered) and may not be statistically robust.
- The claim that "every target for which a sub-2.5 Å binding-site model was available also produced a native-like pose" is presented as a positive result, but it also implies that the docking step adds no value beyond the modeling step for these cases, which is not discussed.
- The assertion that "failures cannot be attributed clearly to the use of transferred ensembles only" is based on the observation that the same systems fail with experimental structures, but the sample size (2 systems) is too small to draw strong conclusions.
