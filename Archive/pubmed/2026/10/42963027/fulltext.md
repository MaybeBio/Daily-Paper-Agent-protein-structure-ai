Protein interaction sites must maintain structural integrity while accommodating conformational fluctuations and sequence perturbations. Molecular determinants that distinguish locally adaptable residues from structurally constrained positions within conserved interfaces remain incompletely understood. Here, we investigate this problem using the prefusion respiratory syncytial virus fusion glycoprotein F as a model system, focusing on a conserved interaction hotspot proximal to the MEDI8897-binding interface. An integrated computational framework combining evolutionary conservation, structure-based energetic analysis, residue interaction networks, and molecular dynamics simulations was used to characterize mutation-dependent structural responses. Analysis of 1848 RSV F sequences revealed strong conservation of the CPINKC hotspot comprising C69, P205, I206, N208, K209, and C212. Despite conservation, saturation mutagenesis and structural analysis revealed pronounced heterogeneity in calculated energetic response to substitution. P205R and N208R produced highly unfavorable calculated energetic effects, whereas I206R exhibited an approximately neutral energetic response. Residue interaction network analysis identified I206 as topologically central residue with the highest betweenness and closeness centrality within the hotspot. Aggregate MD sampling comprised three independent 500-ns simulations for each of seven systems, yielding 10.5 mus of total simulation time. All systems retained the overall prefusion architecture over the simulated timescale but exhibited distinct local and interprotomer responses. N208R produced the strongest local perturbation, including increased hotspot flexibility and reduced interprotomer contact occupancy. In contrast, P205R maintained restrained local dynamics despite its energetic penalty, whereas I206R preserved a broadly wild-type-like dynamic profile. These findings indicate that energetic cost, residue interaction topology, and conformational dynamics provide complementary information for characterizing mutation-induced structural adaptation.
