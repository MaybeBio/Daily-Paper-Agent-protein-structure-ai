Ultrasound pretreatment is increasingly used to modulate protein conformation and facilitate the release of bioactive peptides for functional food applications. In this study, large yellow croaker (Larimichthys crocea) bone proteins were subjected to ultrasound pretreatment to investigate the relationship among ultrasound-induced protein conformational changes, enzymatic hydrolysis behavior, and the osteogenic activity of the resulting hydrolysates. Ultrasonic treatment at 300 W significantly altered protein conformation, increased the accessibility of neutral protease, and consequently enhanced both the degree of hydrolysis and the proportion of low-molecular-weight peptides. Hydrolysates produced under this condition exhibited the highest osteogenic activity, as evidenced by increased MC3T3-E1 cell proliferation, alkaline phosphatase activity, and mineralized nodule formation. LC-MS/MS analysis combined with molecular docking-based virtual screening proposed three proline-rich peptides, GPPGPMGPPG, GPSGPVGP, and APDPGPGP, as candidate osteogenic peptides. Refined molecular docking predicted favorable interactions of all three peptides with the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR). The 100-ns molecular dynamics simulation further supported the stability of the representative GPPGPMGPPG-EGFR complex. Altogether, ultrasound pretreatment facilitates the generation of low-molecular-weight peptide-rich hydrolysates with enhanced osteogenic potential from fish bone proteins. These findings provide a feasible strategy for valorizing marine by-products into functional food ingredients for bone health management.
