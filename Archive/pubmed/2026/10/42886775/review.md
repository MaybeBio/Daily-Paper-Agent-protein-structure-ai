### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that x-ray crystallography and molecular dynamics simulations elucidate the structure and dynamics of scALAS upon substrate binding, and that critical conformational changes occur during catalysis.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no structural or simulation details provided  
- **Concern** The abstract provides no information on the resolution of the crystal structures, the number of structures determined, the ligands or substrate analogs used, the simulation length, force field parameters, or convergence criteria. Without these details, the reliability of the structural and dynamic conclusions cannot be evaluated.  
- **Why it matters** Structural and simulation claims are central to the paper's contribution. If the crystallographic data are low resolution or the simulations are insufficiently sampled, the identified conformational changes may be artifacts rather than biologically relevant states.  
- **Resolution test** Provide crystallographic statistics including resolution, R-factors, and validation metrics, and describe simulation protocols with evidence of convergence and reproducibility.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Support for claims  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the identified conformational changes regulate enzyme activity and protein-protein interactions specific to eukaryotic heme synthesis.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no functional or interaction data described  
- **Concern** The abstract does not describe any experiments that directly test the functional consequences of the observed conformational changes, such as mutagenesis coupled with activity assays or binding studies. The claim that these changes regulate activity and protein-protein interactions is therefore unsupported by the evidence presented.  
- **Why it matters** The regulatory claim is a key part of the paper's significance. Without direct functional validation, the conformational changes remain descriptive and their biological relevance is speculative.  
- **Resolution test** Include mutagenesis or biochemical assays that link specific conformational states to changes in enzyme activity or interaction partners, with appropriate controls and statistical analysis.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Support for claims  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that evolution-guided engineering generated scALAS variants with altered catalytic activity and cellular metabolic profiles.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no variant details or assay results provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that variants were generated and characterized but provides no information on the engineering strategy, the number of variants, the magnitude or direction of activity changes, or the metabolic phenotypes observed. The claim of altered catalytic activity and metabolic profiles is therefore not verifiable.  
- **Why it matters** The engineered variants are presented as a key outcome with potential therapeutic relevance. Without quantitative data, the significance and reproducibility of this result cannot be assessed.  
- **Resolution test** Provide detailed descriptions of the engineering approach, the specific mutations introduced, and quantitative data on enzyme kinetics and metabolic profiling with appropriate statistical comparisons.
