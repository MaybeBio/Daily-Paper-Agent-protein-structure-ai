## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract; no methods, figures, or statistical details were available for evaluation
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that TRPV1 activators stabilize functionally distinct open states with discrete single-channel conductance levels, that these states arise from distinct open-pore conformations supported by anomalous mole fraction effect measurements and site-directed fluorescence recordings, that Rosetta-guided structural models are consistent with these observations, and that coactivation by two stimuli slows channel opening, suggesting conformational incompatibility across subunits. The authors conclude that TRPV1 gating is a stimulus-partitioned, multistate process with implications for selective modulation in pain management.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials were provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, methods, statistical analyses, raw electrophysiology and fluorescence data, structural model details, and any control experiments

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially significant revision of the prevailing model of TRPV1 activation, moving from a single activated state toward stimulus-specific open states. The combination of single-channel conductance measurements, anomalous mole fraction effects, site-directed fluorescence, and Rosetta-guided modeling is intellectually compelling and would be of broad interest to the ion channel and pain research communities. However, the abstract alone does not provide sufficient methodological detail, quantitative support, or control information to assess the robustness of the claims. The central conclusions rest on several inferences that require careful experimental validation, particularly the link between conductance levels and discrete pore conformations, and the interpretation of slowed coactivation kinetics. The work is promising but not fully established from the provided material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Ion channel biophysicists studying gating mechanisms, structural biologists interested in conformational dynamics of TRP channels, pharmacologists and drug developers targeting TRPV1 for pain management, and neuroscientists studying nociceptor signaling. The claim of stimulus-partitioned open states would challenge current assumptions about TRPV1 activation and could inform selective modulation strategies.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a fundamental and unresolved question in TRPV1 biology. The use of multiple orthogonal approaches, including electrophysiology, fluorescence, and computational modeling, is a notable strength. The observation of discrete conductance levels and the anomalous mole fraction effect provides functional evidence for distinct open states. The coactivation experiment is a clever design that may reveal subunit-level conformational interactions. The potential therapeutic implication for selective TRPV1 modulation is clinically relevant.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **R1-M1** (Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness, Claim pointer: "TRPV1 activators stabilize functionally distinct open states that yield discrete single-channel conductance levels", Evidence pointer: "location not provided"): The abstract does not specify which activators were tested, how many conductance levels were observed, or whether these levels were statistically distinguishable across experiments. Discrete conductance levels could arise from subconductance states that are not necessarily linked to distinct open-pore conformations. Without details on the number of independent recordings, the amplitude resolution, and the criteria used to classify conductance levels, the claim of functionally distinct open states is not fully supported.
  - **R1-M2** (Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness, Claim pointer: "These functional states appear to arise from distinct open-pore conformations, as evidenced by anomalous mole fraction effect measurements", Evidence pointer: "location not provided"): The anomalous mole fraction effect is a valuable tool, but its interpretation requires careful control of ion species and concentrations. The abstract does not describe the ion conditions, the magnitude of the effect, or whether the effect was observed for all activators or only a subset. Without these details, the link between the mole fraction effect and distinct pore conformations is suggestive but not conclusive.
  - **R1-M3** (Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness, Claim pointer: "Structurally, stimulus-specific changes in pore conformation were directly observed using site-directed fluorescence recordings, consistent with Rosetta-guided structural models", Evidence pointer: "location not provided"): The abstract claims "direct observation" of conformational changes, but site-directed fluorescence reports on local environmental changes, not direct structural coordinates. The consistency with Rosetta-guided models is mentioned without any description of the model quality, the number of models generated, or the criteria for consistency. The abstract does not indicate whether the fluorescence changes were calibrated against known structural states or whether the models were validated by independent methods.
  - **R1-M4** (Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness, Claim pointer: "coactivation by two stimuli markedly slows channel opening rather than producing synergistic acceleration, suggesting potential conformational incompatibility across subunits", Evidence pointer: "location not provided"): The interpretation of slowed opening kinetics as evidence for conformational incompatibility is one possible explanation, but alternative mechanisms, such as altered ligand binding kinetics, desensitization, or channel block, are not addressed. The abstract does not specify which two stimuli were used, the concentration ranges, or whether the slowing was observed across multiple coactivation pairs. Without these details, the conclusion is speculative.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that TRPV1 activators stabilize functionally distinct open states is not fully supported without details on the number and statistical separation of conductance levels.
- The claim that distinct open states arise from distinct open-pore conformations is inferred from the anomalous mole fraction effect but lacks the necessary experimental context.
- The claim of "direct observation" of conformational changes via site-directed fluorescence is overstated, as this method reports on local environmental changes rather than direct structural coordinates.
- The interpretation of slowed coactivation kinetics as evidence for conformational incompatibility is speculative without ruling out alternative mechanisms.
- The therapeutic implication for selective modulation of TRPV1 is not substantiated by the evidence described in the abstract.
