## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, and supplementary figure/table references.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, methodological soundness, robustness of claims, and clarity of presentation. No assessment of editorial fit or journal-specific policy.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present PrOMet, a two-stage structure-based deep learning framework for predicting metal-binding sites and metal types in protein structures. The method combines a Random Forest pruning stage with an Equivariant Graph Neural Network (EGNN) optimization stage, trained on a broad dataset of protein-metal complexes from BioLiP. The authors claim state-of-the-art performance in metal localization and type classification, robustness to non-crystallographic structures (cryo-EM and AlphaFold models), and superiority over AlphaFold3 in dedicated metal-site prediction tasks.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures (Figures 1-5), Tables 1, supplementary figures and tables referenced (Figures S1-S23, Tables S1-S7), and detailed methods descriptions.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Actual figure images and table data are not provided; only figure/table captions and in-text descriptions are available. Supplementary figures and tables are referenced but not included. No quantitative performance values for baseline comparisons are given in the text beyond a few specific numbers. The GitHub repository link is provided but not assessed.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a well-motivated and technically sound approach to a challenging problem in structural bioinformatics. The two-stage architecture is a sensible design choice, and the inclusion of diverse experimental structures and predicted models in evaluation is commendable. However, the evidence provided in the text is insufficient to fully validate the claimed performance advantages, particularly against AlphaFold3 and other baselines. Several key quantitative results are only described qualitatively or with single summary statistics, and the robustness analysis on cryo-EM and AlphaFold models lacks sufficient detail. The work has clear potential, but the current manuscript does not fully establish the case for its claimed superiority.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists, computational biochemists, and researchers in metalloprotein engineering would find this work relevant. The method addresses a practical need for accurate metal-site annotation in increasingly common medium-resolution cryo-EM structures and AlphaFold-predicted models. The comparative analysis with AlphaFold3 is timely and of broad interest to the protein structure prediction community.
- **Major strengths** 1. The two-stage prune-and-optimize architecture is a well-reasoned approach to balance computational efficiency and prediction accuracy. 2. The dataset construction, including biological-site filtering and handling of metal heterogeneity, is thoughtful and addresses known pitfalls in the field. 3. The evaluation on non-crystallographic structures (cryo-EM, AF3) is a valuable contribution, as most existing methods are benchmarked only on high-resolution X-ray structures. 4. The comparison with AlphaFold3, including runtime analysis, provides practical guidance for users.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **R1-M1** (Blocking: Yes) The central claim of state-of-the-art performance is not fully supported by the quantitative evidence presented in the text. While PR-AUC values are given for the overall test set, per-metal-type breakdowns, confidence intervals, and statistical significance tests are not reported. The comparison with Metal3D and M-Ionic relies on a single summary metric, which may obscure important differences in performance across metal types or structural contexts.
  - **R1-M2** (Blocking: Yes) The robustness analysis on cryo-EM and AF3 structures is described qualitatively. The text states that performance "showed only a small degradation" and that Metal3D "dropped more sharply," but no numerical results are provided for these subsets. Without quantitative data, the claim of robustness cannot be adequately assessed.
  - **R1-M3** (Blocking: No) The comparison with AlphaFold3, while interesting, is limited to a small positive set (82 cases) and a negative set (316 cases). The text reports a top-1 site-and-type accuracy of 45% for PrOMet versus 23% for AF3, but the selection criteria for these sets, the potential for bias, and the statistical significance of the difference are not discussed. The runtime comparison, while useful, is based on a single example and may not be representative.
  - **R1-M4** (Blocking: No) The ablation study identifies the two-stage design and directional features as important contributors, but the analysis is incomplete. For example, the text does not report the performance of the EGNN without pruning when trained on the full (unfiltered) dataset, which would isolate the effect of the pruning stage from the training data distribution. The interaction between dataset composition and architecture choice is not fully explored.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - **R1-m1** The abstract states that PrOMet achieves a PR-AUC of 0.739 for localization and an average F1 of 0.743 for type classification. It would be helpful to also report the range of F1 scores across metal types, as the average may mask poor performance on rare metals.
  - **R1-m2** The description of the directional feature (polarity vector and angle θ) is clear, but the physical interpretation could be expanded. The text mentions "lone-pair electrons" but does not explain how the polarity vector is computed from atomic properties. A brief clarification in the methods would improve reproducibility.
  - **R1-m3** The negative set for the AF3 comparison consists of proteins with no annotated metals. It would be informative to also test on proteins with metals that are not among the seven modeled types, to assess whether PrOMet can correctly reject non-target metals.
  - **R1-m4** The manuscript mentions that the dataset includes "all ligands and nucleic acids except waters." It would be useful to specify how ligand-metal interactions are handled in the feature computation, as this could affect performance on protein-ligand interfaces.
  - **R1-m5** The inference time is reported for a single medium-sized protein. A more systematic analysis across protein sizes would be helpful for users planning large-scale applications.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 and R1-M2 are the primary technical concerns. The lack of quantitative results for the cryo-EM and AF3 subsets, and the absence of statistical rigor in the main benchmark, prevent a full assessment of the method's claimed advantages.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The two-stage architecture is a novel combination of existing techniques, and the focus on robustness to non-ideal structures is a meaningful contribution. However, the individual components (RF, EGNN) are well-established, and the novelty lies primarily in their integration and application.
  - Scientific importance: The problem of metal-site prediction is of significant practical importance, and the potential to apply the method to cryo-EM and predicted structures broadens its utility. The comparative analysis with AlphaFold3 addresses a question of current interest.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work is primarily of interest to computational structural biologists and bioinformaticians. The methods and results are presented in a way that is accessible to this audience, but the broader biological significance could be emphasized more.
  - Technical soundness: The methodology is generally sound, but the evidence presented is insufficient to fully validate the claims. The lack of quantitative detail in key comparisons is a significant weakness.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is well-written and the concepts are explained clearly. The abstract and introduction are accessible, but the results section assumes familiarity with machine learning and structural biology terminology.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core idea is promising and the methodology is well-motivated, but the current manuscript does not provide sufficient quantitative evidence to establish the claimed performance advantages. The authors should provide detailed results for the robustness analyses, add statistical measures to the main benchmark, and expand the ablation study to fully support their conclusions.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that PrOMet "outperforms existing methods" is not fully supported, as only a single summary metric (PR-AUC) is reported for the main comparison, and no statistical significance testing is described.
- The claim of robustness to cryo-EM and AF3 structures is unsupported by quantitative data in the text; only qualitative descriptions are provided.
- The claim that PrOMet is "among the first" structure-based deep learning methods to accept ligand-bound inputs is plausible but not verified against a comprehensive literature search.
- The statement that the two-stage design is the "dominating factor" for success is based on a single ablation experiment and may be an overstatement, as the interaction with dataset composition is not fully explored.
- The generalizability of the method to unseen metal types or non-standard coordination environments is not assessed.
