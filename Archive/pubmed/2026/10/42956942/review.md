## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a carefully hedged and intellectually honest framing of a speculative interdisciplinary topic. The authors explicitly avoid overclaiming, which is commendable. However, the abstract is largely programmatic, and the visible evidence base is insufficient to evaluate whether the proposed framework is novel, rigorous, or practically actionable. The scientific case rests on future possibilities rather than demonstrated results, which limits the current impact.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Evolutionary immunologists studying gene family diversification, biophysicists interested in quantum effects in biological systems, and researchers working on fish immunity and host-pathogen coevolution. The proposed roadmap may interest method developers in structural biology and computational chemistry.
- **Major strengths** The abstract demonstrates commendable scientific restraint by explicitly distinguishing between established evidence, indirect evidence, and hypotheses. The five-level evidence hierarchy is a useful organizing principle. The proposal of a specific chemically defined candidate (NADPH-to-flavin hydride transfer) grounds the speculation in a testable system.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** Yes  
    **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
    **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that increasing genomic, structural, transcriptomic, single-cell, and functional-genomic resolution provides a tractable framework for testing quantum-biological possibilities in teleost immunity.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract, general statement; location not provided  
    **Concern** The abstract asserts that current technological resolution is sufficient to test the proposed hypotheses, but no specific data, methods, or examples are provided to substantiate this claim. The link between available technologies and the feasibility of testing quantum effects in teleost PRRs is asserted rather than demonstrated.  
    **Why it matters** The central premise of the review is that the proposed research direction is tractable. Without evidence that the necessary structural, dynamic, and quantum-chemical methods can be meaningfully applied to teleost PRR systems, the roadmap remains speculative.  
    **Resolution test** Provide specific examples from the literature or preliminary data showing that structure prediction, molecular dynamics, or QM/MM methods have been successfully applied to teleost PRR paralogs or closely related systems.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** Yes  
    **Axis** Novelty and added value  
    **Claim pointer** The abstract proposes a falsifiable research roadmap linking receptor evolution to structure-function relationships, signaling diversity, redox processes, and candidate quantum-sensitive molecular events.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract, final sentence; location not provided  
    **Concern** The abstract does not explain how this roadmap differs from existing frameworks in evolutionary immunology or biophysics. The novelty of linking PRR evolution to quantum-sensitive events is unclear, and the abstract does not identify gaps in prior reviews or models that this roadmap uniquely addresses.  
    **Why it matters** For a review to be impactful, it must offer a new synthesis or perspective. If similar roadmaps or calls for interdisciplinary investigation already exist, the added value of this manuscript is diminished.  
    **Resolution test** In the full text, explicitly compare the proposed roadmap with prior frameworks and articulate the specific conceptual or methodological advances it offers.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that current technological resolution provides a tractable framework for testing quantum-biological possibilities is unsupported in the abstract, as no specific evidence or examples are provided.
- The assertion that the proposed roadmap is falsifiable is not substantiated with specific predictions or experimental designs in the abstract.
- The relevance of hydrogen tunneling and radical-pair chemistry to teleost immunity is acknowledged as a "bounded mechanistic precedent," but the abstract does not provide sufficient detail to evaluate the strength of this precedent.
- The overall novelty of the review relative to existing literature cannot be assessed from the abstract alone.
