## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, methods, results, discussion, data availability) as provided. Figures, tables, and supplementary materials referenced but not included.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific and technical evaluation of the described methodology, its validation, and the claims made. No assessment of code functionality beyond what is described.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present MultimerMapper, a software pipeline for analyzing ensembles of AlphaFold protein structure predictions of multimeric complexes. The tool systematically explores stoichiometric space, detects protein-protein interactions, identifies context-dependent conformational changes, infers stoichiometries, and provides interactive visualizations. Validation is claimed on trypanosomatid systems and on external benchmarks for stoichiometry inference, conformational change prediction, and PPI dynamics classification.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text methods and results descriptions, including benchmark performance numbers (stoichiometry 60%/80%, conformational changes 6/7, PPI dynamics 8/12), a worked example (MRGBP/NuA4, Hsp90 system), and methodological details for all core modules.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures, tables, and supplementary materials (including benchmark datasets, detailed results, videos, and parameter sensitivity analyses) are referenced but not provided. This substantially limits the ability to verify the described results and assess the robustness of the methodology.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript describes a timely and potentially useful software tool for interpreting AlphaFold predictions of protein complexes. The core concept of "context-dependent structure prediction analysis" is interesting and the systematic exploration of stoichiometric space is a reasonable approach. However, the manuscript as presented has significant limitations. The validation is described at a high level with key details relegated to inaccessible supplementary materials. Several methodological choices require justification, and the statistical framework for the association analyses is acknowledged by the authors themselves to be exploratory. The distinction between this work and existing tools, and the specific advantages offered, could be articulated more clearly. The manuscript would benefit from a more critical discussion of failure cases and limitations. Overall, the idea has merit, but the evidence provided in the main text is insufficient to fully establish the tool's utility and reliability.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in structural bioinformatics and computational biology who use AlphaFold or similar tools for predicting protein complexes, particularly those studying large, dynamic, or poorly characterized assemblies. The tool's focus on stoichiometry inference and context-dependent conformational changes addresses a real gap in the field. Biologists studying multi-protein complexes, especially in non-model organisms, could also find the tool useful for generating hypotheses about assembly pathways and interaction dynamics.
- **Major strengths**
  - Addresses a relevant and timely problem: interpreting the output of deep learning structure prediction tools for multi-protein complexes.
  - The concept of context-dependent analysis is novel and goes beyond static structure prediction.
  - The software appears to be user-friendly, with interactive visualizations and minimal prior computational expertise required.
  - The authors provide open-source code and a stable version on Zenodo, which is commendable for reproducibility.
  - The validation strategy includes both the development system (trypanosomatids) and external benchmarks, which is a strength.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Validation rigor): The central validation results are presented without sufficient detail in the main text. The benchmark for stoichiometry inference (20 complexes, 60% unambiguous, 80% stable) is described in a single sentence, with all details in supplementary materials. Similarly, the conformational change and PPI dynamics benchmarks are summarized without specifics on the systems, the definition of "correct," or the comparison to baselines. Without this information, the reader cannot assess the significance or robustness of these results.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Methodological justification): The choice of cutoffs (e.g., miPAE ≤ 9.7 Å for AF2, ≤ 7.5 Å for AF3, Nmodels = 4, Ccutoff = 5, FMC ≥ 0.2) is presented as optimized, but the optimization procedure is not described in the main text. The sensitivity of the results to these parameters is not discussed. This is critical for a tool that aims to be widely applicable.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Statistical validity): The authors acknowledge that the φ and rpb statistics are exploratory due to non-independence of observations (multiple models per prediction). This is a significant caveat that limits the strength of the conclusions that can be drawn from these analyses. The manuscript would be strengthened by a more thorough discussion of this limitation and potential solutions.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Comparative analysis): The manuscript does not provide a clear comparison of MultimerMapper to existing tools or approaches. For example, how does the stoichiometry inference compare to other methods? What is the added value of the context-dependent analysis compared to simply running AlphaFold on different combinations? A more explicit comparison would help establish the tool's niche.
  - **R1-M5** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Failure analysis): The discussion of failure cases is brief. For the PPI dynamics benchmark, 4/12 systems failed, and the authors mention a tendency to overpredict negative dynamics. A more detailed analysis of these failures would be valuable for users to understand the tool's limitations.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity): The manuscript would benefit from a figure in the main text that provides a clear schematic overview of the MultimerMapper pipeline, from input sequences to final outputs.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Reproducibility): The description of the "modified Discoba database" is vague. More details on the modification and its impact on MSA quality would be helpful.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Terminology): The term "predicted dynamic interactions" is used, but the distinction between this and simply "dynamic interactions" observed in experiments could be clarified.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Presentation): The results section for the MRGBP example is detailed but would be more compelling with a quantitative comparison to the experimental data, rather than a qualitative description.
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Accessibility): The authors state the tool is for users without extensive experience, but the manuscript assumes familiarity with concepts like miPAE, pDockQ, and PAE matrices. A brief primer on these metrics would improve accessibility.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
  - R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3 as described above. The lack of detailed validation data and parameter sensitivity analysis in the main text prevents a full assessment of the tool's reliability.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality**: The concept of context-dependent analysis of structure predictions is a novel contribution. The systematic exploration of stoichiometric space guided by stability is a reasonable and original approach. However, the individual components (e.g., PPI detection, clustering) are not new, and the novelty lies in their integration.
  - **Scientific importance**: The problem addressed is important. As AlphaFold and similar tools become more widely used, there is a growing need for methods to interpret their output for complex systems. The tool could be valuable for hypothesis generation in many biological contexts.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The tool is likely to be of interest to a broad audience in structural biology, bioinformatics, and molecular biology. However, the manuscript's readability for non-specialists is limited by the heavy use of technical jargon without sufficient explanation.
  - **Technical soundness**: The described methods are generally sound, but the validation is incomplete from the main text. The statistical caveats are acknowledged but not fully addressed. The lack of parameter sensitivity analysis is a concern.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The manuscript is written for a specialist audience. The abstract is clear, but the methods and results sections would be challenging for a non-expert to follow.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core idea is promising and the tool could be a valuable resource. However, the manuscript in its current form does not provide sufficient evidence to fully establish the tool's utility and reliability. The authors must provide the detailed validation data, parameter sensitivity analyses, and a more thorough discussion of limitations and comparisons to existing methods.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that MultimerMapper "infers stoichiometries" is not fully supported by the main text, as the benchmark details are not provided.
- The claim that the tool "identifies alternative interaction modes and conformational changes statistically associated with the presence or absence of specific protein partners" is weakened by the acknowledged limitations of the statistical framework.
- The generalizability of the tool beyond the tested systems is not established, given the limited detail on the benchmarks.
- The statement that the tool "offers a new perspective on the dynamic nature of predicted protein complex structures" is more of an assertion than a demonstrated result, as the biological insights derived from the tool are not deeply explored in the main text.
