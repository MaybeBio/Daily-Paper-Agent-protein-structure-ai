### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that D2-loop closure creates a cryptic subpocket, termed Hotspot-3, through hydrophobic packing reinforced by hydrogen bonds, and that this conformational heterogeneity complicates structure-based inhibitor design.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The existence and functional relevance of Hotspot-3 are stated as findings, but no structural, computational, or experimental evidence is presented in the abstract. It is unclear whether this is a novel observation from the authors' own work or a synthesis of prior literature. The mechanistic description of loop closure and subpocket formation is presented with confidence that the visible evidence does not support.  
- **Why it matters** The central premise of the perspective rests on the validity of Hotspot-3 as a defined and druggable feature. Without evidence, the claim is unverifiable and the subsequent arguments about inhibitor design lose their foundation.  
- **Resolution test** Provide structural data (e.g., crystal structures, MD simulations) showing the open and closed states of the D2 loop, quantitative characterization of the subpocket (e.g., volume, hydrophobicity, hydrogen-bond occupancy), and evidence that Hotspot-3 is distinct from previously described binding sites.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Claim-data alignment  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that residence-time optimization, rather than affinity alone, is a key objective for next-generation PfAMA1 inhibitors.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** This claim is presented as a conclusion, but the abstract provides no kinetic data, binding assays, or comparative analysis of residence time versus affinity for any existing or proposed inhibitors. It is unclear whether this is a hypothesis, a recommendation, or a finding from the authors' own studies.  
- **Why it matters** The recommendation to prioritize residence time is actionable only if supported by data showing that residence time correlates with efficacy in relevant models. Without such evidence, the claim is speculative and could misdirect future drug design efforts.  
- **Resolution test** Present kinetic binding data (e.g., SPR, ITC) for representative inhibitors, correlate residence time with functional outcomes (e.g., invasion inhibition), and compare with affinity-based metrics to justify the prioritization.
