A Time-Dependent Diffusion MRI Framework for Clinical Characterisation of Human Brain Cellular Architecture

Leibovici A; Soler EE; Mesika D; Tsarfaty G; Livny A; De Santis S; Eggl MF
medRxiv 预印本 · 2026-09-05 · 全文(europepmc) · 原文

该文开发基于扩散MRI的脑组织微结构成像框架，不涉及蛋白质结构计算，与课题无关。

摘要

<h4>ABSTRACT</h4> Diffusion-weighted MRI, beyond the commonly used diffusion tensor framework, offers a unique window into tissue microstructure in vivo , yet its clinical adoption has remained limited. Major barriers include the complexity of diffusion MRI sequence design, lengthy acquisition protocols, and the challenges associated with robust estimation of high-dimensional microstructural model parameters. Here, we address these limitations by combining optimised diffusion encoding with state-of-the-art simulation-based inference, establishing a clinically feasible framework for multi-compartment diffusion modelling. We validate the approach through i) in-depth in silico experiments and ii) in vivo studies made up of both human and rodent data. The resulting microstructural metrics are robust, reproducible across healthy individuals and show significant spatial associations with brain-wide expression patterns of cell-specific genes. Requiring less than 10 minutes of acquisition time, this framework substantially lowers the barriers to advanced microstructural imaging, a prerequisite step toward its eventual evaluation for the diagnosis, stratification, and monitoring of brain disorders.

摘要翻译

<h4>摘要</h4> 扩散加权MRI（diffusion-weighted MRI），超越了常用的扩散张量框架（diffusion tensor framework），为体内组织微结构（tissue microstructure in vivo）提供了独特的窗口，但其临床采用仍然有限。主要障碍包括扩散MRI序列设计的复杂性、冗长的采集协议，以及高维微结构模型参数稳健估计所面临的挑战。在此，我们通过将优化的扩散编码（optimised diffusion encoding）与最先进的基于模拟的推断（simulation-based inference）相结合，建立了一个临床可行的多室扩散建模（multi-compartment diffusion modelling）框架，从而解决了这些限制。我们通过i) 深入的在硅实验（in silico experiments）和ii) 包含人类和啮齿动物数据的在体研究（in vivo studies）来验证该方法。所得的微结构指标（microstructural metrics）稳健、在健康个体间可重复，并与细胞特异性基因（cell-specific genes）的全脑表达模式显示出显著的空间关联。该框架所需采集时间少于10分钟，显著降低了先进微结构成像的障碍，这是其最终评估用于脑部疾病诊断、分层和监测的先决步骤。
