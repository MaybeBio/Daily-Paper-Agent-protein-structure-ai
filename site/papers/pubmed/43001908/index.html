Mutations in the nsP1 dodecamerization surface impair Semliki Forest virus RNA synthesis in mosquito cells.

Xin, Qilin; Kavil, Siji; Konno, Melis N; Anderson, Michelle A E; Alphey, Luke
Journal of virology · 2026-11-09 · 仅摘要 · 原文

该文用结构建模和分子动力学模拟设计nsP1十二聚体界面突变，结合细胞实验验证其对病毒RNA复制的影响，虽以病毒蛋白为对象，但结构计算与MD方法可迁移至蛋白质结构计算研究。

摘要

Positive-sense RNA (+ssRNA) viruses remodel host membranes to form replication organelles (ROs), a process largely driven by the oligomerization of virus-encoded membrane-binding proteins. In the case of Togaviruses, non-structural protein 1 (nsP1) assembles into dodecameric rings that serve as scaffolds bridging viral replication spherules with the host plasma membrane. However, the precise role and necessity of nsP1 oligomerization in viral replication remain incompletely understood. In this study, we employed structure-based computational modeling to design nsP1 mutants predicted to disrupt monomer-monomer interactions within the dodecamer. Molecular dynamics simulations further indicated that these mutations may impair the membrane-binding affinity of nsP1. Western blotting was performed under denatured reducing conditions, and confocal microscopy was used to assess the expression levels and subcellular localization of mutant nsP1, respectively. Mutant proteins appear to not be stably retained at the plasma membrane, instead being mislocalized to endosomal compartments. Three-component trans-replicase assays and viral challenge experiments revealed that nsP1 dodecamerization-surface mutants are deficient in their ability to support viral replication. This study highlights the critical role of nsP1 dodecamerization in Togavirus replication and proposes a potential antiviral strategy targeting oligomeric protein structures in +RNA viruses. IMPORTANCE: Togaviruses are emerging arboviruses capable of causing severe disease and sporadic outbreaks. During viral replication, viral nsP1 assembles into dodecameric rings that anchor replication organelles called spherules to host membranes; however, the functional necessity of the oligomeric organization remains poorly defined. In this study, we combined structure-based modeling, molecular dynamics simulations, and cell-based assays to investigate how mutations that are predicted to destabilize nsP1 oligomers affect viral replication. We found that these mutations reduced membrane-binding affinity, altered subcellular localization, and nsP1 harboring these mutations failed to support viral RNA replication. These findings indicate that nsP1 dodecamerization is required for its stabilization on plasma membranes. More broadly, our findings suggest that targeting the oligomerization of membrane-associated replication proteins represents a viable antiviral strategy for Togaviruses and potentially other positive-sense RNA viruses.

摘要翻译

正链RNA（+ssRNA）病毒重塑宿主膜以形成复制细胞器（ROs），这一过程主要由病毒编码的膜结合蛋白的寡聚化驱动。在披膜病毒中，非结构蛋白1（nsP1）组装成十二聚体环，作为桥接病毒复制球体与宿主质膜的支架。然而，nsP1寡聚化在病毒复制中的精确作用和必要性仍未完全阐明。在本研究中，我们采用基于结构的计算建模设计了预测会破坏十二聚体内单体-单体相互作用的nsP1突变体。分子动力学（molecular dynamics）模拟进一步表明，这些突变可能损害nsP1的膜结合亲和力。在变性还原条件下进行了Western blotting，并使用共聚焦显微镜分别评估了突变nsP1的表达水平和亚细胞定位。突变蛋白似乎无法稳定保留在质膜上，而是错误定位到内体区室。三组分反式复制酶实验和病毒攻击实验揭示，nsP1十二聚化表面突变体在支持病毒复制方面存在缺陷。本研究强调了nsP1十二聚化在披膜病毒复制中的关键作用，并提出了一种针对+RNA病毒寡聚蛋白结构的潜在抗病毒策略。重要性：披膜病毒是新兴虫媒病毒，能够引起严重疾病和零星暴发。在病毒复制过程中，病毒nsP1组装成十二聚体环，将称为球体的复制细胞器锚定到宿主膜上；然而，寡聚组织在功能上的必要性仍不明确。在本研究中，我们结合基于结构的建模、分子动力学模拟和细胞实验，探讨了预测会破坏nsP1寡聚体的突变如何影响病毒复制。我们发现这些突变降低了膜结合亲和力，改变了亚细胞定位，且携带这些突变的nsP1无法支持病毒RNA复制。这些发现表明，nsP1十二聚化是其稳定存在于质膜上所必需的。更广泛地说，我们的发现提示，靶向膜相关复制蛋白的寡聚化代表了针对披膜病毒及潜在其他正链RNA病毒的一种可行抗病毒策略。

03 研究问题

  • 具体问题：nsP1 的十二聚化（dodecamerization）在 Togavirus 复制中是否功能必需？破坏单体-单体界面的突变是否足以损害膜结合和 RNA 合成？
  • 为什么重要：+ssRNA 病毒通过寡聚化膜结合蛋白重塑宿主膜形成复制细胞器（ROs），nsP1 是 Togavirus 的关键支架蛋白。理解其寡聚化的功能必要性，有助于揭示复制机制并开发靶向寡聚界面的抗病毒策略。
  • 现有方法不足：此前已知 nsP1 形成十二聚体环，但缺乏系统性突变研究来区分「寡聚化本身」与「单体膜结合」的功能贡献；且多数研究在哺乳动物细胞中进行，对 mosquito 细胞（病毒自然宿主）中的功能需求了解有限。
  • 精确研究问题：Can structure-guided mutations that disrupt the nsP1 dodecamerization interface impair membrane binding and viral RNA synthesis in mosquito cells?

06 核心思想

  1. 表面方法：结构建模设计 nsP1 十二聚体界面突变 → MD 模拟预测膜结合变化 → 细胞实验（Western blot、confocal microscopy、trans-replicase assay、viral challenge）验证功能。
  2. 核心洞察：nsP1 的十二聚化不仅是膜结合的「增强器」，而是其在质膜上稳定驻留的必要条件；破坏寡聚化导致蛋白错误定位到内体，从而丧失复制支持功能。这暗示寡聚化可能通过「多价膜锚定」机制实现稳定膜结合。
  3. 可能的普适教训 [Analysis]：对于膜结合寡聚蛋白，功能丧失可能主要源于「定位错误」而非「催化活性丧失」。在蛋白质设计/工程中，评估寡聚界面突变时，应同时检查亚细胞定位变化，而非仅测活性。

  • 算力成本：未提供。

  • 模型依赖：MD 模拟的力场和膜模型未指定，影响可重复性。
  • 最难情形：未测试突变体在哺乳动物细胞中的表型，也未测试其他界面残基组合。

MD 模拟用于膜结合预测：MD 可定性预测突变对膜结合亲和力的影响，但需注意膜模型选择（如 POPC vs 含胆固醇的膜）对结果的影响。本课题中可迁移到膜蛋白或膜锚定蛋白的设计。 3. 定位-功能关联：蛋白亚细胞定位变化（质膜→内体）可作为寡聚化破坏的敏感表型读出。在蛋白质设计验证中，confocal 定位分析是比活性测定更早的表型指标。 4. 多价膜锚定概念：寡聚化通过多价相互作用增强膜结合稳定性，这一原理可迁移到设计膜结合蛋白或纳米结构（如设计多聚体支架以增强膜驻留）。 5. 计算-实验闭环：即使 MD 预测是定性的，也能指导实验设计。本课题中可借鉴「预测→验证→修正」的迭代模式，但需在论文中明确预测的置信度。 6. 抗病毒策略：靶向寡聚化界面作为抗病毒策略，可迁移到其他 +ssRNA 病毒（如 Zika、CHIKV）的 nsP1 同源蛋白，或作为 AI 辅助药物设计的目标。

  2. 名称：膜组成对 nsP1 寡聚化-膜结合耦合的 MD 系统研究 - 来源局限/观察：本文 MD 模拟未说明膜模型，mosquito 细胞膜含高比例胆固醇和鞘脂。 - 核心假设：膜组成（胆固醇含量、负电荷脂质比例）显著影响 nsP1 寡聚体的膜结合稳定性。 - 初步方法：在不同膜组成下进行粗粒化 MD（如 Martini），计算寡聚体-膜结合自由能，与实验突变体表型对比。 - 验证方式：改变 mosquito 细胞膜胆固醇水平（如甲基-β-环糊精处理），检测突变体表型变化。 - 创新状态：unverified。

  4. 名称：跨病毒保守性分析指导的广谱抗 Togavirus 寡聚化抑制剂 - 来源局限/观察：本文仅研究 SFV，但 Togavirus 属内 nsP1 界面可能保守。 - 核心假设：SFV nsP1 界面残基在 CHIKV、RRV 等中保守，靶向保守残基可实现广谱抑制。 - 初步方法：多序列比对 + 结构叠加分析界面保守性，设计针对保守口袋的抑制剂。 - 验证方式：跨病毒 trans-replicase assay 测试抑制剂广谱性。 - 创新状态：unverified。

  5. 名称：nsP1 寡聚化状态转换的粗粒化模型 - 来源局限/观察：本文未研究寡聚化的动态组装过程，仅关注稳态。 - 核心假设：nsP1 从单体到十二聚体的组装是协同过程，膜结合提供驱动力。 - 初步方法：构建 nsP1 单体的粗粒化模型，模拟不同膜条件下的组装路径和自由能景观。 - 验证方式：与实验突变体的组装效率对比，预测新的组装缺陷突变。 - 创新状态：unverified。

审稿人评审

Review setup

  • Input scope Abstract only
  • Assessment boundary Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract; no full-text methods, figures, or data are available for verification
  • Shared manuscript claim summary The authors report that structure-based computational modeling was used to design nsP1 mutants predicted to disrupt dodecameric assembly in Semliki Forest virus. Molecular dynamics simulations suggested reduced membrane-binding affinity. Experimental assays (Western blotting, confocal microscopy, trans-replicase assays, viral challenge) indicated that mutant nsP1 is mislocalized to endosomal compartments and fails to support viral RNA replication. The authors conclude that nsP1 dodecamerization is required for plasma membrane stabilization and propose targeting oligomerization as an antiviral strategy.
  • Visible evidence base Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods details, or numerical data are provided
  • Missing materials affecting confidence Full methods, all figures and tables, statistical analyses, simulation parameters, mutant design details, replication assay protocols, viral challenge data, and any control experiments

Reviewer

  • Overall assessment The abstract presents a coherent and potentially interesting story linking nsP1 dodecamerization to membrane localization and viral replication. However, the evidence as presented is entirely descriptive and lacks quantitative detail. The central claims regarding the necessity of dodecamerization for replication are plausible but cannot be rigorously evaluated from the abstract alone. The computational predictions and experimental results are described in a way that suggests correlation rather than causation, and the proposed antiviral strategy is speculative without demonstration of therapeutic feasibility.
  • Who would be interested in the results, and why Researchers studying togavirus replication, alphavirus molecular biology, viral replication organelle biogenesis, and antiviral drug development targeting viral protein oligomerization. The work may also interest structural biologists studying membrane-associated protein complexes and those working on other positive-sense RNA viruses with analogous replication strategies.
  • Major strengths The study addresses a specific and mechanistically important question about the functional role of nsP1 oligomerization. The combination of computational design, molecular dynamics, and cell-based assays represents a multi-pronged approach. The use of trans-replicase assays and viral challenge experiments provides complementary functional readouts. The potential translational relevance to antiviral development is clearly articulated.
  • Major Concerns
  • Concern ID R1-M1
  • Severity Major
  • Blocking Yes
  • Axis Evidence sufficiency
  • Claim pointer The authors claim that nsP1 dodecamerization is required for its stabilization on plasma membranes and that dodecamerization-surface mutants are deficient in supporting viral replication.
  • Evidence pointer Abstract sections describing Western blotting, confocal microscopy, trans-replicase assays, and viral challenge experiments; location not provided
  • Concern The abstract states that mutant proteins "appear to not be stably retained at the plasma membrane" and are "mislocalized to endosomal compartments," but no quantitative data are presented. It is unclear whether the observed mislocalization is due to disrupted dodecamerization per se or to protein misfolding, aggregation, or reduced expression levels. The Western blotting was performed under denaturing reducing conditions, which would not preserve oligomeric states, so this assay cannot directly confirm or refute dodecamer disruption in cells. The trans-replicase and viral challenge results are described only as showing deficiency, without effect sizes, statistical significance, or comparison to appropriate controls.
  • Why it matters The central conclusion that dodecamerization is functionally required depends on excluding alternative explanations such as general protein instability or misfolding. Without quantitative localization data, oligomerization state assessment in cells, and replication data with statistical support, the causal link between dodecamerization and replication competence is not established.
  • Resolution test Provide quantitative colocalization analysis with plasma membrane and endosomal markers, include a properly folded but oligomerization-deficient control mutant, show that mutant proteins are expressed at comparable levels to wild type, and present replication data with statistical analysis and appropriate positive and negative controls.
