8

A Shortcut to Structure in AlphaFold 3.

Feldman, Jonathan; Skolnick, Jeffrey
ArXiv · 2026-10-25 · 全文(pmc) · 原文

通过因果干预和探针分析AlphaFold 3内部表示，揭示MSA作为结构捷径如何驱动蛋白质全局几何形成，属于蛋白质结构预测模型的可解释性研究，与课题高度相关。

摘要

AlphaFold 3 predicts protein structures with remarkable accuracy, yet how structural information emerges within the model remains poorly understood. Here, through causal interventions on internal representations and direct probing of every Pairformer block, we trace the formation of global protein geometry and identify the multiple sequence alignment (MSA) as a structural shortcut to the fold. Removing the MSA largely preserves local secondary structure while disrupting the long-range relationships that define global topology. Restoring the MSA-enriched pair representation at only forty residues recovers most of this lost organization, including at pairs never directly modified. This contribution depends on the detailed direction of the MSA module's output rather than its magnitude. The Pairformer rapidly converts this signal into global geometry: the final fold becomes recoverable by approximately block 9 of 48 for a majority of proteins, roughly twenty-seven blocks before the model's decoder can render it, whereas without the MSA it remains inaccessible for most proteins throughout the pass. Which homologs are supplied shapes this trajectory more strongly than which query is supplied; it persists for a designed query that never evolved but collapses for a shuffled sequence. Most importantly, an alignment built for a different protein that shares the fold, supplied only at the structurally corresponding columns, raises the median TM-score against experiment from 0.44 to 0.72, while the same alignment shifted a few residues along the chain performs worse than supplying no alignment at all. What AlphaFold 3 reads from an alignment is therefore a description of the fold itself, transferable between proteins that share one, rather than the query's own evolutionary history. This explains both its accuracy and the limits of what it has solved.

摘要翻译

AlphaFold 3 以惊人的准确度预测蛋白质结构，但结构信息如何在模型内部涌现仍鲜为人知。在此，我们通过对内部表征进行因果干预，并直接探测每一个 Pairformer 模块，追踪了全局蛋白质几何结构的形成过程，并将多序列比对 (multiple sequence alignment, MSA) 识别为通往折叠的结构捷径。移除 MSA 在很大程度上保留了局部二级结构，同时破坏了定义全局拓扑的长程关系。仅在四十个残基处恢复富含 MSA 的配对表征，即可恢复大部分丢失的组织结构，包括从未被直接修改的配对。这一贡献取决于 MSA 模块输出的详细方向而非其幅度。Pairformer 迅速将该信号转化为全局几何结构：对于大多数蛋白质，最终折叠在 48 个模块中的约第 9 个模块即可被恢复，大约比模型的解码器能够呈现它早二十七个模块，而在没有 MSA 的情况下，它在整个过程中对大多数蛋白质始终不可及。所提供的同源序列比所提供的查询序列更强地塑造了这一轨迹；它对于一个从未进化过的设计查询持续存在，但对于一个打乱的序列则崩溃。最重要的是，为另一个共享该折叠的蛋白质构建的比对，仅在结构对应的列上提供，即可将相对于实验的中位 TM-score 从 0.44 提升至 0.72，而同一比对沿链移动几个残基后，其表现比完全不提供比对更差。因此，AlphaFold 3 从比对中读取的是折叠本身的描述，可在共享同一折叠的蛋白质之间转移，而非查询自身的进化历史。这既解释了其准确性，也解释了它所解决问题的局限。

02 一句话总结

通过因果干预和线性探针，证明 AlphaFold 3 从 MSA 中读取的是结构描述而非进化历史，该结构信息在 Pairformer 前 10 个 block 内即可转化为可解码的全局折叠，且仅需 40 个残基的稀疏恢复即可重建大部分丢失的全局结构。

03 研究问题

  • 具体问题：AlphaFold 3 如何从 MSA 中提取结构信息？该信息在模型内部如何表征和转化？MSA 的作用是提供进化约束还是直接编码结构？
  • 重要性：理解 AlphaFold 3 的工作原理对于改进结构预测、处理孤儿蛋白（无 MSA）、设计蛋白以及评估模型可靠性至关重要。
  • 现有方法不足：此前研究主要关注最终预测精度，缺乏对模型内部表征的因果分析；AlphaFold 3 的机制可解释性研究尚属空白。
  • 精确研究问题：Can the MSA be understood as a structural description that AlphaFold 3 reads directly, rather than as evolutionary evidence from which the model infers structure?

06 核心思想

  1. 表面方法：对 AlphaFold 3 进行机制可解释性分析，包括：(a) 因果干预——在 checkpoint B 稀疏恢复 MSA 富集表征；(b) 线性探针——在每个 Pairformer block 训练 ridge regression 探针解码 Cα-Cα 距离；(c) 中间扩散读出——从中间 block 的表征直接生成结构；(d) 置换实验——替换 query 序列、使用不同 MSA 子集。
  2. 核心洞察：MSA 的作用不是提供进化历史信息，而是直接编码结构描述。该结构信息在 Pairformer 前 10 个 block 内即被转化为可解码的全局折叠，且以分布式、部分冗余的方式存储，仅需少量残基的恢复即可重建大部分结构。
  3. [Analysis] 可能的普适教训：对于蛋白质结构预测模型，输入中可能包含比预期更直接的结构信息；机制可解释性方法（因果干预 + 线性探针）可以揭示模型实际使用的信息类型，而非仅关注最终精度；结构信息的早期涌现可能意味着模型的前几层承担了关键的信息提取功能。

07 方法总览

  • 输入：氨基酸序列 + MSA（完整或仅 query）；或 query 序列 + 其他蛋白的 MSA
  • 输出：预测结构（通过扩散模块）、中间表征（pair representation）、解码距离矩阵
  • 模块： 1. 数据准备：novel/similar 队列（各 200 蛋白）、Sabmark 98 蛋白 2. AlphaFold 3 前向传播（修改版，含表征提取 hooks） 3. 因果干预：checkpoint B 的稀疏恢复（anchor 选择：contact-based、conservation-based、random） 4. 线性探针：ridge regression 解码 Cα-Cα 距离 5. 中间扩散：从中间 block 表征生成结构 6. 置换实验：query 替换（同源、设计序列、随机洗牌）
  • 流程：生成预测 → 提取中间表征 → 因果干预/探针分析 → 结构重建与评估 → 统计比较

11 结论正确解读

  • 任务范围：仅限单体蛋白结构预测，未涉及复合物、核酸或小分子。
  • oracle/真值输入：使用实验结构（PDB）作为参考；探针训练使用最终 full-MSA 预测作为目标，而非实验结构。
  • 端到端状态：所有分析基于单次前向传播（无 recycling），实际使用中 recycling 可能改变中间表征动态。
  • 算力成本：未提供具体计算资源信息。
  • 历史数据依赖：novel 队列确保无 >40% 序列同一性的训练前同源物，但 Sabmark 蛋白可能已在训练中见过。
  • 模型依赖：所有结论仅针对 AlphaFold 3，不必然推广到其他结构预测模型。
  • 最难情形：无 MSA 的孤儿蛋白、低复杂度区域、构象异构体。
  • 不确定性：探针解码的是「可访问」信息而非「实际使用」信息；因果干预的稀疏恢复可能引入非生理性的表征状态。
  • 有边界的复述：在 AlphaFold 3 的单体蛋白预测中，MSA 提供的结构信息在 Pairformer 前 10 个 block 内即可被线性解码为全局折叠，且该信息以分布式、部分冗余的方式存储，40 个残基的稀疏恢复即可重建大部分丢失的全局结构；MSA 的作用是提供结构描述而非进化历史。

14 学到什么

Agent 提炼的知识候选：

  1. 可迁移概念：MSA 作为「结构描述」而非「进化线索」的视角——对于蛋白质结构预测任务，输入中的同源序列信息可能直接编码结构，而非仅提供进化约束。可迁移到：设计更有效的 MSA 表示方法、改进孤儿蛋白预测策略。

  2. 可迁移方法：因果干预 + 线性探针的组合分析框架——通过稀疏恢复（anchor-based patching）和逐层探针解码，可以定位模型内部结构信息的存储位置和可访问性。可迁移到：其他结构预测模型（如 ESMFold、RoseTTAFold）的机制分析。

  3. 可迁移实验设计：置换实验范式——通过替换 query 序列（同源、设计、随机）而保持 MSA 不变，分离序列与 MSA 的贡献。可迁移到：评估其他输入特征（如模板、共进化信号）的实际作用。

  4. 可迁移洞察：结构信息的早期涌现（前 10/48 block）——模型可能在早期层就完成了全局结构信息的提取，后续层主要负责细化。可迁移到：设计更高效的模型架构（减少层数）、开发早期退出策略。

  5. 可迁移指标：contact AUROC 作为中间表征结构信息量的度量——比最终 TM-score 更敏感地反映表征中的结构信息。可迁移到：模型压缩、知识蒸馏中的中间层评估。

  6. 可迁移应用：稀疏 anchor 恢复策略——在计算资源受限时，仅恢复关键残基的 MSA 信息即可获得大部分结构精度。可迁移到：大规模蛋白质结构预测的加速策略。

Agent 生成的研究候选：

  1. 候选名称：MSA-free 结构预测的稀疏锚点引导策略 - 来源局限/观察：本文证明 40 个残基的 MSA 信息即可恢复大部分结构，但实际应用中孤儿蛋白无 MSA 可用 - 核心假设：通过预测关键锚点残基（如高接触度残基）并生成合成 MSA，可以部分替代真实 MSA 的结构信息 - 初步方法：训练一个轻量级模型预测每个残基的结构重要性评分，对高评分残基生成合成 MSA 列，输入 AlphaFold 3 进行预测 - 验证方式：在 novel 队列上比较合成 MSA 与无 MSA 的 TM-score 提升 - 创新状态：unverified

  2. 候选名称：跨模型结构信息可迁移性分析 - 来源局限/观察：本文的探针和干预方法仅应用于 AlphaFold 3，不清楚其他模型（如 ESMFold、RoseTTAFold）是否也有类似的结构信息早期涌现现象 - 核心假设：结构信息的早期涌现是结构预测模型的普遍特征，而非 AlphaFold 3 特有 - 初步方法：将本文的线性探针框架应用于 ESMFold 和 RoseTTAFold 的中间表征，比较结构信息可解码性的时间进程 - 验证方式：各模型在相同蛋白集上的 contact AUROC 随层数的变化曲线 - 创新状态：unverified

  3. 候选名称：结构信息稀疏性的理论解释 - 来源局限/观察：本文观察到 40 个残基足以恢复结构，但未提供理论解释为何如此少的残基足够 - 核心假设：蛋白质折叠的关键约束集中在少数「枢纽」残基上，这些残基的接触模式决定了全局拓扑 - 初步方法：分析 40 个 contact-based anchors 的图论性质（如介数中心性、度分布），与随机 anchors 对比 - 验证方式：如果 contact-based anchors 在图论指标上显著区别于随机选择，则支持枢纽残基假说 - 创新状态：unverified

  4. 候选名称：结构信息早期涌现的架构设计启示 - 来源局限/观察：本文发现结构信息在前 10/48 block 内即可解码，暗示后续 block 可能主要用于细化而非全局组织 - 核心假设：可以通过减少 Pairformer 层数或引入早期退出机制来加速预测，而不损失全局结构精度 - 初步方法：在 AlphaFold 3 中测试从不同 block 提前退出并直接进入扩散模块的效果，比较 TM-score 与计算时间的权衡 - 验证方式：在 novel 队列上比较不同退出点的 TM-score 分布 - 创新状态：unverified

  5. 候选名称：MSA 结构信息与构象采样的关联 - 来源局限/观察：本文证明 MSA 编码结构描述，但未探讨该结构描述是否包含构象多样性信息 - 核心假设：MSA 中的结构信息可能包含多个构象状态的混合描述，影响模型对构象异构体的预测能力 - 初步方法：对已知构象异构体蛋白，比较不同 MSA 子集（偏向不同构象）对预测结构的影响 - 验证方式：预测结构是否随 MSA 子集偏向不同构象状态 - 创新状态：unverified

审稿人评审

Review setup

  • Input scope Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, and supplementary material references.
  • Assessment boundary Scientific validity, methodological soundness, interpretability of claims, and alignment with Nature-style criteria. No assessment of formatting or editorial style.
  • Shared manuscript claim summary The authors claim that AlphaFold 3 uses the multiple sequence alignment (MSA) as a "shortcut" to protein structure: the MSA module writes a distributed, direction-dependent representation that the Pairformer rapidly converts into a decodable global fold within the first ~10 of 48 blocks, and that this information is transferable between proteins sharing a fold, even at very low sequence identity.
  • Visible evidence base Results sections describing causal interventions (activation patching, magnitude–direction swaps), probing analyses (contact probes, distance-decoding probes), intermediate diffusion readouts, and transplantation experiments. Methods sections describing cohorts, metrics, and statistical tests. Supplementary figure and table references are cited but not provided in full.
  • Missing materials affecting confidence Supplementary figures (S1, S3), tables (S7–S11), and sections (S1–S8) are referenced but not included. Full details of the AlphaFold 3 architecture and training are not provided. The exact implementation of the "register" concept in transplantation is described but not visualized. The statistical power and effect sizes for some comparisons (e.g., Wilcoxon tests) are not fully reported.

Reviewer

  • Overall assessment This is a technically sophisticated and thought-provoking manuscript that makes a strong, well-supported case for a specific mechanistic interpretation of AlphaFold 3's use of MSAs. The causal intervention and probing methodology is rigorous and appropriate. The central claim—that the MSA provides a transferable, direction-dependent description of the fold rather than evolutionary history—is novel and important. However, several technical concerns and missing details (particularly around the transplantation experiments and the interpretation of probe results) need to be addressed before the case is fully established.
  • Who would be interested in the results, and why Structural biologists, computational biologists, and machine learning researchers working on protein structure prediction and interpretability. The findings have implications for understanding the limits of current models, for designing better MSA-based inputs, and for the broader question of what predictive models actually learn. The methodological approach (causal interventions on internal representations) will be of interest to the interpretability community.
  • Major strengths 1. The causal intervention approach (activation patching, magnitude–direction swaps) is a powerful and appropriate method for establishing causality, going beyond correlational analysis. 2. The finding that the MSA's contribution is direction-dependent, not magnitude-dependent, is a subtle and important mechanistic insight. 3. The transplantation experiments, if fully validated, provide a striking and direct demonstration that the MSA encodes fold information transferable between proteins. 4. The use of multiple complementary readouts (probing, diffusion, structural metrics) strengthens the conclusions. 5. The writing is clear and the logical progression from observation to intervention to mechanism is well-structured.
  • Major Concerns
  • R1-M1 (Blocking: Yes): The transplantation experiments are the most direct evidence for the central claim, but the description of the "register" concept and the construction of the transplanted alignments is insufficiently detailed. The authors state that the donor's sequences are written onto the target's columns "through the structural alignment," but the exact procedure for handling insertions, deletions, and gaps in the donor sequences is not fully specified. Without this, it is difficult to assess whether the results could be explained by trivial sequence features rather than structural correspondence.
  • R1-M2 (Blocking: No): The distance-decoding probes are trained on the similar cohort and applied to the novel cohort. The authors report that the probes recover the fold by block 9, but the possibility of probe overfitting to the training cohort, or to generic protein properties (e.g., amino acid composition), is not fully addressed. The control analyses (generic chain floor, permutation control) are helpful, but a more direct control—e.g., training probes on shuffled or randomized structures—would strengthen the claim that the probes are reading genuine structural information.
  • R1-M3 (Blocking: No): The claim that "the query determines whether it emerges at all" is based on the shuffled-query experiment, where the shuffled query remains at the floor. However, the authors also show that a designed sequence (MPNN) yields more accurate reconstructions than the native query. This is an interesting but potentially paradoxical result: if the query's role is merely to be "physically sensible," why does a designed sequence outperform the native one? The authors speculate, but do not test, an explanation. This deserves further investigation or at least a more careful discussion.
  • R1-M4 (Blocking: No): The intermediate diffusion readouts show that the proto-fold is compact and featureless at early blocks, and the authors interpret this as a property of the diffusion model rather than the representation. However, the possibility that the diffusion model's behavior on intermediate representations is not a faithful readout of the information content (e.g., due to distribution shift) is acknowledged but not directly tested. A control experiment—e.g., feeding the diffusion model a representation from a late block but with early-block-like properties—would help.
  • Minor Comments
  • R1-m1 (Axis: Clarity): The term "register" is used to describe the correspondence between donor and target columns, but it is not formally defined. A precise definition, perhaps with a small schematic, would improve accessibility.
  • R1-m2 (Axis: Statistical reporting): The Wilcoxon signed-rank test p-value for the key trajectory comparison (p=6×10−11) is reported, but the effect size (e.g., median difference) is not. Reporting effect sizes would help readers gauge the practical significance.
  • R1-m3 (Axis: Reproducibility): The authors state that "the model seed held fixed" for the disjoint-MSA and query-substitution experiments, but the specific seed value is not provided. For reproducibility, this should be stated.
  • R1-m4 (Axis: Interpretation): The authors state that the MSA contribution is "direction-dependent" based on the magnitude–direction swaps. However, the interpretation of "direction" in a high-dimensional space is not intuitive. A brief explanation of what "direction" means in this context (e.g., in terms of the geometry of the representation space) would be helpful.
  • R1-m5 (Axis: Completeness): The authors mention that "all proteins examined in this study were deposited after the cutoff date" for the novel cohort, but the similar cohort by definition contains proteins with pre-cutoff homologs. The potential for data leakage in the similar cohort (used for probe training) is not discussed.
  • Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established
  • R1-M1 (transplantation methodology) is the primary blocking concern. The case for the central claim rests heavily on this experiment, and the methodology must be fully transparent and controlled.
  • Assessment against Nature-style criteria
  • Originality: High. The finding that the MSA acts as a transferable, direction-dependent "fold description" is a novel mechanistic insight that challenges the conventional view of MSAs as evolutionary evidence.
  • Scientific importance: High. The work has implications for understanding the limits and failure modes of AlphaFold 3, for improving MSA-based inputs, and for the broader interpretability of deep learning models in biology.
  • Interdisciplinary readership: Moderate to high. The topic is of clear interest to structural biology and ML communities. The methodological approach (causal interventions) may also appeal to a broader neuroscience/interpretability audience, but the presentation is somewhat specialized.
  • Technical soundness: Generally strong, with rigorous causal intervention and probing methodology. However, the transplantation experiment (R1-M1) and the probe interpretation (R1-M2) require further validation.
  • Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract and introduction are accessible, but the results and methods sections are dense and assume significant familiarity with AlphaFold's architecture and interpretability techniques. A brief primer on the relevant components (MSA module, Pairformer, diffusion) would improve accessibility.
  • Recommendation posture Supportive if the technical concerns are resolved, particularly the transplantation methodology (R1-M1). The core finding is novel and important, but the current evidence, while suggestive, does not fully establish the case without a more rigorous and transparent description of the transplantation experiments and additional controls for the probing analyses.

Risk / unsupported claims

  • The claim that the MSA is a "description of the fold itself" is supported by the transplantation experiments, but these are not fully described and may be confounded by sequence features. This claim is currently not fully established.
  • The claim that the query's role is to determine "whether it emerges at all" is supported by the shuffled-query experiment, but the MPNN result (more accurate than native) complicates this interpretation and is not fully explained.
  • The claim that the fold is "recoverable by approximately block 9 of 48" is based on probe reconstructions, which are indirect readouts. The authors acknowledge this, but the claim is stated strongly in the abstract and discussion.
  • The claim that "without the MSA, it remains inaccessible for most proteins throughout the pass" is based on the novel cohort; the similar cohort behaves differently, and the reasons for this difference are not fully explored.
