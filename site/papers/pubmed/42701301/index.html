Enhanced Sampling and Ligandability Assessment to Expand the Repertoire of Potentially Druggable Cryptic Pockets.

Vithani, Neha; Zhang, She; Gunther, Judith; Purkey, Hans; Lawson, J David; Nicholls, Anthony; Skillman, A Geoffrey; LeBard, David N
Journal of the American Chemical Society · 2026-09-06 · 仅摘要 · 原文

该文使用加权系综分子动力学模拟结合正常模式分析，从apo结构出发预测蛋白质隐秘口袋，并评估其配体可药性，直接服务于蛋白质结构计算中的构象采样和药物设计问题。

摘要

Certain proteins known to be involved in life-threatening diseases have remained challenging therapeutic targets for decades, simply because a suitable binding pocket for a potent molecular inhibitor could not be identified in their ground-state apo structures. In the past decade, the discovery of cryptic pockets in challenging targets like KRAS and Werner helicase has proven to be a major turning point for therapeutic development. However, the alternate protein conformations required for these cryptic pockets to exist were only revealed by experiments conducted in the presence of ligands that can bind to them. Time-consuming and expensive experiments currently used to uncover these biologically rare events could be usefully complemented by a computational method capable of reliably identifying cryptic pockets. We have previously shown that aqueous and mixed-solvent Weighted Ensemble molecular dynamics (WEMD) simulations, driven by normal modes representing the direction of the most collective motion of a protein, can predict known cryptic pockets in the KRAS oncoprotein. Here, we evaluate this cryptic pocket detection technique on a data set of diverse proteins and show that it successfully samples cryptic pockets within 2 A of the known holo conformation 57% of the time, starting with just the apo structure. The predicted pockets in the most holo-like conformations show at least 20%, 50%, and 80% volume overlap with the bound ligand in the holo structure, with success rates of 92%, 84%, and 46%, respectively. We also show that we can successfully rank candidate pockets from WEMD using our pocket ligandability prediction model, Target X.

摘要翻译

某些已知与危及生命的疾病相关的蛋白质，数十年来一直是难以攻克的治疗靶点，原因很简单：在其基态无配体（apo）结构中，无法为强效分子抑制剂识别出合适的结合口袋。在过去十年中，在KRAS和Werner解旋酶等棘手靶点中发现隐蔽口袋（cryptic pockets），已被证明是治疗开发的一个重大转折点。然而，这些隐蔽口袋存在所需的替代蛋白质构象，仅通过在与能结合它们的配体存在下进行的实验才得以揭示。目前用于揭示这些生物学罕见事件的方法耗时且昂贵，而一种能够可靠识别隐蔽口袋的计算方法，可对其进行有益的补充。我们此前已表明，由代表蛋白质最集体运动方向的简正模式（normal modes）驱动的水溶液和混合溶剂加权系综分子动力学（WEMD）模拟，能够预测KRAS癌蛋白中已知的隐蔽口袋。在此，我们在一个多样化的蛋白质数据集上评估了这种隐蔽口袋检测技术，并表明仅从apo结构出发，它能在57%的情况下成功采样到与已知全配体（holo）构象相差2 Å以内的隐蔽口袋。在最接近holo构象的预测口袋中，与holo结构中结合配体的体积重叠至少达到20%、50%和80%，成功率分别为92%、84%和46%。我们还表明，利用我们的口袋配体可及性预测模型Target X，能够成功对WEMD产生的候选口袋进行排序。

03 研究问题

  • 具体问题：如何从蛋白质的apo（未结合）结构出发，可靠地预测和评估隐式口袋（cryptic pockets）的存在及其可药性？
  • 为什么重要：许多疾病相关蛋白在apo结构中缺乏明显的结合口袋，导致传统药物发现困难。隐式口袋的发现（如KRAS、Werner helicase）是治疗开发的关键转折点，但实验发现耗时且昂贵。
  • 现有方法为何不足：实验方法（如共晶结构）需要配体存在，无法从apo结构直接预测；计算方法的可靠性和泛化能力有限。
  • 精确的「Can ... ?」研究问题：Can a computational pipeline combining normal-mode-driven Weighted Ensemble molecular dynamics (WEMD) and a ligandability prediction model (Target X) reliably identify cryptic pockets from apo protein structures across a diverse set of targets?

04 背景与发展脉络

  • 脉络来源：经外部核验（基于领域常识）。
  • 阶段1：实验发现：通过共晶结构或配体结合实验偶然发现隐式口袋（如KRAS G12C突变体），耗时且昂贵。
  • 阶段2：常规MD模拟：可采样构象，但隐式口袋是罕见事件，常规MD难以在合理时间内采样到。
  • 阶段3：增强采样方法：如加权系综（WE）、伞形采样、元动力学等，可加速罕见事件采样，但需要先验知识或计算成本高。
  • 阶段4：简正模式引导的WEMD：作者先前工作（KRAS）证明，用简正模式（代表最集体运动方向）初始化WEMD可预测已知隐式口袋。
  • 本文主张的位置：将上述方法扩展到多样化的蛋白质数据集，并整合配体能力预测模型（Target X）进行口袋排名，验证其泛化能力和实用性。

06 核心思想

  1. 表面方法：使用简正模式（normal modes）初始化加权系综分子动力学（WEMD）模拟，从apo结构出发对蛋白质进行增强采样，然后使用配体能力预测模型Target X对采样到的口袋进行排名。
  2. 核心洞察：蛋白质的集体运动（由简正模式描述）是隐式口袋暴露的关键驱动力；WEMD可以高效地沿这些方向进行采样，而Target X可以区分可药性与不可药性的口袋构象。
  3. 可能的普适教训：[Analysis] 对于罕见构象事件（如隐式口袋、变构位点），将物理先验（如简正模式）与增强采样方法结合，可以显著提高采样效率，而无需大量训练数据。这种“物理先验 + 增强采样”的范式可迁移到其他构象采样问题。

07 方法总览

  • 输入：蛋白质的apo结构（PDB文件）。
  • 输出：预测的隐式口袋构象及其配体能力评分。
  • 模块： 1. 简正模式分析：计算apo结构的简正模式，选择代表最集体运动的低频模式。 2. WEMD模拟：沿选定的简正模式方向初始化多个WEMD副本，进行增强采样。 3. 口袋检测：从WEMD轨迹中提取构象，使用Fpocket或类似工具检测口袋。 4. 配体能力预测：使用Target X模型对检测到的口袋进行评分和排名。
  • 训练：WEMD无需训练；Target X是预训练模型（本文未详述其训练过程）。
  • 工具：WEMD（加权系综分子动力学）、简正模式分析（如NMA）、Fpocket（口袋检测）、Target X（配体能力预测）。
  • 反馈回路：无显式反馈回路；WEMD采样后直接进行口袋检测和评分。
  • 假设：简正模式方向足以引导WEMD采样到隐式口袋构象；Target X评分可区分可药性与不可药性口袋。
  • 文字流程：从apo结构出发 → 计算简正模式 → 沿低频模式初始化WEMD模拟 → 运行WEMD采样 → 从轨迹中提取构象 → 使用Fpocket检测口袋 → 使用Target X对口袋评分 → 输出排名靠前的口袋构象。

11 结论正确解读

  • 任务范围：仅针对已知存在隐式口袋的蛋白质（如KRAS），未测试未知靶点。
  • oracle/真值输入：使用holo结构作为验证标准，但方法本身仅依赖apo结构。
  • 端到端状态：方法为端到端（从apo结构到口袋排名），但Target X是预训练模型，其泛化性依赖于训练数据。
  • 算力成本：未提供，但WEMD通常比常规MD计算成本高。
  • 历史数据依赖：Target X依赖历史配体-蛋白复合物数据训练。
  • 模型依赖：结果依赖于简正模式选择和WEMD参数设置。
  • 最难情形：高体积重叠（80%）成功率仅46%，说明精确构象采样仍是挑战。
  • 群体/领域边界：仅测试了有限数量的蛋白质，泛化性需进一步验证。
  • 不确定性：57%的成功率意味着43%的案例失败，方法并非普适。
  • 有边界的复述：在测试的蛋白质集合中，使用简正模式引导的WEMD模拟从apo结构出发，有57%的概率采样到与holo构象RMSD < 2 Å的隐式口袋，且大多数采样口袋与配体有显著体积重叠；Target X可辅助排名，但精确构象采样和高重叠成功率仍需改进。

Reviewer

  • Overall assessment The manuscript addresses a timely and important problem in drug discovery—the computational identification of cryptic pockets from apo protein structures. The proposed approach, combining normal-mode-driven WEMD with a ligandability model, is conceptually interesting and builds on prior work by the authors. However, the provided abstract alone is insufficient to evaluate the robustness, reproducibility, or generalizability of the claims. Critical details on the dataset, simulation protocols, validation metrics, and statistical significance are missing, preventing a rigorous assessment of the method's performance and its potential impact.
  • Who would be interested in the results, and why Computational chemists, structural biologists, and drug discovery researchers would be interested, as the method offers a potentially cost-effective and scalable alternative to experimental cryptic pocket discovery, which could accelerate target identification for challenging proteins like KRAS and Werner helicase.
  • Major strengths The problem is of high scientific and therapeutic importance. The use of normal modes to guide WEMD simulations is a clever strategy to enhance sampling of rare conformational events. The integration of a ligandability prediction model (Target X) to rank pockets adds practical value for prioritization in drug design.
  • Major Concerns
    • Concern ID R1-M1
    • Severity Major
    • Blocking Yes
    • Axis Technical soundness
    • Claim pointer The method successfully samples cryptic pockets within 2 Å of the known holo conformation 57% of the time, starting with just the apo structure.
    • Evidence pointer Abstract; location not provided
    • Concern The abstract reports a 57% success rate for sampling pockets within 2 Å RMSD of the holo conformation, but no details are given on the dataset size, diversity, or how "success" is defined (e.g., per-residue RMSD, pocket-wide RMSD, or ligand-binding site alignment). The threshold of 2 Å is also not justified. Without these details, the claim is unverifiable.
    • Why it matters The core claim of the method's predictive accuracy depends on this metric. If the dataset is small or biased, or if the RMSD threshold is too lenient, the reported success rate may not reflect true performance on unseen targets.
    • Resolution test Provide the full dataset (number and names of proteins, their structural classes, and known cryptic pockets), define the RMSD calculation method, and justify the 2 Å threshold. Include a confusion matrix or per-target success/failure list.
    • Concern ID R1-M2
    • Severity Major
    • Blocking Yes
    • Axis Reproducibility
    • Claim pointer The predicted pockets show at least 20%, 50%, and 80% volume overlap with the bound ligand in the holo structure, with success rates of 92%, 84%, and 46%, respectively.
    • Evidence pointer Abstract; location not provided
    • Concern The volume overlap metric is not defined. Is it the Jaccard index, Dice coefficient, or another measure? How is the pocket volume computed (e.g., using a grid-based method, solvent-accessible surface, or a tool like fpocket)? The success rates at different overlap thresholds are presented without confidence intervals or statistical tests.
    • Why it matters Volume overlap is a key validation metric. Without a clear definition and statistical context, the reported success rates cannot be interpreted or compared to other methods.
    • Resolution test Define the volume overlap metric explicitly, describe the pocket detection algorithm, and provide per-target overlap values with error bars or confidence intervals. Include a comparison to a baseline method (e.g., random sampling or standard MD).
    • Concern ID R1-M3
    • Severity Major
    • Blocking Yes
    • Axis Generalizability
    • Claim pointer The method is evaluated on a "data set of diverse proteins."
    • Evidence pointer Abstract; location not provided
    • Concern The abstract does not specify the number of proteins, their sequence or structural diversity, or whether they represent different fold classes, sizes, or functional families. The claim of diversity is unsubstantiated.
    • Why it matters A method that works on a few well-studied targets (e.g., KRAS) may not generalize to other proteins. The diversity of the test set is critical for assessing the method's broad applicability.
    • Resolution test List all proteins in the dataset, their PDB IDs, and key properties (size, fold, number of known cryptic pockets). Provide a phylogenetic or structural diversity analysis.
    • Concern ID R1-M4
    • Severity Major
    • Blocking Yes
    • Axis Validation
    • Claim pointer The ligandability prediction model Target X can successfully rank candidate pockets from WEMD.
    • Evidence pointer Abstract; location not provided
    • Concern No details are given on Target X: its architecture, training data, features, or performance metrics (e.g., AUC, precision-recall). The claim of "successfully rank" is vague—what is the success criterion (e.g., top-1, top-3, or top-10 ranking)? How does it compare to existing ligandability predictors?
    • Why it matters The practical utility of the method hinges on the ability to prioritize pockets. Without validation of Target X, the ranking step is unsubstantiated.
    • Resolution test Describe Target X in detail, provide its performance on a held-out test set, and show ranking results for the cryptic pockets identified by WEMD (e.g., a table of predicted vs. actual ligandability scores).
