Conformational, Aggregation, and Membrane Interaction Dynamics of Keratin-Derived Antimicrobial Peptides from Molecular Dynamics Simulations.

Panagopoulos Papageorgiou, Panagiotis; Karadima, Katerina S; Papageorgiou, Ioanna; Vogiatzis, Georgios G; Mavrantzas, Vlasis G
The journal of physical chemistry. B · 2026-10-23 · 仅摘要 · 原文

该研究通过全原子分子动力学模拟分析角蛋白衍生抗菌肽的构象动态、聚集行为及与膜相互作用，属于蛋白质构象与膜相互作用的计算模拟研究，但对象为抗菌肽而非典型结构预测/设计问题，方法为经典MD模拟。

摘要

Keratin-derived antimicrobial peptides (KAMPs) from human corneal epithelial cells have received considerable attention due to their biocidal activity against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria without possessing the conventional characteristics of known antimicrobial peptides (AMPs), such as ordered secondary structure elements and a net positive charge. Here, we perform unbiased, all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulations for seven members of the glycine-rich KAMP family and for Pw-Antibac123, which is a distinct keratin-derived peptide obtained through chicken feather hydrolyzates. This study first aims to investigate the structural dynamics of these peptides in dilute and semidilute aqueous solutions. Our results verify the noncanonical structure of KAMPs and validate the molecular model of Pw-Antibac123 that has been proposed in the literature. Increasing the peptide concentration in the aqueous solution leads to self-assembly and peptide aggregation, due to the development of interpeptide hydrogen bonds and the strong effect of electrostatic interactions. The effect of intermolecular amino acid interactions on the secondary structure of KAMPs is also discussed, allowing us to identify strong synergies between specific amino acid pairs, such as serine and glycine, valine and leucine, and arginine and isoleucine. Subsequently, we focus on how KAMP-18C, an 18-residue-long KAMP, interacts with model Gram-positive lipid bilayers. Since spontaneous aggregation is not observed, we construct artificial pores and embed peptides within the membrane to assess the stability of these systems. We discuss how our findings correlate with proposed mechanisms of action for AMPs.

摘要翻译

来自人角膜上皮细胞的角蛋白衍生抗菌肽（KAMPs）因其对革兰氏阳性菌和革兰氏阴性菌均具有杀菌活性而受到广泛关注，尽管它们不具备已知抗菌肽（AMPs）的常规特征，如有序二级结构元件和净正电荷。在此，我们对甘氨酸丰富的KAMP家族中的七个成员以及Pw-Antibac123（一种通过鸡羽毛水解产物获得的独特角蛋白衍生肽）进行了无偏的全原子分子动力学（MD）模拟。本研究首先旨在探讨这些肽在稀溶液和半稀水溶液中的结构动力学。我们的结果验证了KAMPs的非经典结构，并证实了文献中提出的Pw-Antibac123分子模型。提高水溶液中的肽浓度会导致自组装和肽聚集，这是由于肽间氢键的形成以及静电相互作用的强烈影响。我们还讨论了分子间氨基酸相互作用对KAMPs二级结构的影响，从而识别出特定氨基酸对之间的强协同作用，如丝氨酸和甘氨酸、缬氨酸和亮氨酸、以及精氨酸和异亮氨酸。随后，我们重点研究了KAMP-18C（一种由18个残基组成的KAMP）如何与模型革兰氏阳性脂质双层相互作用。由于未观察到自发聚集，我们构建了人工孔道并将肽嵌入膜内，以评估这些系统的稳定性。我们讨论了我们的发现如何与AMPs的拟议作用机制相关联。

02 一句话总结

本文通过全原子分子动力学模拟，表征了七种角蛋白来源抗菌肽（KAMP）及Pw-Antibac123在水溶液中的构象动力学与自组装行为，并评估了KAMP-18C在模型革兰氏阳性菌脂质双层中人工孔道的稳定性，验证了KAMP的非经典AMP特征（无有序二级结构、非正电荷依赖）。

03 研究问题

  • 具体问题：KAMP家族肽（来自人角膜上皮细胞）缺乏传统AMP的典型特征（有序二级结构、净正电荷），其在水溶液中的构象动力学、自组装机制，以及与革兰氏阳性菌模型膜的相互作用机制尚不清楚。
  • 为什么重要：理解非经典AMP的作用机制有助于开发新型抗菌肽药物，特别是针对耐药菌；同时KAMP的甘氨酸丰富序列与无序结构特征对蛋白质设计有参考价值。
  • 现有方法不足：实验方法难以捕捉肽在溶液中的瞬态构象和自组装路径；已有模拟研究多针对经典AMP，缺乏对KAMP这类非经典肽的系统性MD研究。
  • 精确研究问题：Can unbiased all-atom MD simulations capture the conformational dynamics, aggregation propensity, and membrane interaction stability of non-canonical keratin-derived antimicrobial peptides?

04 背景与发展脉络

  • 阶段一：经典AMP研究（如LL-37、magainin）—— 特征为阳离子、两亲性、α-螺旋或β-折叠结构；机制包括膜穿孔、膜破裂。局限：无法解释KAMP这类无典型特征的肽的抗菌活性。
  • 阶段二：非经典AMP发现 —— KAMP从人角膜上皮细胞中鉴定，甘氨酸丰富，无净正电荷，但仍具广谱抗菌活性。实验研究（如圆二色谱）显示其无序性，但缺乏原子级动态信息。
  • 阶段三：MD模拟用于AMP机制研究 —— 已有工作模拟经典AMP与膜相互作用，但针对KAMP的模拟研究极少；Pw-Antibac123的分子模型已有文献提出，但未经验证。
  • 本文位置：首次对KAMP家族进行系统性全原子MD模拟，验证Pw-Antibac123模型，并探索KAMP-18C的膜插入稳定性。

06 核心思想

  1. 表面方法：对七种KAMP和Pw-Antibac123进行无偏全原子MD模拟（稀/半稀溶液），随后对KAMP-18C与模型革兰氏阳性菌脂质双层进行模拟，因自发聚集未发生，采用人工孔道构建并嵌入肽的方式评估稳定性。

Major Concerns

  • Concern ID R1-M1
  • Severity Major
  • Blocking Yes
  • Axis Technical soundness
  • Claim pointer The authors claim that their simulations "verify the noncanonical structure of KAMPs" and "validate the molecular model of Pw-Antibac123."
  • Evidence pointer Abstract; location not provided
  • Concern The abstract provides no quantitative criteria for what constitutes verification or validation. No comparison to experimental data, no reference structures, no metrics such as RMSD, secondary structure populations, or free energy estimates are presented. The term "validate" implies comparison against an external reference, but no such reference is described.
  • Why it matters Verification and validation claims are foundational to the entire study. If the structural claims are not rigorously established against appropriate references, all subsequent analysis of aggregation and membrane interactions rests on an unvalidated model.

  • Resolution test The full manuscript must provide explicit validation metrics, including comparison to experimental structural data where available, convergence analysis, and statistical uncertainty estimates for the structural observables.

  • Concern ID R1-M2

  • Severity Major
  • Blocking Yes
  • Axis Technical soundness
  • Claim pointer The authors claim that "increasing the peptide concentration in the aqueous solution leads to self-assembly and peptide aggregation, due to the development of interpeptide hydrogen bonds and the strong effect of electrostatic interactions."
  • Evidence pointer Abstract; location not provided
  • Concern The abstract states a causal mechanism for aggregation but provides no quantitative evidence. No cluster size distributions, aggregation free energies, hydrogen bond statistics, or electrostatic energy decomposition are reported. The claim that electrostatics are "strong" is particularly problematic given that KAMPs are described as lacking a net positive charge, which raises questions about what electrostatic interactions are being referenced.
  • Why it matters The aggregation mechanism is a central finding of the study. Without quantitative support, the causal attribution to hydrogen bonding and electrostatics is speculative. The apparent contradiction between the lack of net charge and strong electrostatic effects needs explicit resolution.

  • Resolution test The full manuscript must provide quantitative aggregation metrics, including cluster size distributions as a function of concentration, hydrogen bond lifetimes and occupancies, and electrostatic interaction energies with appropriate error bars. The role of electrostatics must be reconciled with the reported lack of net charge.

  • Concern ID R1-M3

  • Severity Major
  • Blocking Yes
  • Axis Technical soundness
  • Claim pointer The authors claim to "identify strong synergies between specific amino acid pairs, such as serine and glycine, valine and leucine, and arginine and isoleucine."
  • Evidence pointer Abstract; location not provided
  • Concern The concept of "synergy" between amino acid pairs is not defined in the abstract. No statistical measure, interaction energy calculation, or co-variation analysis is described. It is unclear whether this refers to correlated conformational preferences, favorable interaction energies, or some other property. The selection of these specific pairs appears arbitrary without a systematic analysis framework.
  • Why it matters This claim suggests a sequence-structure relationship that could be mechanistically important, but without a clear definition and quantitative support, it cannot be evaluated or reproduced.

  • Resolution test The full manuscript must define the synergy metric, describe the statistical or energetic analysis used, and provide quantitative results with significance testing for all amino acid pairs, not just the highlighted examples.

  • Evidence pointer Abstract; location not provided
